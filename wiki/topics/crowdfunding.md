Crowdfunding here means a Kickstarter or Indiegogo campaign that pre-sells the first batch of a physical product. All three sources are consumer brands that used it to fund inventory.

## Definition

A crowdfunding campaign takes orders before the product exists, so customers pay for the first production run. [BeRKQkU5Ceo](../sources/BeRKQkU5Ceo.md) [6:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeRKQkU5Ceo&t=408s)

## Repeated patterns

- **Bring the buyers with you.** No source relies on the platform to find buyers. [Hush Blankets](../businesses/hush-blankets.md) had 3,000 people who said on the phone that they planned to buy. [IuoscQiQQLg](../sources/IuoscQiQQLg.md) [6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=366s). [Sheets & Giggles](../businesses/sheets-and-giggles.md) built a waitlist of 11,000 emails. [dphK9rh2F5U](../sources/dphK9rh2F5U.md) [6:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=360s). [NeuroGum](../businesses/neurogum.md) got help from a Reddit community that a founder was part of. [BeRKQkU5Ceo](../sources/BeRKQkU5Ceo.md) [6:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeRKQkU5Ceo&t=408s)
- **Fund inventory, not salaries.** Indiegogo paid for NeuroGum's first inventory. [BeRKQkU5Ceo](../sources/BeRKQkU5Ceo.md) [6:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeRKQkU5Ceo&t=408s). Hush needed $100,000 for its first roll of custom fabric and had $4,000 left. [IuoscQiQQLg](../sources/IuoscQiQQLg.md) [5:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=357s)
- **Press and TV follow.** NeuroGum got Time Magazine coverage and a Dr. Oz appearance after the campaign, then Shark Tank in 2019. [BeRKQkU5Ceo](../sources/BeRKQkU5Ceo.md) [6:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeRKQkU5Ceo&t=408s), [10:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeRKQkU5Ceo&t=648s). Hush went on Dragon's Den after its Kickstarter. [IuoscQiQQLg](../sources/IuoscQiQQLg.md) [7:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=426s)
- **Validate before the campaign.** Hush pre-sold with basic images of one sample and bought the most expensive Google keyword. [IuoscQiQQLg](../sources/IuoscQiQQLg.md) [2:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=129s). Sheets & Giggles locked in a launch price for sign-ups, then surveyed the list on colors and price. [dphK9rh2F5U](../sources/dphK9rh2F5U.md) [5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=300s)

## Concrete examples with numbers

- **The day-one rule.** Plan for 30% of the goal on day one. A $100K goal needs $30K on day one, or 300 buyers at $100. Email lists convert at about 3%, so you need about 10,000 emails. [Colin McIntosh](../people/colin-mcintosh.md) gives this rule. [dphK9rh2F5U](../sources/dphK9rh2F5U.md) [6:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=399s)
- **Sheets & Giggles.** A $100 set offered at $69 to early sign-ups captured 11,000 emails at a 46% capture rate. Launch day brought 450 customers and $45K. The campaign made $284K. [dphK9rh2F5U](../sources/dphK9rh2F5U.md) [6:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=360s), [6:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=399s)
- **Hush Blankets.** The Kickstarter goal was $25K, and it raised over $1M in 30 days (2019). It was a top-10 Canadian Kickstarter of all time. Total sales went from $0 to $48M in 48 months before a sale in October 2021. [IuoscQiQQLg](../sources/IuoscQiQQLg.md) [6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=366s), [9:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=597s)
- **NeuroGum.** The Indiegogo goal was $30K, and it hit it in 3 to 4 days. Year 1 revenue was a little over $100K. The company now makes almost $10M/month (2025-02). [BeRKQkU5Ceo](../sources/BeRKQkU5Ceo.md) [6:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeRKQkU5Ceo&t=408s), [8:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeRKQkU5Ceo&t=518s), [14:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BeRKQkU5Ceo&t=852s)

## Disagreements

No source contradicts another. The sources differ on where the first buyers come from: phone calls (Hush), an email waitlist (Sheets & Giggles) or an online community (NeuroGum).
