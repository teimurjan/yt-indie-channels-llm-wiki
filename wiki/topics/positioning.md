Positioning is the choice of which customer a product serves, which category it claims, and what it is the obvious answer for. This page collects how founders in these sources picked or changed their position and what it did to growth.

## Definition

A position is the one line a buyer repeats about the product, such as "Alia equals pop-ups" ([source](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md), [4:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLAH5OZ5FO8&t=270s)). It names a narrow category and a clear customer.

## Repeated patterns

### Learn the position from your best customers

- [Alia](../businesses/alia.md) sold a customer education tool, but its best customers paid for it as a pop-up tool. The team repositioned around pop-ups only ([source](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md), [3:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLAH5OZ5FO8&t=217s)).
- [Voicy](../businesses/voicy.md) found in user calls that 20 to 30% of users were seniors or disabled people who needed dictation to work. They were happy with the MVP, so [Kourosh](../people/kourosh.md) built for them ([source](../sources/LrLDsvbdPr0.md), [10:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LrLDsvbdPr0&t=602s)).
- "Your ideal customer profile picks you." Post everywhere early and chase the easiest market, not the biggest ([source](../sources/LrLDsvbdPr0.md), [11:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LrLDsvbdPr0&t=684s)).
- [Gaurabh Mathure](../people/gaurabh-mathure.md) moved [VueMagnet](../businesses/vuemagnet.md) from YouTubers to experts who want a personal brand on LinkedIn. The tagline became "where experts become creators" ([source](../sources/AoE1yX9ogCM.md), [6:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AoE1yX9ogCM&t=418s)).

### Niche down

- [Neural Frames](../businesses/neural-frames.md) changed "text to video for everyone" to "the platform to create music videos". A musician does not need to guess, and tells friends ([source](../sources/miEUhwSR2-0.md), [6:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=395s)).
- **Own a narrow category.** Define it narrowly enough to be number one, but keep it believable. [Flo](../businesses/flo.md) narrowed health to period tracking. Runna narrowed fitness to Couch to 5K ([source](../sources/u4tpE9AvBpc.md), [9:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=560s), [11:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=700s)).
- **Over-deliver for one user.** [Raycast](../businesses/raycast.md) built for Mac developers who live on the keyboard. Everyone else later wanted in ([source](../sources/eWGNjTLK84c.md), [8:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eWGNjTLK84c&t=522s), [10:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eWGNjTLK84c&t=650s)).
- **Go deep on the interface for the niche.** [Tiimo](../businesses/tiimo.md) dropped text rows for colored time blocks for ADHD users. Notion cannot follow, because its audience is too broad ([source](../sources/HA4yB_sd62Q.md), [1:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=115s)).

### Bet against the leader's assumption

- Each category runs on an unspoken belief about the user. A new app that builds the opposite is hard to copy, because the leader risks the product that pays its bills ([source](../sources/zH0SVLzLBpU.md), [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=0s), [2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=178s)).
- [Timeleft](../businesses/timeleft.md) removed bios, swiping and planning. Bumble had to build a second app to try the same idea ([source](../sources/zH0SVLzLBpU.md), [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=60s)).
- [Speak](../businesses/speak.md) asks users to talk instead of tap. [Evidation](../businesses/evidation.md) runs on the watch you already own, which device makers cannot copy ([source](../sources/zH0SVLzLBpU.md), [2:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=121s)).
- [MacroFactor](../businesses/macrofactor.md) dropped the shame that other macro trackers rely on ([source](../sources/HA4yB_sd62Q.md), [7:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=443s)). [Cal AI](../businesses/cal-ai.md) made the camera the home page ([source](../sources/HA4yB_sd62Q.md), [5:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=313s)).

### Compete on clarity

- Against Alfred, Raycast chose a cleaner UI, better defaults and a large central search bar instead of more features ([source](../sources/eWGNjTLK84c.md), [9:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=eWGNjTLK84c&t=590s)).
- Use speed against incumbents. Alia's pop-up rivals are 15 to 20 years old and move slowly ([source](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md), [10:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLAH5OZ5FO8&t=608s)).
- Win by being seen first. Seniors compare Voicy to 15-year-old dictation tools, not to well-funded Wispr Flow ([source](../sources/LrLDsvbdPr0.md), [11:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LrLDsvbdPr0&t=684s)).

### Roll out a new position

- Alia changed four places: website copy, content, sales calls and internal language ([source](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md), [4:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLAH5OZ5FO8&t=270s)).
- Test it in steps. Sales calls first: they got shorter and closed more often. Content next, then the website and internal words ([source](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md), [8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLAH5OZ5FO8&t=483s)).
- Alia's framework: read April Dunford's "Obviously Awesome", do the exercises with co-founders, and watch the close rate on live calls ([source](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md), [8:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLAH5OZ5FO8&t=515s)).
- A tight link creates inbound. People who ask in Slack groups or on X for the best pop-up tool get Alia as the answer ([source](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md), [5:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLAH5OZ5FO8&t=316s)).

## Disagreements

- **Message or product.** Alia changed its position with words alone: copy, content, sales calls and internal language ([source](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md), [4:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MLAH5OZ5FO8&t=270s)). [Tim Gabe](../people/tim-gabe.md) says you own a niche by going deep on the interface. In his view, a defensible position comes from building the opposite of the leader ([source](../sources/HA4yB_sd62Q.md), [2:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HA4yB_sd62Q&t=176s), [source](../sources/zH0SVLzLBpU.md), [2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=178s)).
> Conflict: [MLAH5OZ5FO8](../sources/MLAH5OZ5FO8.md) repositions through messaging. [HA4yB_sd62Q](../sources/HA4yB_sd62Q.md) says a position is won through product design.
- **Easiest market or biggest category.** Kourosh says chase the easiest market, not the biggest ([source](../sources/LrLDsvbdPr0.md), [12:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LrLDsvbdPr0&t=746s)). [Josh](../people/josh.md) of [Blue Throne](../businesses/blue-throne.md) wants a category narrow enough to lead. He still sizes it from the top 30 apps in Sensor Tower, because a nine-figure exit needs about $17M to $33M ARR ([source](../sources/u4tpE9AvBpc.md), [6:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=373s), [13:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u4tpE9AvBpc&t=800s)).
> Conflict: [LrLDsvbdPr0](../sources/LrLDsvbdPr0.md) favors the easiest market. [u4tpE9AvBpc](../sources/u4tpE9AvBpc.md) wants a narrow category that is still large enough for a big exit.
