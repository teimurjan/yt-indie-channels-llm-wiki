This page collects what the sources say about creator programs: teams of paid creators who post an app's content every day.

## Definition

A creator program is a group of creators that a brand recruits, trains, pays and manages to post its content on a steady schedule. It is closer to an in-house content team than to one-off influencer posts. See [UGC](ugc.md) and [Influencer marketing](influencer-marketing.md).

## Repeated patterns

### Prove the format before you hire

- Jay of [NomadTable](../businesses/nomadtable.md) says paying creators for unproven formats is useless ([szgT-8gXd3s](../sources/szgT-8gXd3s.md), [38:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2328s)).
- His own viral videos also prove to creators that a CPM deal is easy money ([szgT-8gXd3s](../sources/szgT-8gXd3s.md), [39:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2390s)).
- Ryan Thorp turns a video that hits target metrics at a few hundred dollars a day into a brief for all creators ([DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [13:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=788s)).

### Recruit creators, not influencers

- Ryan's best follower range is 2K to 15K or 20K. He filters with a paid test task and on-time delivery ([DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [34:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=2080s)).
- [Coconote](../businesses/coconote.md) hires marketers, not influencers. It scouts on Handshake, in DMs and through referrals ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [14:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=846s)).
- [StudyFetch](../businesses/studyfetch.md) recruits TikTok Shop creators, because affiliate pay taught them to sell ([CdBuMyrqWCs](../sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md), [23:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdBuMyrqWCs&t=1413s)).
- StudyFetch vets on comments, not views. A creator with 10K views and an active comment section wins ([CdBuMyrqWCs](../sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md), [14:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdBuMyrqWCs&t=853s)).
- Keep onboarding short: a DM, a 5-minute call, then a Notion page ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [13:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=792s)). Find 2 to 3 reliable "rock" creators as the backbone ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [13:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=835s)).
- Coconote ranks creators on a pyramid. At the base, they want to win. In the middle, they can explain why a video went viral. At the top, they create new trends ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [17:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=1029s)).

### Manage them like a team

- Keep about 1 manager per 10 creators, with a range of 5 to 15 ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [9:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=569s)). At Coconote, one coach covers 12 creators ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [18:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=1139s)).
- Founders start hands-on. They review videos before posting for the first 3 days, then step back once CPMs stay under $4 ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [11:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=716s)).
- Jay manages every creator himself in a WhatsApp group and one-on-one ([szgT-8gXd3s](../sources/szgT-8gXd3s.md), [9:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=558s)).
- StudyFetch found that contractors failed on incentives. It now has full-time scouts, creative managers and a head of the program ([CdBuMyrqWCs](../sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md), [37:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdBuMyrqWCs&t=2254s)).
- Coconote creators sit in Slack with the engineers and ask for screens that look good on camera ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [11:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=660s)).

### Measure what converts

- Engagement rate is (likes + comments + saves + shares) / views. Above 12% often goes viral ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [18:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1104s)).
- Jay's signal is 3-second retention. Good formats keep at least 70% of viewers past 3 seconds ([szgT-8gXd3s](../sources/szgT-8gXd3s.md), [27:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1660s)).
- Coconote prefers a 10M-view video that converts to a 40M-view novelty video ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [7:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=453s)).
- [Noise](../businesses/noise.md) pauses a playbook after 1 to 3 weeks without a hit ([_aNVPMvd8WU](../sources/_aNVPMvd8WU.md), [29:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_aNVPMvd8WU&t=1792s)).

### Boost winners with paid ads

- Spark a video that does 3x to 5x the account average. Start at $20 to test. If engagement falls below 5%, kill it ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [35:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2145s)).
- Do not pay view bonuses on paid views ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [29:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=1784s)).
- Ryan runs three levels of TikTok ad sets. The top 1 or 2 videos become evergreen ads that take 90% of spend ([DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [16:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=971s)). See [Meta ads](meta-ads.md) and [TikTok ads](tiktok-ads.md).

| StudyFetch influencer on a new account | About $10K/month for a daily video (2026-05) | [CdBuMyrqWCs](../sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md), [13:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdBuMyrqWCs&t=809s) |
| Ryan Thorp's portfolio | $30 to $40 per video, up to $80 to $100. $200 to $1,000 bonus for an ad that takes off | [DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [40:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=2444s) |
| Top SideShift programs | $800/month for 60 videos. New creators get $200 to $300 (2026-06) | [qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [39:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2391s) |
| [NomadTable](../businesses/nomadtable.md) | $1 to $2 CPM, no cap. Top payout about $8,000 for 8M views (2026-03) | [szgT-8gXd3s](../sources/szgT-8gXd3s.md), [40:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2435s) |
| Usual slideshow deal | $200 to $500/month for 20 slideshows (2026-01) | [_aNVPMvd8WU](../sources/_aNVPMvd8WU.md), [29:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_aNVPMvd8WU&t=1792s) |

- Do not cap a breakout creator. One creator got 180M views in 2 weeks but was capped at $4K, not about $75K. The brand lost him ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [44:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2698s)).
- On Noise, brands set the CPM they pay from their target cost per install ([_aNVPMvd8WU](../sources/_aNVPMvd8WU.md), [10:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_aNVPMvd8WU&t=647s)).

## Concrete examples with numbers

- StudyFetch: over 7M users and 2B views. It had almost 1,000 creators at the peak (2026-05) ([CdBuMyrqWCs](../sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md), [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdBuMyrqWCs&t=0s)), ([CdBuMyrqWCs](../sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md), [42:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdBuMyrqWCs&t=2578s)).
- Coconote: $6.7M ARR with 10 to 12 part-time creators at the peak (2026-04) ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [13:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=800s)).
- Coconote's "PDF to brain rot" feature got 100M to 200M views but made only about $25K ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [4:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=251s)).
- Ryan Thorp: just over 2,000 creators on the books, about 250 regular, with a team of three ([DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [34:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=2049s)), ([DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [18:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=1080s)).
- One of Ryan's creators reached 66.9M views. Copies by 20 to 40 other creators failed ([DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [20:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=1253s)).
- NomadTable: 60 creators get 30M to 44M views a month (2026-03) ([szgT-8gXd3s](../sources/szgT-8gXd3s.md), [8:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=508s)).
- Noise: over 2.5B views in about one year, with at least 100,000 posts a day (2026-01) ([_aNVPMvd8WU](../sources/_aNVPMvd8WU.md), [3:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_aNVPMvd8WU&t=212s)).
- SideShift: 850,000 creators, about 150,000 monthly active (2026-06) ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [49:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2959s)).
- SideShift CPMs: expect $5 to $8 in month one and under $3 by month three. Top programs reach $0.80 to $0.90 ([qnDdabj8Fok](../sources/qnDdabj8Fok.md), [46:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qnDdabj8Fok&t=2777s)).

## Disagreements

- **Small elite team or volume**. Coconote wants "the Navy SEALs, not the Navy" ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [13:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=800s)). StudyFetch ran almost 1,000 creators ([CdBuMyrqWCs](../sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md), [42:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CdBuMyrqWCs&t=2578s)). Noise sends out 100,000 posts a day ([_aNVPMvd8WU](../sources/_aNVPMvd8WU.md), [3:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_aNVPMvd8WU&t=212s)).
  > Conflict: [E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md) favors a small creator team. [CdBuMyrqWCs](../sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md) and [_aNVPMvd8WU](../sources/_aNVPMvd8WU.md) favor volume.
- **Strict briefs or freedom**. Ryan sets the sound, hook type, length and about 25 to 27 cuts ([DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [22:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=1342s)). Coconote gives its creators freedom ([E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md), [13:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_4gRy4O03w&t=800s)).
  > Conflict: [DcDOgWGh4bc](../sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md) uses strict briefs. [E_4gRy4O03w](../sources/E_4gRy4O03w.md) gives creators freedom.
