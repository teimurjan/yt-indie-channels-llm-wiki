This page collects what the sources say about TikTok farming: running many warmed-up social accounts on real phones to post app content at volume.

## Definition

TikTok farming means one team runs many TikTok or Instagram accounts, often several per phone. Each account is warmed up in a niche, then posts cloned or AI-made content that plugs an app. The goal is free organic reach at a scale that one account cannot reach.

## Repeated patterns

### Warm up each account on a real device

- [Louis David](../people/louis-david.md) warms up a US account with a new iPhone, a US SIM and an always-on US VPN. He watches, likes and comments in the niche 15 to 30 minutes a day for about 3 days ([LYdJ3dfNCVs](../sources/LYdJ3dfNCVs.md), [4:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYdJ3dfNCVs&t=272s)).
- [An Nayal](../people/an-nayal.md) factory-resets each phone and uses a new Apple ID and a proxy per phone. One shared Apple ID once linked all accounts and cost 120 team hours ([pb4JIH8FubE](../sources/pb4JIH8FubE.md), [0:26:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pb4JIH8FubE&t=1591s)).
- Emulators such as BlueStacks worked in 2023. They fail now because of TikTok device fingerprinting ([kMO0VYTBC7U](../sources/kMO0VYTBC7U.md), [3:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMO0VYTBC7U&t=219s)).
- Automated farms warm up with AI. Software searches niche terms, and an LLM reads screenshots to decide whether to engage ([kMO0VYTBC7U](../sources/kMO0VYTBC7U.md), [3:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMO0VYTBC7U&t=219s), [SyVVLbMgAXc](../sources/SyVVLbMgAXc.md), [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=225s)).

### Clone proven formats, then vary them

- [Clover Labs](../businesses/clover-labs.md) says about 90% of a winning campaign is cloned content and 10% is new ([ka3gqzmUuvc](../sources/ka3gqzmUuvc.md), [2:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ka3gqzmUuvc&t=163s)).
- A clone gets a median of about 20% of the original's views. Five to ten clones match the original ([ka3gqzmUuvc](../sources/ka3gqzmUuvc.md), [4:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ka3gqzmUuvc&t=291s)).
- Change the hook, CTA, avatar or background so the platform does not flag a duplicate ([ka3gqzmUuvc](../sources/ka3gqzmUuvc.md), [5:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ka3gqzmUuvc&t=346s)).
- [Double Speed](../businesses/double-speed.md) clones a TikTok from a link into a template, then changes hooks, images and text ([kMO0VYTBC7U](../sources/kMO0VYTBC7U.md), [20:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMO0VYTBC7U&t=1211s)).
- Louis David copies a working trend and puts his app on it ([LYdJ3dfNCVs](../sources/LYdJ3dfNCVs.md), [4:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYdJ3dfNCVs&t=272s)).

### Filter formats before you scale them

- An's VSC test. Viral: over 100K views on accounts under 5K followers. Scalable: you can make it 100 times. Convertible: comments ask "what's the app?" ([-VlJIgj6gM4](../sources/-VlJIgj6gM4.md), [5:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VlJIgj6gM4&t=324s), [6:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VlJIgj6gM4&t=393s)).
- An's 300-view rule. Under 300 views is an account problem. 300 to 1K is a content problem. 1K to 5K means keep the format and vary the copy ([pb4JIH8FubE](../sources/pb4JIH8FubE.md), [0:29:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pb4JIH8FubE&t=1741s)).
- Formats decay. One strategy works for a few months, a year at most ([kMO0VYTBC7U](../sources/kMO0VYTBC7U.md), [18:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMO0VYTBC7U&t=1084s)).

### Track everything by hand

- An logs each account, its phone, warm-up phase, views and hooks in a daily sheet. Winning hooks get small changes the next day ([-VlJIgj6gM4](../sources/-VlJIgj6gM4.md), [9:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VlJIgj6gM4&t=543s)).
- Owning both creation and posting gives the feedback loop that pure content tools lack ([kMO0VYTBC7U](../sources/kMO0VYTBC7U.md), [5:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMO0VYTBC7U&t=304s)).

### Seed comments

- An replies from the same account and his other accounts to steer viewers to the app ([-VlJIgj6gM4](../sources/-VlJIgj6gM4.md), [8:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VlJIgj6gM4&t=492s)).
- [Nick](../people/nick.md) and [Ivan](../people/ivan.md) run a comment farm on Instagram. Accounts look like sub-pages or friends of the founder, and comment early under rising videos ([SyVVLbMgAXc](../sources/SyVVLbMgAXc.md), [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=225s), [6:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=375s)).
- Louis David's referral codes made users post codes in the comments, which pushed engagement ([LYdJ3dfNCVs](../sources/LYdJ3dfNCVs.md), [8:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYdJ3dfNCVs&t=488s)).

### Expand by country

- Each new country adds about a 20% view lift. Adding Canada to US content is the easy first step ([ka3gqzmUuvc](../sources/ka3gqzmUuvc.md), [12:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ka3gqzmUuvc&t=766s), [15:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ka3gqzmUuvc&t=958s)).
- AI dubbing and lip-sync carry a video into new languages ([ka3gqzmUuvc](../sources/ka3gqzmUuvc.md), [13:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ka3gqzmUuvc&t=815s)).

## Examples with numbers

- [GlowUp](../businesses/glowup.md): about 7 accounts posting 8 to 12 times a day at peak. One video got close to 48M views, 60K downloads and about $12.5K in sales in a day. $800K in its first year (2025-12) ([LYdJ3dfNCVs](../sources/LYdJ3dfNCVs.md), [7:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYdJ3dfNCVs&t=423s), [8:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYdJ3dfNCVs&t=488s), [1:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYdJ3dfNCVs&t=72s)).
- [Flame](../businesses/flame.md): 50M views and 150K users, all organic (2026-02) ([pb4JIH8FubE](../sources/pb4JIH8FubE.md), [0:02:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pb4JIH8FubE&t=123s)). Later 250,000 downloads and $10K MRR (2026-06) ([-VlJIgj6gM4](../sources/-VlJIgj6gM4.md), [1:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VlJIgj6gM4&t=70s)).
- Flame view-to-download by format: memes and slideshows 0.05% to 0.2%, reaction plus app clip 0.3% to 0.5%, direct demo 0.5% to 1% ([pb4JIH8FubE](../sources/pb4JIH8FubE.md), [0:33:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pb4JIH8FubE&t=2001s)).
- A Flame slideshow with 120,000 views gave only 8 or 9 trials. Comment seeding lifted conversion to about 1% ([-VlJIgj6gM4](../sources/-VlJIgj6gM4.md), [7:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VlJIgj6gM4&t=452s), [8:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VlJIgj6gM4&t=492s)).
- Nick and Ivan: about 300,000 Instagram comments gave about 7,000 clicks for a US health app (2026-01). They ran 60 phones ([SyVVLbMgAXc](../sources/SyVVLbMgAXc.md), [34:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2080s), [37:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2222s)).
- Double Speed: one client got 4.7M views from 15 accounts in under 4 weeks (2025-10) ([kMO0VYTBC7U](../sources/kMO0VYTBC7U.md), [19:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMO0VYTBC7U&t=1149s)).
- Clover Labs: about 250M views a month for clients, $8M a year in six months (2026-09) ([ka3gqzmUuvc](../sources/ka3gqzmUuvc.md), [2:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ka3gqzmUuvc&t=163s)).

## Disagreements

- How many accounts fit on one phone.
  - Louis David runs 2 to 3 accounts per phone ([LYdJ3dfNCVs](../sources/LYdJ3dfNCVs.md), [7:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYdJ3dfNCVs&t=423s)).
  - An runs 3 to 4 per iPhone, or 2 to 5 ([pb4JIH8FubE](../sources/pb4JIH8FubE.md), [0:28:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=pb4JIH8FubE&t=1715s), [-VlJIgj6gM4](../sources/-VlJIgj6gM4.md), [11:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-VlJIgj6gM4&t=715s)).
  - Nick and Ivan say TikTok allows close to 10 per device. Their custom Android build handles 100 to 200 TikTok accounts per device per day ([SyVVLbMgAXc](../sources/SyVVLbMgAXc.md), [24:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1452s), [25:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1543s)).
> Conflict: [LYdJ3dfNCVs](../sources/LYdJ3dfNCVs.md) and [pb4JIH8FubE](../sources/pb4JIH8FubE.md) keep 2 to 5 accounts per phone. [SyVVLbMgAXc](../sources/SyVVLbMgAXc.md) puts the limit near 10 per device, or far more with ROM swapping.
- Whether platforms tolerate it. Nick and Ivan argue content that performs fills a supply gap the platform wants filled. Joseph Choi notes that platforms prefer ad spend over mass UGC ([SyVVLbMgAXc](../sources/SyVVLbMgAXc.md), [29:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1757s), [29:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1777s)).
- Risk is real. TikTok banned Zuhair Lani's shop after his 3PL failed on 1,000 orders ([kMO0VYTBC7U](../sources/kMO0VYTBC7U.md), [26:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kMO0VYTBC7U&t=1612s)).
