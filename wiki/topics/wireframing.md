# Wireframing

Wireframing is the step that sets which content and sections a page or app needs and how they are arranged, before visual design.

## Definition

A wireframe shows what content and elements are needed and the visual structure, like an architect's blueprint ([40UAK-LKheA](../sources/40UAK-LKheA.md), [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=0s)).

## Repeated patterns

- **"Frankenstein" wireframes for landing pages.** [Tim Gabe](../people/tim-gabe.md) screenshots sections from sites he likes and stacks them in a Figma auto layout as Lego bricks ([RdHXcv1k_Dg](../sources/RdHXcv1k_Dg.md), [5:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdHXcv1k_Dg&t=327s); [40UAK-LKheA](../sources/40UAK-LKheA.md), [3:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=186s)).
- **Standard widths.** 1440 px for desktop and 375 px for mobile, captured at 75% zoom so details stay readable ([40UAK-LKheA](../sources/40UAK-LKheA.md), [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=62s), [2:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=123s); [RdHXcv1k_Dg](../sources/RdHXcv1k_Dg.md), [7:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdHXcv1k_Dg&t=449s)).
- **Free responsive references.** Sections from live sites are already tested on mobile. Keep the source URLs ([RdHXcv1k_Dg](../sources/RdHXcv1k_Dg.md), [3:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdHXcv1k_Dg&t=201s)).
- **Lock and fade the reference,** then design your own version on top ([40UAK-LKheA](../sources/40UAK-LKheA.md), [4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=249s)).
- **Take structure, not style.** Copy the layout idea, use your own colors and fonts ([40UAK-LKheA](../sources/40UAK-LKheA.md), [4:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=294s); [RdHXcv1k_Dg](../sources/RdHXcv1k_Dg.md), [1:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdHXcv1k_Dg&t=78s)).
- **Wire flows for apps.** For apps, map user journeys, go deep on onboarding and light on settings, and settle states and edge cases before visuals ([wmTkiF23GRQ](../sources/wmTkiF23GRQ.md), [5:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wmTkiF23GRQ&t=325s), [6:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wmTkiF23GRQ&t=387s)).

## Concrete examples

- A typical landing page has hero, value proposition, features, call to action and footer ([RdHXcv1k_Dg](../sources/RdHXcv1k_Dg.md), [6:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdHXcv1k_Dg&t=388s)).
- Assets make up more than 50% of a layout; assets plus text make up 80% to 90%, so a wireframe without them misleads ([RdHXcv1k_Dg](../sources/RdHXcv1k_Dg.md), [8:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdHXcv1k_Dg&t=509s), [10:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RdHXcv1k_Dg&t=631s)).
- Zipsnap puts UX (wire flows) as stage three of six, after onboarding and discovery and before UI ([wmTkiF23GRQ](../sources/wmTkiF23GRQ.md), [1:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wmTkiF23GRQ&t=113s)).
- Inspiration source: Lapa Ninja ([40UAK-LKheA](../sources/40UAK-LKheA.md), [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=62s)).

## Disagreements

- For landing pages, Tim calls low-fidelity wireframes overrated: they do not give a realistic structure and the design changes later anyway ([40UAK-LKheA](../sources/40UAK-LKheA.md)). For apps, his agency uses low-fidelity layouts to get buy-in early ([wmTkiF23GRQ](../sources/wmTkiF23GRQ.md), [6:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wmTkiF23GRQ&t=387s)).

> Conflict: [40UAK-LKheA](../sources/40UAK-LKheA.md) skips low-fidelity wireframes for landing pages; [wmTkiF23GRQ](../sources/wmTkiF23GRQ.md) uses them for app flows. The difference is page versus multi-screen app.
