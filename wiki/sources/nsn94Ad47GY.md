## Summary

Jack and Nick Sweeney are brothers who built Coherence, a breathwork app that helps users regulate the nervous system. Nick turned to breathwork after a skiing injury and coaches others now. Jack ran a YouTube channel and built no-code apps. They made content first and built the app second. They tested ideas on X, then moved winners to short-form video and YouTube. A viral "vortex breath" post gave them the app idea. The app made about $85K in its first 8 months (2025-11).

## Key facts

- About $85K revenue, over 15,000 downloads, 8 months after launch (2025-11). [1:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=72s)
- Average about $11K/month, best month $22K. Just over 2,000 users (2025-11). [1:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=92s)
- MVP done and launched in February 2025. Jack wrote no code by hand. They used Cursor. [5:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=315s)
- Pricing: $40/year with a 3-day free trial, or $10/month with no trial (2025-11). [10:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=611s)
- Top video: about 8M views across platforms, 1M by the next day. [8:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=519s)
- Monthly costs (2025-11): video editors $2.5K, influencers $1K to $2K, ghostwriter for threads $800, virtual assistant $500, coding agents $300, RevenueCat $100, other tools $100. Total $5K to $6K, about 50% margin. [11:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=662s)
- Stack: React Native with Expo, Supabase, Claude Code and Codex, Mixpanel, RevenueCat. The marketing site runs on Next.js and Vercel. They also use Figma, a 3D design tool and ManyChat for Instagram DMs. [10:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=630s)
- Jack had grown a YouTube channel to about 20,000 subscribers in university. [1:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=112s)

## Tactics

- **Content first, product second.** They posted about topics they cared about. What went viral showed what the market wanted. They then built the app around it. [3:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=224s)
- **X as an idea battleground.** A post that got traction on X became a short-form script for Instagram and TikTok. Winners there became YouTube videos, a longer format. [3:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=224s), [4:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=286s)
- **Five-step playbook:** [6:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=417s), [7:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=459s)
  1. Pick a painful niche and make content about it. If you are not technical, find a technical co-founder.
  2. Batch content ideas weekly.
  3. Film in bulk: 30 to 60 videos at a time, about two months of content.
  4. Amplify winners with Spark Ads and Meta ad money. This tests whether people will pay.
  5. Outmarket everyone. Post every day, make many reels, hire clippers.
- **Polarizing hooks.** The 8M-view video opened with a polarizing hook: what "they" do not want you to know. They turned it into a series. [8:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=519s)
- **Re-film is optional.** Jack filmed the viral take three more times because he thought it was bad. The first take won. [8:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=519s)
- **Simple app with light gamification.** A streak counter, six techniques (coherence, vortex energy, focus, stress relief, sleep), a timer and inhale and exhale sounds. [9:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nsn94Ad47GY&t=565s)
