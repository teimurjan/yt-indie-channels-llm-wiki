# I Shipped My App in 12 Hours and Now It Makes $15K/Month

## Summary

Louis Pereira works full-time in his family business in India and builds apps part-time. He failed with 15 to 20 no-code tools first. Then he built the AudioPen MVP in about 12 hours during Half Day Build, a Twitter hackathon he started. AudioPen turns a voice note into clean text in a chosen style. Beta testers paid before the 12 hours ended, without being asked. It now makes about $15K/month as a focused one-feature product.

## Key facts

- About $15K/month, about 200,000 users and over 5,000 paying customers (2025-12). [1:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=104s), [2:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=120s)
- Price: $99/year or $159 for 2 years, as non-recurring plans that users choose to renew. A free tier gives a taste (2025-12). [2:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=120s)
- Prime users get about 15 minutes per recording. [9:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=563s)
- He tried no-code in 2015, came back in 2021 with Bubble, and built 15 to 20 tools before AudioPen. [2:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=171s)
- Half Day Build started in 2022: builders go from idea to at least $1 of revenue between noon and midnight on a Sunday. [3:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=206s)
- AudioPen was one of 4 or 5 tiny tools he built that week on his personal site. [3:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=206s), [4:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=254s)
- Monthly stack costs: Bubble web app about $130, Xano back end about $260, Loops email about $800, Plausible analytics $19. Draftbit for the native app costs about $300/year. AI API costs vary (2025-12). [11:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=690s)

## Tactics

- **Build many small things.** Tests cost little. Ship small tools that you can shut down without harm. Double down on the one that gets a signal. [4:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=254s), [7:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=466s)
- **DM the people who react.** He asked early fans why they liked the tool and designed for their use cases. [6:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=375s)
- **Design before you build.** He collected Pinterest inspiration and designed in Figma before the 12 hours began. He says design now sets products apart and takes longer than you expect. [6:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=375s), [7:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=466s)
- **Waitlist before the product.** About 10 hours in, he opened a waitlist. Testers then paid through Stripe before the hackathon ended. [6:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=375s)
- **Why it sold on day one.** It met a real need. He had built in public on Twitter for months to years, so he had credibility. He built hype first. The hackathon community cheered him on. [6:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=375s), [7:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=426s)
- **Launch plain, raise the price later.** Ship the simplest version that does the job well. Raise the price as the product improves. A Google Form works as a first email list. [7:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=466s)
- **Act like an indie hacker.** Do not sound like a big company. Users say they like being part of his story. [8:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=526s)
- **One pointy feature.** He was early, and he resists adjacent markets. Competitors that do too much get distracted. [10:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k2jecxFu2as&t=628s)
