# How I Finally Built a $10K/Month SaaS (30 Failures)

## Summary

Pat Walls talks with Thomas Sanlis, who launched over 30 projects that failed before Uneed worked. Uneed began as a directory of front-end tools that peaked at $200/month. He pivoted it into a launch platform, a Product Hunt alternative, at a moment when indie hackers complained about Product Hunt. Revenue dropped at first, then grew to about $10K/month (2025-08). He names five reasons projects fail and two tests for a good idea.

## Key facts

- Uneed makes $8K to $10K/month, with $10K in the month before the video (2025-08). Revenue swings with his marketing and social posts. [8:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=495s)
- About 40,000 users, about 2,000 customers who paid to skip the queue or advertise, and about 30,000 unique visitors per month (2025-08). [8:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=495s)
- Uneed sent about 10,000 clicks to listed products in the past month. He calls this his most important number. [8:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=495s)
- Before the pivot, the tool directory peaked at about $200/month. [4:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=274s)
- Failed projects include Gum Affiliates, a marketplace for Gumroad sellers and affiliates (about $500 over 2 years), Frisbee, Gidley, a Twitter feed and a bookmark manager. [1:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=81s)
- Stack: Next.js, Supabase, Bento for email and marketing automation, a Hetzner VPS managed with Coolify, self-hosted analytics, a small support SaaS, Typefully for social scheduling, and Polar as merchant of record. Tool names are spelled from garbled captions. [9:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=556s)

## Tactics

- **Five reasons projects fail.** People give up after a week or two. The headline does not say what the product does. Momentum dies when you stop posting. "Build it and they will come" is wrong. Timing is off. [2:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=153s), [3:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=198s)
- **Momentum from posting.** Each post puts your name and product in feeds again. A few weeks of silence means you rebuild from zero. [3:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=198s)
- **Ride a market moment.** He pivoted when indie hackers said Product Hunt only featured big products and big audiences. He thinks the same pivot a year earlier would have failed. [5:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=315s)
- **A good idea is one you know how to sell.** You know the market, own a distribution channel, or have a marketing angle. He knew launch platforms and indie hackers and had a Twitter following. Gum Affiliates failed because he knew neither Gumroad nor affiliates. [7:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=426s)
- **Competitors are a good sign.** No competitors means no market. Creating a market is "nearly impossible". [7:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=470s)
- **Monetize a launch platform.** Makers pay to skip the waiting line or to advertise. [8:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=495s)
- **Make it sustainable.** He does not work 10-hour days. He cycles and sees friends, and treats the business as a marathon. [10:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=607s)

## Quotes

- "Nobody's going to scroll down if they don't understand your headline." [2:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=153s)
- "If you don't know how to sell your idea, it's a bad one." [7:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=426s)
- "You're not Steve Jobs. Creating a new market is nearly impossible." [7:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=470s)
