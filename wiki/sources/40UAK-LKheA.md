# The UI/UX Wireframe Hack Schools Don't Teach

## Summary

Tim Gabe argues that traditional low-fidelity wireframes are overrated for landing pages, which are usually simple. Low-fidelity wireframes do not give a realistic structure, and the design changes later anyway. He uses "Frankenstein wireframes" instead: real screenshots of sections from sites he likes, cropped and stacked in Figma at desktop and mobile widths. This gives a high-fidelity visual and structural reference plus free responsive references. He then designs his own version on top of the faded reference.

## Key facts

- The purpose of a wireframe: show what content and elements are needed and what the visual structure looks like, like an architect's blueprint [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=0s).
- Capture widths: 1440 px for desktop and 375 px for mobile, set in the browser inspector's responsive mode at 75% zoom (not 50%), so details stay readable [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=62s), [2:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=123s).
- Inspiration source he names: Lapa Ninja [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=62s).
- He shares the Figma file in the video description.

## Tactics

- **Collect screen dumps.** Open the sites you like in tabs and screenshot them at both widths. Keep the URL visible, so you can find the source again when the nav is cropped out [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=62s).
- **Crop per section in Figma.** Change the image fill from "fill" to "crop", trim each section, and resize to exactly 1440 or 375 px wide [2:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=123s), [3:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=186s).
- **Stack sections in auto layout.** Put the desktop sections in one auto layout called "desktop" and the mobile sections in one called "mobile". The result looks like a real website [3:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=186s), [4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=249s).
- **Lock and fade the reference.** Create a "desktop container" frame on top, lock the screenshot layout and lower its opacity, so it guides you without distracting you or moving by accident. Repeat for mobile [4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=249s), [4:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=294s).
- **Take structure, not style.** Copy the layout idea (logo left, menu right) but use your own colors, fonts and implementation [4:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=294s), [5:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=40UAK-LKheA&t=355s).
