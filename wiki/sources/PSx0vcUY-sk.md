# I Got Rejected From YC, Then Built a $200M Startup

## Summary

Sabba Keynejad and his co-founder Tim started VEED, a browser video editor, in 2018 in London. They had no funding and about a year of runway. For two years they made no money, lost their office, lost their interns, and sold crypto to keep going. They took jobs and put half of each salary back into the company to pay two engineers. VEED reached 30,000 monthly users, but Y Combinator rejected them twice, once after they added 20 paid users in a weekend. Sabba then focused only on growth through SEO landing pages and YouTube videos, and revenue grew from $0 to $1M and then to $40M.

## Key facts

- Founded in 2018 by Sabba and Tim, who met online through a global hackathon a year after university. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=0s), [2:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=145s)
- First idea: basic video editing in the browser (upload, add text, trim), inspired by Giphy's GIF editor. [3:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=205s)
- They had about 1 year of runway. They asked Crisp to cut its chat tool from $100/month to $20/month, and Crisp agreed. [4:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=241s), [4:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=285s)
- After 2 years with no revenue and no funding, both founders took jobs. They put half of each salary into the company to hire 2 engineers. [5:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=346s)
- Users went from 0 to 30,000 monthly users in about 9 months, all free. [5:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=346s)
- YC rejected them after the interview. The email asked why they did not charge users. They added payments over a weekend, got 20 paid users, and emailed YC at 9:00 AM Monday. YC rejected them again. [8:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=519s), [9:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=581s), [10:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=631s)
- Revenue timeline: $0 to $1M in the first year, $2M 4 months later, $3M 2 months later, $6M 6 months after that. Later growth went to $10M, $20M, $30M and $40M in revenue (2025-05). [11:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=693s)
- The video title values the company at $200M (2025-05).
- Host Pat Walls says YC rejected him three times too. [14:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=854s)

## Tactics

- **Ship one full cycle first.** Early projects tested basic skills. Can the pair buy a domain, build a back end and ship a landing page? [2:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=145s)
- **Cut every cost.** They shared one co-working entry card, biked everywhere and negotiated SaaS prices down. [4:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=241s), [4:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=285s)
- **Fund the company from day jobs.** Half of each salary went to the company account every month. [5:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=346s)
- **Charge early.** The YC feedback was that they did not charge. A weekend of work produced 20 paid users. [9:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=581s)
- **Use one goal metric.** After the rejection, Sabba's only job was to make traffic and revenue higher than the day or week before. [10:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=647s)
- **Programmatic SEO by brute force.** People search terms like "trim video" and "crop video". He found about 500 of these terms and built a landing page for each one. He also recorded a YouTube video for each landing page. [11:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PSx0vcUY-sk&t=693s)
