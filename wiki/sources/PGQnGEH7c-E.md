## Summary

Tim Gabe fixes a weak website with seven guidelines. He says the top 1% of UI designers follow them. The guidelines are typography, spacing by relationship, grids, breaking the grid, a three-color system, strong visuals, and subtle details. He gives concrete pixel values for spacing and widths, and opacity values for text and buttons. He builds the example in Framer and promotes his upcoming Framer course.

## Key facts

- Use one font. To pair two fonts, copy the text styles of a well-designed site. In Chrome or Brave, inspect the text, open Computed, and copy family, size, weight, letter spacing and line height. [0:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=21s), [1:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=82s)
- Spacing values: 24 px base inside a text group, 2x (48 px) between text and buttons, 16 px base for smaller text. Use 80 to 96 px between a text group and an image. [2:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=179s), [3:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=239s)
- Text blocks no wider than 600 px. At least 160 px vertical padding per section. 32 px padding in cards. [4:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=299s)
- Container max width of 960 px for this design, with content split into equal chunks (2x2, 3x3, 1x1). [6:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=400s), [6:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=416s)
- Text opacity: about 80% for the largest sizes and 60% to 70% for smaller ones. Secondary buttons and cards use the primary color at 5% opacity. [10:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=624s)

## Tactics

- **Type hierarchy.** H1 with the largest body size for the hero and the final CTA. H2 for most section headings, H3 for subheadings. Drop one or two sizes for dense sections such as social proof. [1:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=82s), [2:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=143s)
- **Space by relationship.** Elements that belong together sit closer. Use a 2x multiplier for the next level of grouping. More breathing room is the safer choice. [2:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=179s), [3:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=239s)
- **Use a grid as a beginner.** Turn on the grid feature and keep most content in the max-width container. [5:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=338s)
- **Break the monotony.** Stick to the grid, then break it. Let logo sliders and carousels bleed off the edge. Center the final CTA in open space. Aim for a page flow like a wave. [7:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=472s)
- **Three colors.** A base color for backgrounds, a primary color for CTAs and small details, and a neutral for text. Base and primary need good contrast. Find palettes on sites you like or in Coolors. [9:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=562s)
- **Visuals matter.** Wealthsimple's site works almost on visuals alone. Free sources: Pexels, Figma Community, Spline community, Midjourney and DALL-E. [10:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=624s), [11:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=684s)
- **30-second Midjourney workflow.** Choose niche, style and colors that match the site. Ask for a white background. Then remove the background and upscale in Canva. [11:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=712s)
- **Subtle details.** Add a blurred gradient glow, a trend he credits to Linear. Add a small white inner shadow at the top of buttons with a faded drop shadow. Secondary buttons get a 5% primary border. Reuse the style on cards. [12:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=775s), [13:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGQnGEH7c-E&t=836s)
