## Summary

Joseph Choi hosts the Superwall channel. He talked with over 700 app founders. In this essay he explains why views are the wrong number to chase. The app has to be the main character of the video. The hook must describe the viewer's problem in their own words. Seeing an app many times in the feed drives App Store searches and does not cause fatigue. Onboarding gets users ready to pay before they really use the app. One promise has to run through the video, the store page, onboarding and the paywall. Clever pricing tricks cannot fix a funnel that breaks that promise.

## Key facts

- CocoPix: prank videos got over 30M views but did not convert best. Simple before-and-after videos sold the app better ([0:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=38s)).
- Pingo, a language app: videos of an AI voice roasting learners for mistakes went viral on Instagram and TikTok, and they also marketed the product ([1:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=92s)).
- Choi's own test: a 6-minute "idea guy" video on a new Instagram account got 200K views and about 1,000 comments (2026-10) ([2:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=146s)).
- Cal AI grew to over 2,000 influencers, and its cost per download went down as it grew (2026-10) ([3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=220s)).
- Welltory: onboarding focuses on stress and its effect on health. The paywall has no feature list and repeats the pain point ([6:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=367s)).
- Another app ran an influencer campaign with millions of views and made barely $500 ([8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=483s)).

## Tactics

- **Make the product the main character.** Do not hide the app to avoid "feeling like an ad", and do not open with "download my app". Find the part of the product people want to watch and build the video around it. Test: can the viewer picture themselves using it? ([0:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=38s), [1:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=92s))
- **Use the viewer's words, not trigger checklists.** Curiosity, social proof and FOMO matter less. Name the problem exactly as the viewer feels it ([2:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=146s)).
- **Find content-market fit first.** Before you spend, study the niche. What do people already watch? What do they ask in comments? Which results do they share? Which creators get a response without naming a product? ([3:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=197s)).
- **Do not fear repeat exposure.** Many downloads that show up as App Store search come from TikTok. People see the app several times from different creators, remember the name and then search for it ([3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=220s), [4:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=273s)).
- **Use onboarding to focus the user on the problem.** Make the user think about why they came, not tour the features. Each quiz answer must change what the app shows. Personal questions followed by a generic result add friction and lose trust ([6:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=367s), [6:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=400s)).
- **Keep one promise through the funnel.** Ad, App Store page and onboarding must sell the same result. Fix that first, then test price, annual or weekly plans, trials, anchors and decoys ([7:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=462s), [8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=483s)).
- **Track the whole chain.** Look at who watched, searches and downloads, the onboarding steps, and whether trial users stay and pay. A video that feels too much like an ad can convert well but get no reach ([8:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qh314MypshM&t=531s)).
