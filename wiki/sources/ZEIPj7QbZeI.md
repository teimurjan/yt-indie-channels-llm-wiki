## Summary

Guillaume Moubeche grew up in Paris with little money. A t-shirt business with his father sold six shirts. Next, he built a website personalization startup with developers in Russia. It died when LinkedIn changed one line of code. With $1,000 left, he built lemlist, a cold email tool, in a crowded "red ocean" market and won on personalization. lemlist grew to $10M ARR, hit a plateau, and broke through it with a "magnet persona" strategy. At the time of the video it made $30M ARR with $10M EBITDA and a valuation above $150M.

## Key facts

- Before lemlist he spent about a year at a friend's lead generation agency and booked meetings for tens of companies across industries [5:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=346s).
- The Russian startup cost him $4,000 to $5,000, all his savings, paid to junior developers [7:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=443s).
- He started lemlist with $1,000 in the bank. His girlfriend paid the rent at that time [8:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=482s).
- The first MVP took two weeks. He closed the first 100 customers with live demos and outbound [10:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=643s).
- First month revenue: $600, with growth of 40% month over month (2024-12 retelling) [11:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=706s).
- Activation (sign-up to first campaign launched) was 15%. After a full product redesign it rose to 35%. Growth fell to 0% that month, then jumped about 60% the next [12:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=768s).
- ARR timeline: $250K after year one and $1M in year two. It was about $8M in year three and $10M at three and a half years. From $1M to $10M, growth was 15% to 25% month over month [13:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=836s).
- Growth plateaued near $10M ARR. Two co-founders left. He then spent a year and a half to rebuild the architecture [15:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=932s), [16:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=993s).
- $30M ARR, $10M EBITDA, about 100 staff, customers in over 100 countries, valued above $150M (2024-12) [18:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=1092s).

## Tactics

- **Pick a red ocean.** Competitors prove that product-market fit exists. You only need to be better on one axis. lemlist focused on human, personalized sales emails and tied the product to meetings booked, a direct ROI [9:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=542s).
- **Done-for-you onboarding.** Early buyers got campaigns written by Guillaume himself. In return they agreed to appear as success stories in his content [10:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=643s).
- **Founder-as-user growth loop.** Use the product daily and help customers with campaigns. Publish the best content and push it into a community. Collect complaints, improve the product, then repeat with new templates and stories [11:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=706s).
- **Fix activation even at a cost.** He shipped a redesign to lift activation. Then he put every angry user on a Zoom call and restored removed features. He worked until 4 a.m. for a month and got a 2.3x activation lift [12:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=768s).
- **Plan the next S-curve.** Growth is never exponential. It is a series of S-curves with plateaus [14:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=899s).
- **Magnet persona.** Find the users who never churn and who make others want to join. For lemlist this was sales reps, so the brand became "the sales tool for sales teams". Apple did the same with designers [17:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZEIPj7QbZeI&t=1031s).
