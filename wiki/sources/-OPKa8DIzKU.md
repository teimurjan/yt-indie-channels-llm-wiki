# My Apps Failed Until I Discovered This Playbook ($35K/month apps)

## Summary

Loïc Berthelot is a French founder. He spent his first five years on a SaaS that nobody used. He then launched more than 10 SaaS products, and three worked: Infuspy, Minea and Drop Magic, all for ecommerce sellers and all bootstrapped. The turn came when an ecommerce owner brought him a real problem. He validated it with a newsletter, then a no-code app, then a coded app. His growth engine is YouTube creators, paid as promoters (cash or affiliate) or brought in as co-founders for equity. More than 60% of his new customers come from YouTube.

## Key facts

- Infuspy (Instagram and Snapchat influencer finder for ecommerce) reached $20K MRR in 2 months. It is now shut down [1:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=102s).
- Minea (product research for dropshippers) peaked at "750K MRR" within 2 years, as he states it [1:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=102s).
- Drop Magic (AI store builder for beginners) passed $45K MRR in its first 4 months (2025-11) [1:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=102s).
- Each of the three hit at least $35K MRR early, all 100% bootstrapped [1:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=64s).
- They work with more than 500 YouTube creators across Minea and Drop Magic. YouTube brings more than 60% of new customers (2025-11) [3:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=233s), [4:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=289s).
- Costs: about $100/month for servers and about $1.5K/month for LLM calls, because each free user gets 5 AI images. Minea ran at about 80% margin, and the target for Drop Magic is 70 to 80% (2025-11) [11:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=712s).
- Stack: Supabase, Next.js and Vercel, Sentry, Trigger.dev, Cursor and Claude Code, Nano Banana for images, Gemini and OpenAI for copy, and Loops for email [11:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=671s).

## Tactics

- **Validate cheapest first.** He went from newsletter to no-code app to a real app. Each stage kept the risk low [2:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=164s).
- **Why YouTube over TikTok.** It has the best ROI and a predictable view floor. Long videos let the creator demo the app, and old videos still convert 3 years later. You can also cut long videos into shorts and ads. TikTok builds awareness but does not convert [4:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=289s).
- **4-step creator system** [7:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=424s), [8:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=485s):
  1. Find. Search YouTube by keyword and study which creators your competitors use.
  2. Qualify. Views divided by followers must be over 10%, with at least 100 comments per video. Prefer creators whom one brand hired at least 3 times, because a one-off deal suggests the brand lost money.
  3. Outreach. Send a 7-email sequence, each email with a different angle, and also message on Instagram and Twitter.
  4. Deal. Use a fixed fee plus commission. Open about 30% low and offer two packages: more upfront with a lower commission, or less upfront with a higher commission. Test one video and look for break-even after a month, then buy a 3 to 4 video package at a lower rate per video.
- **Creator as co-founder.** It removes the distribution risk: Drop Magic's creator co-founder made $10K MRR certain at launch. The creator is a power user who brings product feedback from the audience. The team can also test scripts, funnels, pricing and angles fast at no cost. The resulting unit economics then tell you what to pay other creators [9:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=547s), [9:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=597s).
- **Kill projects by deadline.** Use simple deadlines and milestones, not emotion, to decide whether to continue [12:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-OPKa8DIzKU&t=755s).
