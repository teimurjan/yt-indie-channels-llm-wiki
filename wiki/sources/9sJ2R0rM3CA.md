## Summary

Katie Keith runs Barn2, a remote team that built 20 products over 10 years. Nineteen are WordPress and WooCommerce plugins, and one is a new Shopify app. Together they turn over about $1.8M/year (2026-02). The first plugin came from a vote on the WooCommerce Ideas Forum and grew through SEO tutorials. Later plugins came from client work and customer feature requests. Cross-promotion between closely related plugins is the core growth engine.

## Key facts

- 2025 plugin revenue just over $1.7M. About $1.8M in total with affiliate income and development services (2026-02). [2:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=156s)
- Average about $150K/month, higher in November and lower in December (2026-02). [2:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=156s)
- $9.8M in lifetime plugin sales. Premium plugins run on over 90,000 sites. 17,000 active subscriptions (2026-02). [2:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=156s)
- Plans: 1, 5 or 20 sites, on an annual subscription or a lifetime deal. Lifetime deals are about 15% of revenue (2026-02). [2:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=156s), [3:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=217s)
- Example plugins: WooCommerce Protected Categories (first), Posts Table Pro (from a client project), Document Library Pro, product tables and product options. [1:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=112s), [3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=220s)
- The first plugin ranked at the top of Google at once and made its first sales within a few days. [5:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=317s)
- Stack: ClickUp (projects, time tracking, chat), GitHub, Zapier, WordPress, and Easy Digital Downloads for sales and licensing at about $300/year. [11:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=713s), [12:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=767s)

## Tactics

- **Mine idea forums.** The first idea had many votes on the WooCommerce Ideas Forum and no existing plugin. [3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=220s)
- **Let users send ideas.** Feature requests sometimes fit better as a separate plugin. [4:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=281s)
- **Win organic search with tutorials.** Most customers come from organic search. Katie publishes helpful, unique posts for each use case, such as how to password-protect WooCommerce categories. [5:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=317s)
- **Cross-sell closely related products:** [7:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=461s), [8:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=503s)
  - Each product has one chosen companion plugin to upsell, plus two-plugin bundles.
  - Three days after a purchase, an email offers 50% off the next plugin.
  - On Black Friday, prospects and existing customers get separate campaigns. Existing customers see "discount off your next plugin".
  - Banners on plugin settings pages promote related plugins.
- **Five-step multi-product playbook for 2026:** [9:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=595s), [10:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=656s)
  1. Work in an area you already know, such as WordPress products for a WordPress freelancer.
  2. Make a long list of ideas, validate them, and keep a handful with overlapping markets.
  3. Build the one with the largest audience first and launch it alone.
  4. Launch the second as a standalone product. Cross-promote with site links, emails with discounts, beta invites, docs pages and post-purchase emails.
  5. Repeat, but do not spread the team too thin.
- **Separate products beat a monolith.** Gus notes that Katie builds different apps for the same customer and sells them separately. Customers install only what they need. AI cuts the engineering cost of many products. [14:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9sJ2R0rM3CA&t=899s)
