# My Coding Game Makes $1M Per Month

## Summary

Pat Walls interviews Lane Wagner, founder of Boot.dev, a gamified and interactive platform for learning back-end development. Lane started it as a side project while he managed a Go team and found few back-end candidates. All content is free, and members pay for interactivity. Growth went from a blog, to free 8-hour courses on freeCodeCamp's YouTube, to paid YouTube sponsorships aimed at gaming audiences. Boot.dev made $5.7M in 2024 with about $2.5M profit.

## Key facts

- Boot.dev makes almost $1M/month, about $30K/day (2025-04). [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=0s)
- It has about 30 courses and 25,332 active paying members (2025-04). [1:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=96s)
- Lane earned about $200K total comp as a manager of 3 engineers. Boot.dev made about $2K/month at the time he went full time. [3:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=181s)
- A former CFO and his partner gave $330K in angel funding. That gave a couple of years of runway. [3:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=226s)
- The idea came in 2020. Lane posted Go jobs and got only 5 to 7 applicants, because learning platforms push new coders to front-end work. [4:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=255s)
- 2024 numbers: $5.7M revenue, $300K cost of goods sold, $600K to $700K on salaries and contractors, almost $2M on marketing, about $2.5M profit. [12:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=772s)
- Stack: Go, Postgres, Google Cloud, Cloudflare, Kubernetes, Docker, Vue, Nuxt and TypeScript. PostHog does analytics, SendGrid does email and Stripe does payments. [12:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=724s)

## Tactics

- **Differentiate on content.** Serve back-end learners, a market that front-end-heavy rivals ignore. [2:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=127s)
- **Be a purple cow.** From Seth Godin's book: do not copy competitor websites. Make the product feel unlike anything else. [2:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=127s)
- **One persona, tight scope.** Solve one problem very well. Filter out requests from customers you do not want to serve. [4:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=291s)
- **MVP means minimum quantity, not minimum quality.** A poor-quality MVP will flop. [5:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=348s)
- **Growth stages.** $0 to $2K/month came from Lane's blog. From $10K to $30K/month came from freeCodeCamp collabs: Lane gave them free 8-hour courses for their YouTube channel. Past $30K/month, paid YouTube integrations with other creators scaled it. [8:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=481s), [9:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=541s)
- **Influencers borrow trust.** Trust matters most in education, and creators already have it. Boot.dev runs most sponsorships on gaming channels, not coding channels. [8:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=481s), [9:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=541s)
- **Find creators through customers.** Ask current users who they watch. Make the deal easy: shoot your own B-roll and do the work for the creator. This gets better one-off rates. [9:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=575s)
- **Product before marketing.** Do only enough marketing to get feedback until you reach product-market fit. [10:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=624s)
- **Free content, paid interactivity.** Users understand the product before they pay. In effect, it is a long free trial. [11:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=666s)
- **Founder stays on product.** Lane has about 3 internal meetings a week. He spends most days writing courses. [13:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=814s)
- **Act early and learn hard skills.** Start a side project now instead of only consuming content. Do not plan to delegate every skill. [14:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tMkpiFIW8Xg&t=873s)

## Quotes
