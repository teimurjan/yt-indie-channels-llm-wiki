## Summary

Pat Walls interviews Alex Pattis in New York. Alex runs a syndicate: he sets up a special purpose vehicle (SPV) for one startup deal and lets limited partners join with as little as $1,000. He started with a $1,000 check. He deployed about $60M into 270+ startups. He still works a full-time startup job. He explains how a syndicate works, his five deal-sourcing channels, how he evaluates founders, and how he gets paid through carried interest.

## Key facts

- About $60M deployed into 270 to 275 companies as a side project (2023-11). He splits time about 70% day job, 30% syndicate. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=0s), [5:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=338s)
- LPs can invest from $1,000 per deal. Example: the SPV puts $100K into a company. An LP who puts $1,000 in owns 1% of the SPV. [1:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=92s), [2:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=154s)
- Platforms such as AngelList and Sydecar let people with zero assets under management set up SPVs. [2:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=154s)
- Background: medical device sales at J&J, then first employee at Market Access Transformation, which later exited. He started by backing Jason Calacanis's syndicate for about a year. [3:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=214s), [4:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=276s)
- He began to co-syndicate deals with other leads at the end of 2020. Then growth took off. [5:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=338s)
- First deal: a company raised $2M at a $6M valuation. His SPV took the final $150K. It later raised at a $500M valuation. [8:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=482s)
- Deal timeline: about 2 days for the deal memo and the SPV. Then LPs get 1 to 2 weeks to commit. Then he wires the money to the company. [13:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=833s)
- Business model: 20% carry on profits above principal. LPs keep 80%. He earns money only from returned capital. [14:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=895s)
- One 50x to 250x winner must pay for 10 to 50 losers, so volume and diversification matter. [14:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=895s)

## Tactics

- **Brand yourself as the "deal flow hustle guy".** He shared deals with VCs for years. One VC took 10 to 20 deals before it joined one. Now that VC shares its own lead deals with him. [4:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=276s), [13:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=788s)
- **Five sourcing channels.** He uses five: co-syndicates with other leads, VC funds, referrals from portfolio founders, other angels, and his own LP base of operators. [9:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=544s), [10:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=606s), [11:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=666s)
- **Two selling points to LPs.** A tier-one institutional fund leads the round and sets terms, and the founders have strong backgrounds. [8:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=482s), [9:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=544s)
- **Evaluation order.** Founder-market fit first (previous roles, repeat founder), then market size and growth, early signs of product-market fit, then who co-invests. He leans on the lead fund's diligence because he has no management fees to fund his own. [11:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=666s), [12:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=728s)
- **Work the edges of the day.** Early morning calls, evening meetings and weekend coffees with founders. [5:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=p3xa6mpxvOY&t=338s)
