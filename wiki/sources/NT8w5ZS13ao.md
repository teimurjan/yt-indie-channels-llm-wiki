# Designers Are Leaving Webflow... Here's Why

## Summary

Tim Gabe gives four reasons why designers move from Webflow to Framer for building websites: the learning curve, who the tool is for, the community, and how fast the product ships. He says Framer does not pay him for the video. Webflow needs HTML and CSS knowledge. Framer feels like Figma or Sketch, and you publish with one click. He ends with a link to his Framer tutorial playlist.

## Key facts

- Webflow needs an understanding of HTML structure and CSS. A designer who knows Sketch or Figma feels at home in Framer after a couple of hours (2023-05). [0:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=43s)
- Framer has scroll interactions, parallax, hover effects and drag and drop as native features. [1:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=100s)
- The Framer team helped Dribbble migrate to Framer at no cost (2023-05). [3:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=193s)
- Framer shipped seven big updates in the month before the video (2023-05). [3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=220s)

## Tactics

- **Pick tools for the designer workflow.** Webflow can do anything, but that adds complexity. Framer makes web design "a game of creativity instead of technical ability". [1:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=100s), [2:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=143s)
- **Use the community for speed.** Tim says the Framer Discord gives several answers to a question fast, which helps on tight deadlines. Webflow has a helpful forum, but he calls Framer's a passionate community. [2:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=149s)
- **Browse the community for inspiration.** He suggests visiting it even with no problem to solve. [3:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=193s)
- **Bet on shipping speed.** Framer is smaller and ships faster. It also uses community feedback to choose what to build next. [3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NT8w5ZS13ao&t=220s)
