# How to Build an $18M/Year Apple Watch App (Free Masterclass)

## Summary

Joseph Choi interviews Assia Poloni, Chief Product Officer of Welltory. Welltory reads Apple Watch data and shows people what drains their energy. It is 10 years old and has about $18M ARR. Assia shares her six-part pitch framework for onboarding: human problem, promise, key, mechanism, proof and FOMO. The onboarding asks for health data on the first screen and then shows each user their own stress data as proof. At the paywall, fear of loss beat a benefits story.

## Key facts

- About $18M ARR and about 17M to 18M downloads after 10 years (2026-08). [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=0s), [4:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=280s)
- Over 90% of users connect their health data on the first CTA screen (2026-08). [27:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1640s)
- Paywall: $99.99/year, shown as $8.83/month. Sometimes a monthly option appears. It is a "hardish" paywall with no trial, but users can close it (2026-08). [39:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=2363s), [40:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=2413s)
- Over 60% of conversions happen after day one, over the next couple of months (2026-08). [40:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=2427s)
- About 70% of women say they feel tired or drained. Men tend to say "sometimes stressed" or "pretty good", even with worse data. [28:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1727s)
- Free tier: energy, stress, sleep score, activity and blood pressure. Paid tier: trends, a day timeline and activity advice. [41:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=2475s)

## Tactics

- **Hook cold Meta traffic with a scary inside view.** A long-term promise such as "less tired by evening" does poorly on Meta. Ads say the app shows what happens inside your body, for example "this Apple Watch app catches high cortisol". [6:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=386s), [10:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=609s)
- **Build from the ad's promise, not its features.** Marketing and product were in silos for years. The team now builds to the overall promise that ads prove: make hidden body processes visible. [8:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=531s)
- **Use the six-part pitch.** (1) Know the human problem in deep, emotional detail. Assia spent months on it. (2) Make a bold promise. (3) Give a "key" that only you know, here "sedentary stress". (4) Show the mechanism. (5) Give proof. (6) Close with FOMO. [17:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1074s), [19:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1172s), [20:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1234s)
- **Put the CTA on screen one.** "Your Apple Watch knows what makes you tired and stressed" leads to "sync my health data". Each intro screen she tested before it did worse. [26:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1608s), [27:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1640s)
- **Ask for notifications early.** It breaks best practice, but it worked best for Welltory. [28:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1689s)
- **Bring back the feeling.** One screen asks about tiredness after a day with little activity. The question slows the flow. It takes users back to the problem. [30:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1838s)
- **Use the user's own data as proof.** The app shows, for example, 192 minutes of stress on one day. Then it turns to hope: a timeline shows where the body recovered. A 30-day calendar shows stress building up, with only 20% to 30% relieved. [21:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1296s), [31:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1898s), [33:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1997s)
- **Close with loss, not benefits.** "Stress is building right now" beat an energy-bank progress chart. [35:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=2122s)
- **Match the close to the trigger.** Uber or Spotify have a clear trigger, so benefits sell. Health, fitness and language apps have no obvious trigger, so FOMO works better. [37:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=2248s)
- **Story beats copy.** Assia, a former copywriter, says the full story arc matters more than the exact words. [24:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1489s), [25:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=1552s)
- **Use AI for a story, not a data dump.** Less data and more meaning: tell the day as a story of drain and recovery. [15:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tv5hEdmbUTM&t=929s)
