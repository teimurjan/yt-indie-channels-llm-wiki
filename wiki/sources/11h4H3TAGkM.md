# The Tiny Design Rule Behind Top 1% Apps

## Summary

Tim Gabe explains the peak-end rule from Daniel Kahneman. People do not remember a whole experience. They remember the most intense moment (the peak) and the last moment (the end). Disney designs its parks around this. Airbnb puts a peak on finding a listing and ends the booking with a 3D animation. The Apple Design Award winner Ahead builds a personal brief in front of the user. Uber ends each ride with a rating and a tip. He closes with a 5-step checklist to design peaks and endings in your own app.

## Key facts

- About 70% of Disney park visitors come back for a second visit. He credits the designed peaks and endings, not a flawless day [1:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=111s).
- A Disney day is about a 6-hour, $200 experience. An app has a couple of minutes [2:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=155s).
- Airbnb peak: micro-animations and tags that show the unique value of the listing you picked. End: a 3D purchase animation after booking [3:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=217s).
- Ahead (wellness, Apple Design Award): a quiet splash screen and a self-profiling quiz. The peak is a brief that builds in front of you. The end is a gentle nudge to come back tomorrow [4:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=264s), [5:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=309s).
- Uber end: haptic feedback, then "rate your driver" and "leave a tip". Tips keep drivers on the platform, which makes supply more reliable [5:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=359s), [7:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=420s).

## Tactics

- **1. Map the journey.** Use FigJam or sticky notes to lay out every step from sign-up to task completion. Mark where users slow down, where stress peaks and where it is quiet. Keep the map as a living document [7:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=448s), [8:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=508s).
- **2. Pick one peak.** Put one "holy dang" moment after a core task, at a milestone or at a point of high effort. It can be a badge, a sparkle, an animation or surprise copy [8:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=508s).
- **3. Design the end.** Many apps just stop. Close with a celebration, a summary card or a message such as "you showed up today" [9:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=569s).
- **4. Vacuum the lows.** Wait screens, error states and long forms are remembered too. Add uplifting microcopy and help before users ask, and use delays well [9:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=569s), [10:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=613s).
- **5. Test variations.** Try changes in timing, emojis against icons and animated against static feedback. Watch where users drop off or stay longer [10:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=613s).
- **Use the end for a business goal.** Uber's ending asks for a rating and a tip at the moment the rider feels done. This lowers driver churn [5:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=359s).

## Quotes

- "Our brain doesn't remember an experience as a whole. It compresses it." [0:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=50s)
- "Negative moments are as memorable as positive ones." [10:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=613s)
- "It doesn't have to be fancy, but it should be designed with intent." [9:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=11h4H3TAGkM&t=569s)
