## Summary

Tim Gabe argues that AI is changing the rules of software the way the iPhone changed phones. Teams that bolt AI features onto old interfaces are building a BlackBerry in the iPhone era. He uses two cases of "outcome-first" design: The Browser Company dropped Arc to build Dia, and Intercom paused its roadmap to build Fin. He then gives a matrix to judge where AI can remove a flow and where the interface is still the value. He also lists edge cases where the UI must stay visible.

## Key facts

- BlackBerry once owned almost half of the US smartphone market. It lost because the iPhone brought a touch-first, app-centric interaction layer, not because BlackBerry built bad phones. [0:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=36s)
- The Browser Company sunset Arc despite backlash and built Dia. Dia aims to deliver browsing outcomes without making you browse: you ask and it answers. [1:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=98s), [2:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=164s)
- Intercom paused its roadmap within a week of the ChatGPT launch and built Fin, per chief product officer Paul Adams. [4:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=247s)
- Fin charges per resolved ticket, so the price follows the outcome. It became Intercom's fastest-growing product ever and the company's main focus (2025-12). [5:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=303s), [5:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=333s)

## Tactics

- **Do not pad AI onto a legacy UI.** Rethink what the app is. Ask what the outcome is and how many steps you can skip. [1:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=98s), [6:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=377s)
- **Use a two-axis matrix.** Axis one: does the product deliver utility or experience? Axis two: is the core task repetitive or complex? Place the product, or each flow, on the matrix. [6:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=377s)
- **Repetitive plus utility is the danger zone.** AI will automate or remove these interfaces. In Adobe Premiere, trimming gaps, syncing tracks and removing silences are rule-based. So a new editor asks: do users need the timeline at all? [6:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=377s), [7:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=438s)
- **Experience products are low risk.** In a game the play is the goal. AI can adjust difficulty or write narrative, but the UI stays, and delight matters more. [8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=483s)
- **Keep the UI visible in some cases.** Nobody wants a voice assistant to read a bank balance aloud in a co-working space. AI can still flag odd transactions or predict bills. Dense dashboards, such as investment views, need data side by side. AI can summarize trends and flag anomalies there. [8:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=521s), [9:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=582s)
- **Default to cutting steps.** Find the quickest line from user to outcome. Ask which steps are mechanical and slow today. Ask which steps users never want to do again. [9:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=582s)
- **Price on outcomes.** Fin's per-resolution model shows how pricing can follow the outcome-first design. [5:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=303s)

## Quotes

- "They're not just adding AI to interfaces, they're replacing interfaces with outcomes." [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=0s)
- "Is the interface just a means to an end, or is it the experience itself?" [8:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=521s)
- "Don't think in terms of screens. Think in terms of outcomes." [9:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=582s)
