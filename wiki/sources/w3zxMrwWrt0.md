# This app replaced my 9-5 ($150K/year)

## Summary

Chris, an app developer, built Wishlists, a simple app to store wishes and share them with family. He built it over about 6 years as a side project, first at university and then next to a full-time job. He did no paid marketing. Growth came from asking friends and family for reviews, well-timed in-app review prompts, and close support with users. The app makes about $150K/year from in-app purchases and Amazon affiliate links. He quit his job in July 2025.

## Key facts

- Revenue: about $150K/year (2025-09). Average monthly revenue is about $6K in the low season. Chris says to multiply the numbers by 5 in the high season (Christmas). [0:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=49s), [9:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=598s)
- Users: 1.1M registered users, about 4K paying customers and 110K monthly active users in the low season (2025-09). [9:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=598s)
- Margin: about 99%. Server costs are the only real cost (2025-09). [10:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=628s)
- Team: solo, no funding, no paid marketing. [0:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=49s)
- Timeline: started 2019, first release 2020, first app developer job 2021 (thanks to the app). He relaunched Wishlists 2.0 in October 2023 and hit 100K registered users at that launch. He quit his job in July 2025. [6:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=413s)
- Early 2025: he negotiated a 4-day work week to get one more full day for the app. [3:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=211s)
- Tech stack: Flutter, Cursor, ChatGPT, Firebase (back end and analytics), RevenueCat (in-app purchases), a user feedback tool, OneLink (deep links), sevDesk (accounting). [10:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=628s)
- Monetization: a paywall for premium features such as custom wish list images. Most money comes from affiliate links. Product links that users import, for example from Amazon, become his affiliate links. [11:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=661s)

## Tactics

- **Pick a crowded category and make a prettier version.** A to-do app is the usual first project, but 2M exist. He used an Excel sheet for his own wish list, so he built a nicer wish list app. [2:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=121s)
- **Fixed daily schedule around a job.** At 8:00 he did 1 hour of small tasks (support, analytics). He worked his job from 9 to 6. He built the app from 8 to 11 PM. [2:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=150s)
- **Use winters and working holidays.** Winter evenings are easier for coding. Working holidays with motivated friends mix social time and work. [3:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=230s)
- **Set small goals.** His first goal was one stranger downloading the app, not revenue. Users come first and monetization later. [5:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=306s)
- **Get reviews to rank in the App Store.** He asked everyone he knew for reviews at the start. He shows the in-app review prompt after a success moment, such as adding or fulfilling a wish. [8:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=498s)
- **Close the loop on support.** He saves every feature request and bug report. When he ships the fix, he emails that user and asks a question. He asks for a review only after a positive reply. [9:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w3zxMrwWrt0&t=559s)
