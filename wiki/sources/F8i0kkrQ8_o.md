# How We Built It: $900K Open Source SaaS

## Summary

Pat Walls interviews Marc and Iuliia, a husband-and-wife team. They built Papermark, an open-source alternative to DocSend for secure document sharing, analytics and data rooms. Papermark started as a tweet. Marc built the first version over a weekend, and paying customers arrived soon after. In about 1.5 years, they bootstrapped it to $75K MRR, which is close to $900K ARR. The business uses an open-core model: free self-hosting, a paid hosted version, and paid enterprise licenses for self-hosters.

## Key facts

- Revenue: $75K MRR, close to $900K ARR, after about 1.5 years (2025-08). [0:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=2s), [0:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=18s)
- The idea tweet got about 40K views in a few hours. The launch tweet on the next Monday got about 100K views. [1:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=77s)
- Growth: $20K MRR after year one, $75K MRR by the middle of year two (2025-08). [10:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=608s)
- Scale: about 30K users, about 1,000 paying customers, 60 contributors and 7K GitHub stars (2025-08). [10:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=608s)
- North star metric: views on shared documents, at 800K (2025-08). [10:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=608s)
- Tech stack: two Next.js projects in TypeScript (marketing site and the open-source app), Cursor, GitHub, Vercel, PlanetScale Postgres, Trigger for background jobs, Resend for email and Stripe. [10:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=654s)
- Costs: about 80% on freelancers and founder salaries, about 15% on marketing experiments and about 5% on tools. [11:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=706s)
- At the start, Iuliia did not know how to code. She built her first project on top of existing open-source code. [12:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=779s)

## Tactics

- **Test the idea with a tweet.** Marc posted that he was going to build an open-source DocSend alternative. The response told him to build it. [1:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=77s)
- **Pick a slow incumbent.** DocSend and other data room tools are about 10 years old, focus on enterprise and do not innovate. [2:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=126s)
- **Use open core to make money.** The core is free to self-host. Non-technical teams pay for the hosted version. Self-hosters pay for an enterprise license to get advanced features. [4:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=267s)
- **Use open source as a moat.** Marc lists four benefits. A free core is hard to undercut. There is no barrier to entry. The community adds R&D speed. Banks can audit the code, which builds trust. [5:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=308s), [6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=366s)
- **Build in public.** Share even small, unfinished progress on Twitter and LinkedIn. They also took part in Hacktoberfest, a month-long open-source hackathon. [9:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=546s)
- **Out-ship the incumbent.** Fast shipping gets noticed. At feature parity and beyond, customers switch. [9:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=546s), [12:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=735s)
- **Target smaller niches with AI.** Marc points at CRMs: build a simple, targeted CRM for veterinarians or building managers instead of a one-size-fits-all tool. [7:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=F8i0kkrQ8_o&t=473s)
