## Summary

Tim Gabe shows a four-step workflow to design a landing page hero section in Figma: message, desired outcome, inspiration and design. His example is his own site, which sells a weekly newsletter of UI design tips on Figma and Framer. He sets character limits for the heading and body. He picks one main action, a newsletter signup. He collects structural and style references and then builds the hero on a narrow 12-column grid.

## Key facts

- The hero section is "your first sales pitch". It must catch attention, say the message and convert ([0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=0s)).
- Heading length: 40-60 characters ([1:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=85s)).
- Body text: at most 140-170 characters, often shorter ([2:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=145s)).
- Example copy: the heading is "Weekly UI design tips and tricks". The body is "Every Wednesday you will get Figma and Framer tips and tricks to your inbox" ([2:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=145s)).
- Grid: 640px wide, 12 columns. Nav sits 24px from the top. The illustration is 72px tall, a multiple of 8 ([7:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=422s), [9:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=546s)).
- Inspiration source: he uses Land-book for structural research ([5:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=307s)).

## Tactics

- **Message first.** Find the keywords of the value proposition (here "weekly", "UI design tips", "Figma and Framer"). Use only the words the heading needs. The body adds the context the heading lacks ([1:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=85s), [2:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=145s)).
- **Decide the text-to-visual balance.** Photos suit people-focused products such as HR. 3D illustrations suit tech. Animation or simple logos also work. In his case, text leads ([3:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=209s)).
- **Pick one desired action.** It can be a call booking, a purchase, browsing or a signup. A newsletter needs an email input and a button ([4:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=276s)).
- **Split inspiration in two.** Collect structural references with enough room for your heading, body, CTA and visual. Then collect style references that match the tone. He picked a narrow grid, the visual above the text, a top-right CTA, dark mode and linear gradients ([5:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=307s), [6:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=368s)).
- **Design by mixing references.** Use Auto Layout for the nav (space between) and the copy block. If you have a style guide, use it. Add a glassy button (low-opacity fill) and an email field with a "Join for free" button ([7:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=422s), [10:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=m3NjcBDqFnA&t=606s)).
