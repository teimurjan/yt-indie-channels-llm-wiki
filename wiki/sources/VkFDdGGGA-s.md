# Why Irresistible Apps Are Designed This Way (It's Genius)

## Summary

Tim Gabe explains what he calls the "tick tock formula". The core of a sticky product changes on its own clock, with or without the user. Streaks, points and badges are common, but they do not explain why some apps win. He shows the mechanic in three products: FarmVille crops that rot, Hinge Standouts that reset every 24 hours, and Life Reset boss fights every two days. The lesson: put your hero feature on a timer so the user's own psychology pulls them back.

## Key facts

- Tim says he spent over a decade designing software for companies such as Spotify. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=0s)
- FarmVille launched on Facebook in 2009. A 4-hour crop was ready in 4 hours and stayed safe for about 8 hours. After that, it rotted. [1:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=64s), [1:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=112s)
- At its peak FarmVille had tens of millions of monthly players. Facebook then limited how often games post to feeds, and that hurt FarmVille badly. [2:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=173s), [3:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=235s)
- Hinge added Standouts in December 2020. It is a daily batch of chosen profiles on a 24-hour clock, and users send a rose to show interest. [4:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=242s)
- Life Reset went viral on TikTok as the "66-day life reset challenge". It reportedly has over 1.5 million users (2026-09). [5:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=350s), [6:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=413s)
- In Life Reset, your character fights a monster every two days. The result depends on the tasks you did in those two days. [6:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=413s)

## Tactics

- **Make something change without the user.** A product that changes only on user action gives no reason to come back on impulse. A clock does. [2:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=173s)
- **Use the Zeigarnik effect.** An unfinished task nags at the mind until it is closed. A crop that rots right now is an open loop the brain cannot drop. [1:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=112s)
- **Stack reminders on the clock.** Zynga filled Facebook feeds with friend requests, neighbor help requests and updates. The reminders kept users aware of the clock. The urge to return came from the clock itself. [2:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=173s)
- **Do not depend on one platform's feed.** FarmVille's reminder engine broke after Facebook limited game posts. [3:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=235s)
- **Use loss aversion.** A missed Standouts window is lost for good. Losses hurt about twice as much as equal gains feel good. Users open Hinge from fear of missing out, not only from excitement. [4:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=242s), [5:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=303s)
- **Wrap a boring utility in a game.** Life Reset gives a plain habit tracker characters, quests and boss fights. A visible countdown shows the next fight, and each fight plays as a short cinematic scene. [5:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=350s), [6:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=413s)
- **Apply it to your hero feature.** Ask: can the core feature run on a clock? Give it a rule that changes on a timer. It does not need to be dramatic. [7:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=457s)

## Quotes

- "Something inside these rapidly growing products keep changing on its own, basically on a clock." [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=62s)
- "To create that impulse, you need a clock." [2:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=173s)
- "It just needs to actually move on its own." [7:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=457s)
