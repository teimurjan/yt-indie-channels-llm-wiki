# The 3-Stage Trick Behind Every Addictive App

## Summary

Tim Gabe runs a design agency and was a designer at Spotify. He argues that how a user receives a reward matters as much as the reward itself. His "gift or receipt" framework says most apps deliver results flatly, like a receipt. A gift follows three stages: anticipation, reveal and celebration. He backs this with dopamine research and examples from Apple, Robinhood, Spotify Wrapped, Tinder and Snapchat. He also shows two agency clients, Game Blazers and FreeCash.

## Key facts

- Apple has a room built only to perfect box opening. The lid descends over 2 to 4 seconds through vacuum resistance. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=0s), [3:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=181s)
- In 1989 Kent Berridge found that dopamine tracks anticipation and uncertainty, not pleasure. [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=62s)
- Robinhood added full-screen trade confetti in 2016 and removed it in March 2021. In January 2024 it paid a $7.5M fine to Massachusetts regulators. The consent order bans celebratory imagery tied to trading frequency. [5:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=331s)
- Spotify Wrapped reached 200M users in 24 hours "last year" (2026-04). [7:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=474s)
- Tim says Tinder averages 11 app opens a day (2026-04). [8:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=535s)
- Snapchat: 477M daily active users, 30+ opens a day, and a longest streak of over 4,000 days (2026-04). [8:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=535s)
- Tim has over a decade of product design, for Spotify and dozens of startups (2026-04). [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=0s)

## Tactics

- **Find the gift moment.** Every app delivers something: a match, a report, a score, a streak. A "Congratulations" modal turns it into a bank statement. [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=62s), [1:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=114s)
- **Stage 1, anticipation.** Build a short window of uncertainty before the result. A Game Blazers card pack can hold common or rare cards, and the player does not know which. [3:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=223s)
- **Reveal in sequence.** Cards appear one at a time, so each one resets anticipation. Showing everything at once gives one dopamine event. Showing items in sequence gives many. [4:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=274s)
- **Stage 2, ceremony.** Give the reveal visual, haptic and sometimes audio weight. FreeCash pays earnings through a case-unboxing animation, not a plain deposit. [6:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=392s)
- **Escalate streaks.** FreeCash streaks build across the week toward a special reward chest on the last day. [6:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=392s)
- **Stage 3, afterglow.** After the reveal, give a share prompt, a badge or a stat to screenshot. Framing a result as given, not accessed, raises its perceived value. [7:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=474s), [8:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=535s)
- **Copy the ceremony, not the data.** Wrapped works because Spotify gates the data, reveals it once a year, and wraps it in sequential slides. Most copies just build data summaries. [7:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=474s)
- **Turn rewards into identity.** Streaks become something to protect, and collections become stored value. That raises the cost of leaving. [8:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=535s)
- **Respect the power of the pattern.** Regulators banned Robinhood's confetti, which shows that these effects change behavior. [5:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=331s)
