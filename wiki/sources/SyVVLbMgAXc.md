# The App Marketing Strategy No One Talks About

## Summary

Joseph Choi interviews Nick and Ivan, two founders who run an agency for automated Instagram comments. They post comments under trending videos from branded accounts on real phones that their own Android build controls. One campaign for a US health app used about 300,000 comments to get about 7,000 link clicks. They treat comments like cold email: warm up many accounts, spread the volume, and test angles. They were building a self-serve platform for "social agents" at the time of recording.

## Key facts

- About 300,000 Instagram comments gave about 7,000 clicks in one campaign for a US health app (fitness, nutrition, sleep). They projected about a 4x return at the halfway point (2026-01). [34:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2080s), [35:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2112s)
- They ran 60 phones. Each phone runs 12 to 24 Instagram accounts per day, and each account posts up to 60 comments a day (2026-01). [37:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2222s), [40:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2458s)
- A custom Android build loads and unloads the ROM, so each account gets its own "unique phone". One device handles about 20 Instagram accounts or 100 to 200 TikTok accounts per day. [25:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1543s)
- Plan: 1,000 devices, about 60,000 accounts, 3 videos and 45 comments per account per day. That gives about 1.5M videos and 21M comments a month. [39:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2394s)
- Example account funnel: about 4,900 profile visits and about 750 link clicks, about 15%. The range runs from 3% to 50 or 60%. They call 15% a good benchmark (2026-01). [47:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2827s), [47:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2863s)
- Backstory: 8 years as a team, from a Minecraft server that sold, to private blockchain work, to an AI flight search app. Their automated TikTok accounts got about 100M views. One static image got about 2M views. [17:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1047s), [18:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1103s), [19:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1196s)
- TikTok allows close to 10 accounts per device before trust and reach drop. [24:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1452s)

## Tactics

- **Train the feed first.** AI filters warm up each account so its Reels feed shows trending videos in the target niche. The system skips videos that do not fit. [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=225s)
- **Brand the accounts.** Accounts look like official sub-pages, interns or friends of the founder. The bio links to the App Store or mentions the main brand page. [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=225s), [9:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=573s)
- **Use several comment angles.** Answer a question in the thread and add a CTA at the end. Ask a question and name-drop the app. Tell a short story with a soft mention, for example "Now I use an app for my exercises". Point people to a clip on the profile. [4:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=293s), [8:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=537s), [15:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=917s)
- **Be open about the app.** The "app co-founded by my friends and me" angle beat covert ads. One such comment got about 800 likes and, by their estimate, thousands of profile visits. [10:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=610s), [11:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=673s)
- **Be early on viral videos.** A comment posted early under a rising video sits at the top. Old videos are not worth a comment. Skip threads full of noise. Reply only to people who ask questions or give opinions. [6:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=375s), [7:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=462s)
- **Warm up like cold email.** Some comments have no CTA at all, so the account looks human. This matches inbox warm-up in tools such as Instantly. [38:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2303s)
- **Rotate angles.** One angle worked for two weeks, then Instagram began to filter it. Varied prompts and examples fixed it. Early in the campaign, comments got nowhere until they changed angles fully. [16:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1005s), [36:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2165s)
- **Expect a power law.** A small share of comments brings almost all results, as with UGC videos. [35:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=2127s)
- **Copywriting is the lever.** Automation scales skill but does not replace it. They learned from many iterations, YouTube videos and the book *Influence*. [52:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=3131s), [53:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=3214s)
- **Their view on platforms.** They argue that content which performs fills a supply gap that Instagram wants filled. Joseph notes that platforms prefer ad spend over mass UGC. [29:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1757s), [29:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SyVVLbMgAXc&t=1777s)
