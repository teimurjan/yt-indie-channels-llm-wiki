## Summary

Pat Walls visits Jeremy Redman in Los Angeles. Jeremy built TaskMagic, a browser automation tool for jobs that Zapier cannot reach through APIs. He ran it with one employee, his CTO, and grew it to about $3M a year. He sold it on Acquire.com for a mid-to-upper seven-figure price (2026-04). His growth method is the "tentpole strategy": build small, specific side products that rank in search on their own and funnel paid users to the core product.

## Key facts

- TaskMagic: over 60,000 users, about 8,000 paying customers, over $400K in some months, about $3M a year (2026-04). [2:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=134s) [2:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=163s)

- Sale price: "mid upper seven figures". Over 100 buyers messaged him after the Acquire.com listing. The sale took 6 to 7 months. [3:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=181s) [11:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=696s)

## Tactics

- **Tentpole strategy.** Most founders market one product. Jeremy marketed the core product by building cheap side products that sell features, not information. Each side product ranks on its own and feeds paid users to the core. [3:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=215s) [6:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=397s) [7:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=434s)
- **Step 1: build for the customer's next problem.** TaskMagic users were business owners, agencies and freelancers who needed sales. [7:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=444s)
- **Step 2: keep it simple and specific.** Mail Lead is a simple cold email tool. Specific products rank faster in SEO than general ones. [7:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=444s)
- **Step 3: natural upgrade path.** An "automation" button inside Mail Lead opens TaskMagic. Users who hit a limit start to pay for TaskMagic. [8:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=511s) [8:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=534s)
- **Step 4: stack an ecosystem.** Mail Lead users need leads, so they built LeadQuest. Each product cross-sells the others. [9:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=548s) [9:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=589s)
- **Starting over.** His plan today: make LeadQuest the new tentpole and stack products around it. The products are one ecosystem, not separate side hustles. [10:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=643s)
- **Share the lows.** He warns against the "toxically positive" online culture and asks founders to share bad days. [13:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=837s)
- **Pat's take.** Jeremy treats products almost like content: small products made fast that help the same customers. [14:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iVy5J7iE-3Q&t=899s)
