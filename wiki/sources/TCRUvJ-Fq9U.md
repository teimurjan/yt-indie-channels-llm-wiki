# If I Had to Start a $1M App From Scratch, I'd Do This

## Summary

Joseph Choi, host of the Superwall channel and founder of Consumer Club, gives his blueprint for taking a new consumer app toward $1M a year. The order is: find demand in the App Store top charts and Sensor Tower, mine reviews and video comments for messaging, pre-validate with short-form content, ship a narrow MVP with AI coding tools, and post before launch. Then he tests 3 to 5 content ideas, pushes the winning wording through the whole funnel, and adds creators and paid spend only after a format proves it brings paying users. His running example is an app that helps people quit a habit such as smoking.

## Key facts

- Joseph's track record: has talked to over 1,000 app founders, helped over 700 with positioning and creative strategy, and built organic growth systems that brought in over $6M in revenue (2026-10) [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=0s).
- Example of a bad funnel: one founder paid about $8 per download, and about 70% of those users dropped off. The video drew curious viewers, not people with the problem (2026-10) [4:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=296s).
- Pre-build research: save about 20 videos that the audience already watches about the problem [2:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=141s).
- Content testing: start with 3 to 5 different video ideas, not one format posted daily for a month [4:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=296s).

## Tactics

- **Size demand first.** In the App Store top charts, look for apps that solve one specific problem, such as a baseball card scanner or a quit-smoking app. Check their downloads and revenue in Sensor Tower. Read their reviews for what users like, what frustrates them, and which group is underserved [0:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=32s).
- **Read the comments under viral videos.** Are people asking how to solve the problem, or only laughing at a joke? Comments about "getting through the first 2 days and starting over" reveal the missing message. "A better habits app" means nothing [1:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=78s).
- **Kill the idea if no video can sell it.** If you cannot make a video that interests the right people, scrap the idea before building. Finding a market does not get easier after launch [2:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=141s).
- **Ship only the core loop.** Pick the habit, set a start date, tap a button when you feel like giving in. No social feed, AI coach or support for 10 habits. Big apps "earned the right" to those features [2:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=141s), [3:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=201s).
- **Post before launch.** Test which angle people care about while you build. Then watch early users: do they reach the part the video promised? [3:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=223s)
- **Carry the winning words through the funnel.** Use them on the App Store page and in onboarding. If the video is about surviving day three, the first App Store screenshot should show that, not a generic chart [6:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=370s).
- **Find out why a format wins before scaling.** Vary the hook, tone and the moment the app appears, to rule out a random hit. Then brief new creators on audience, problem and format, and let them use their own voice [6:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=409s).
- **Reinvest in the proven angle, then go paid.** Put the first revenue into more creators on the same angle with small hook changes ("day five" instead of "day three"). Once more than one video works, test the strongest versions as paid ads on a small budget, and keep the organic tests running [7:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TCRUvJ-Fq9U&t=459s).
