## Summary

Tim Gabe presents a three-layer framework for apps that people cannot put down. Layer one, the experience foundation, makes the core job fast and smooth. Layer two, interface boosting, uses visual design as a trust signal and a guide toward revenue actions. Layer three, emotional design integration, adds feeling at chosen moments, as Duolingo does. He says his studio tested the framework on top App Store apps and AI apps with hundreds of thousands of monthly active users.

## Key facts

- Many founders follow the double diamond: explore wide, then go deep. When products skip the details of the last layer, they still fail. [0:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=22s)
- People make trust decisions about an app within 50 milliseconds of seeing it. [4:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=297s)
- With the first two layers done well, an app reaches about the top 10% of apps, in his view. [8:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=481s)
- Client example: Freecash, number 2 on the App Store at filming (2026-02). His studio added a tiered diamond system that lets users collect for years. [11:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=667s)

## Tactics

- **Fix the core job first.** A beautiful pizza app that takes 30 minutes to order fails. Users prefer "Hey Google, order my favorite pizza for 6 p.m." [1:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=95s)
- **Strategy before pixels.** His studio starts every project with a strategy workshop and a written strategy document. They update the document with each new insight. It doubles as prompt material for AI prototyping. [2:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=157s), [3:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=219s)
- **Then screen maps, then wireframes.** Map every flow and its features in design software. Only then make wireframes and prototypes. [3:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=219s)
- **Design is a business tool, not decoration.** Copying trendy gradients without knowing why they work distracts users. Color, contrast and hierarchy drive or block goals. [6:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=360s)
- **Contrast for money actions.** Primary calls to action pop. Secondary actions fade. Lower contrast on minor content. Use direction, color coding, visual weight and white space to guide users to conversion. [7:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=420s)
- **One test for every visual choice.** Each choice must help users see what they need at the moment they need it. [8:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=481s)
- **Emotional design where it pays.** Duolingo celebrates progress, makes you feel guilty for missed days, and builds emotional investment. Without a big budget, pick only the flow moments where emotion is likely to improve business results. [8:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=504s), [9:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=563s)
- **Design the missing human signal.** In person, emotion comes from tone and body language. In apps, you must design it, or it does not exist. Without it, users complete tasks but churn. [10:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=605s)
- **Client patterns.** Freecash: long-term levels and a visible, painful loss of progress. AI note-taker: brand the most-used daily screen. Web3 iOS app: put emotional connection at the front of onboarding to win trust. [11:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=667s), [12:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AnL49w4yIOw&t=727s)
