## Summary

Saeed Ezzati is a software engineer who spent almost a decade at startups. He built Superpower ChatGPT, a browser extension that adds features to the ChatGPT interface, two or three days after ChatGPT launched. He is the only person on it, and it makes $20K to $30K MRR (2024-08) with no ad spend. He kept the extension free for about 9 months and earned first from a newsletter for its users. He argues that building on an existing platform beats a standalone SaaS for speed, competition and validation.

## Key facts

- $20K to $30K MRR after about a year and a half (2024-08). [1:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=76s)
- About 260K to 270K downloads and about 150K weekly active users (2024-08). [1:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=76s)
- First version: plain JavaScript, HTML and CSS with two features. He had never built an extension before and shipped in 2 to 3 days. [5:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=352s)
- No paid ads and no paid articles. Growth came from Reddit posts, word of mouth and third-party "top 10 ChatGPT extensions" lists. [5:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=352s), [6:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=413s)
- The extension was 100% free for the first 9 months. [8:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=519s)
- Newsletter started 3 to 4 months in. The first sponsor paid a few hundred dollars within a week or two of listing. Writing took 6 to 7 hours a day at first, now about 2 hours. [8:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=519s)
- Stack: VS Code, plain JavaScript, AWS for back ends, Beehiiv for the newsletter (with Boosts), Passionfroot for sponsor inbound. [10:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=615s)

## Tactics

- **Build on a big platform.** Gmail, Roblox, Facebook, YouTube or Twitter bring the users. If 2,000 of hundreds of millions want a feature, that pays a living. [2:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=120s)
- **Or pick a small marketplace.** Zoom or Salesforce have fewer users but fewer builders, so competition is easier. [2:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=120s)
- **Extensions validate fast.** A simple extension that runs in the browser is a cheap test of the idea. It tells you quickly whether the idea deserves a full back end. [3:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=180s)
- **Listen where users hang out.** He joined subreddits, Discord, Slack and Facebook groups. He built what people asked for, then went back to tell them the feature existed. He calls this his biggest growth hack. [3:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=228s)
- **Timing matters.** He launched right after ChatGPT. A first Reddit post reached the top of its subreddit that day. [5:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=352s)
- **Monetize the audience first.** A newsletter for the free user base earned sponsor money before the product did. [8:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=519s)
- **Charge only for new features.** After 9 to 10 months he added paid features and kept every existing feature free. He tested higher and lower prices to find a middle ground. [9:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=583s)
- **Find AI niches through users.** Ask users what they do and how they use ChatGPT. Lawyers and doctors who do not use AI yet are open niches. [7:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=466s)
- **Accept platform risk.** OpenAI can kill the product any day. His fallback is to build extensions for other big platforms. [11:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=676s)
- In hindsight he says he waited too long to monetize, but the free period grew the user base. [8:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ty9ZcimL6VE&t=519s)
