## Summary

Tim Gabe builds the base of a design system in Figma with the atomic design method. His main point is that a design system starts small and grows from repeated use. He designs freely first. Then he turns repeated items into atoms, molecules, organisms and templates. He ends with tips: fit the system to your context, use it to save time, and use Figma component properties. The video is a how-to, not a business story, so it has no business metrics.

## Key facts

- Atomic design has four levels in this video: atoms, molecules, organisms and templates. [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=60s), [10:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=616s)
- Example atoms: an icon, Heading 1, Body Large, a large button and a primary color style. [2:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=171s)
- Example icon variants: 48x48 and 32x32, under a property called "size". [3:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=233s)
- Example button states: default, hover (lower opacity) and active (outside stroke, more opacity). [4:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=294s)
- Large systems such as Ant Design cover buttons, icons, typography, grids, dividers, layouts and spacing. Most projects do not need that much. [11:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=716s)

## Tactics

- **Step 1: design freely.** Do not plan components at the start. Watch for items that repeat, such as a heading, an icon size, a body style or a button. Repetition is the signal to make a component or a style. [1:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=90s)
- **Step 2: make atoms.** Turn the icon and button into components. Turn text into text styles and colors into color styles. Add variants for sizes and states. [2:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=171s), [3:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=233s), [4:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=294s)
- **Make molecules from atoms used together.** Body Large text plus a 32x32 icon becomes an "icon label" component. The test is simple: frequent use earns a component. [5:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=355s)
- **Step 3: build organisms.** A card combines a heading, body text, an icon and a button in an auto layout. Add state variants: neutral, success (green) and error (red). [7:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=421s), [8:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=485s), [9:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=546s)
- **Step 4: templates, as needed.** Make a component of a repeated page section, such as a card list. Then reuse it on new pages. [10:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=616s), [11:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=677s)
- **Fit the system to the team.** Tim keeps a design system page inside his working file. A solo designer, a freelancer or a small team needs speed, not a huge system. [12:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=778s)
- **Build only what you need.** Skip an XL button or a 96x96 icon until the need arises. [14:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=841s)
- **Use component properties.** Tim calls them one of the most important Figma features for design systems. [14:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_uva2dQPlV8&t=841s)
