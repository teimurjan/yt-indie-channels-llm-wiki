# Master Figma Auto Layout in 10 Minutes (2022 Tutorial)

## Summary

Tim Gabe explains Figma auto layout, the Figma version of the CSS flexbox model. He gives three reasons to use it: scale, consistency and control. He then walks through every setting in the auto layout panel, from alignment to absolute position. He ends by building a responsive proposal card from loose parts. His favorite feature is absolute position inside an auto layout.

## Key facts

- Auto layout is Figma's equivalent of the web flexbox model: a way to structure content. [0:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=22s)
- Three reasons to use it. Scale: duplicate a table row to make a table. Consistency: change row spacing in one place. Control: set 8, 12 or 16 px once for all items. [0:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=41s)
- Shortcut: Shift+A, or right-click and add auto layout. [1:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=96s)
- Settings covered: alignment, item spacing, direction, padding, spacing mode (packed or space between), stroke inclusion, canvas stacking, text baseline alignment, resizing and absolute position. [1:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=106s), [2:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=160s)
- Resizing has three modes. Fixed does not respond. Fill stretches with the parent. Hug fits the content, such as the length of a text. [3:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=232s)

## Tactics

- **Edit all paddings at once.** Hold Command and click a padding input to change every side together. [2:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=138s)
- **Decide on stroke inclusion.** An included stroke, for example 8 px, pushes the content. An excluded stroke does not. [2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=178s)
- **Align mixed text sizes on a baseline.** Turn on text baseline alignment in the advanced settings. [3:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=215s)
- **Use absolute position for corner items.** It lets one child, such as a tag in the top-right of a card, ignore the auto layout. Before this feature, he wrapped items in an extra frame. [4:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=290s), [5:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=317s)
- **Group what belongs together.** For the card, he wraps title and body in one auto layout, and the avatars and their label in another. The button sits in the same row as the avatars. [5:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=358s), [6:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=418s)
- **Set "fill container" everywhere first.** This makes the card responsive. Then style it: fill color, corner radius, clip content for the image, padding and a shadow. [6:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=418s), [8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=483s)
- **Check resizing after you nest.** A new wrapper often defaults to hug, which breaks the fill behavior of its children. [8:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=483s), [9:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Kf8L3CeiWzQ&t=543s)
