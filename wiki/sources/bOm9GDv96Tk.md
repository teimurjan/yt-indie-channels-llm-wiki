## Summary

Tim Gabe, a designer for over 10 years and ex-Spotify, asks whether AI tools replace designers. His answer is "it depends". AI tools get most design tasks about 80% done in minutes. The last 20% creates traction, and it comes from human insight: asking "why" and finding real user friction. Marketing work can run without a designer. Landing pages are an 80/20 case. Complex products still need senior designers.

## Key facts

- The tools he names: Midjourney or ChatGPT for marketing visuals. Relume to Figma to Figma Sites for landing pages. Google Stitch or Lovable for app flows. [0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=45s)
- AI gets you "80% of the way fast" on most design tasks. The specific tool matters little. [0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=45s)
- At Spotify, a designer named Daniel asked "why" about every prototype. Tim calls it the best design lesson of his career. [2:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=149s), [3:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=188s)
- As the only designer at a large crypto bridge protocol, he used Midjourney so much that marketing ran ads without design help. [5:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=303s), [5:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=357s)
- His studio offers free strategy calls to a few businesses per month. [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=225s)

## Tactics

- **Marketing visuals: AI alone is fine.** It is less pretty, but founders can make converting ads and campaigns with AI and templates. [5:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=303s), [5:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=357s)
- **Landing pages: AI does the 80%.** A scrappy founder can ship a decent page. High-end results need a designer to combine assets, copy, micro-interactions and animation. [5:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=357s)
- **Complex products: keep a human designer.** One-shot prompts give generic answers from generic data. Use AI inside a loop of orchestration and iteration on real user needs. [6:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=402s), [7:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=464s)
- **Start with why.** Apply Simon Sinek's idea to each design choice. AI knows "what" and "how", but not which question to ask. [1:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=116s), [3:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=188s)
- **Watch users for subtle friction.** Misclicks, hesitation and unexpected delight in user tests show what data and general patterns miss. [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=225s)
- **Learn from Raycast.** It did not add features on top of standard rules. It rethought how people interact with their computers. [4:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=287s), [5:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bOm9GDv96Tk&t=303s)
