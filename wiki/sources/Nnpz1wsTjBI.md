## Summary

David and his brother Daniel are non-technical founders who learned marketing instead of code. They saw AI app builders such as Lovable and Base44 grow very fast and decided that 1% of that market was enough to change their lives. They built Shipper, an AI app builder that also makes mobile apps, Chrome extensions and bots. Shipper reached about $50K/month in gross volume with no paid ads (2026-05). Growth came from Product Hunt, Reddit, competitor SEO and building in public on X.

## Key facts

- $25.6K MRR, about $307K ARR. Gross volume about $71K for the month, because of credit top-ups (2026-05). [1:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=99s)
- About 690 paid users and no free plan. 90% of revenue is subscriptions and 10% is one-off top-ups (2026-05). [1:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=99s)
- Credit pricing: the cheapest plan is $25/month. Builder credits cover building, and cloud credits cover back-ends. [0:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=53s), [1:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=99s)
- Market proof they cite: Base44 hit $3M ARR in 6 months. Lovable hit $1M ARR in a week and is valued at $6B. [4:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=258s)
- Their first success, Legit Check, a luxury goods authentication service, funded later projects. They have built, exited and acquired several companies since 2019. [2:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=177s)
- Team at start: the two brothers and one developer. Working product by month 3, caught up with competitors by month 4, ahead on features by months 5 and 6. [5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=300s)
- Stack: Crisp, Notion, Frill, an email marketing tool, Webflow, WordPress for SEO, DataFast, X Premium, Typefully, Tolt for affiliates. Technical: Anthropic Claude models, GitHub, Vercel, Railway, Neon. [5:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=348s)

## Tactics

- **Copy a big market and niche down.** Pick one common pain point in a huge, growing market and triple down on it. [4:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=258s)
- **Mine competitor complaints.** They read competitor Trustpilot pages, public roadmaps and Discord servers. Support chats asked to turn websites into mobile apps, so they added mobile apps. [8:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=485s)
- **Remove jargon.** Competitors still felt technical. Shipper promises to turn ideas into a live business with zero skills. [8:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=485s), [8:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=529s)
- **Ship rough, then iterate.** The first version broke a lot, but they shipped it anyway. [5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=300s)
- **Channel sequence, no paid ads:** [6:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=390s)
  1. Product Hunt launch in week 1 gave the first $50 MRR.
  2. Reddit posts with about 400 upvotes took them from $50 to $1K MRR.
  3. SEO on high-intent keywords: "<competitor> alternatives" and "how much does <competitor> cost".
  4. X: about day 50 of building in public, it went parabolic. About $20K MRR came in 1 to 2 weeks by dogfooding the product in public.
- **Link in the second tweet.** A tip from Rob Hallam of SuperX: put the product link in the second tweet of a thread. [6:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=390s)
- **Idea formula.** Take a big product and imagine a version made for people like you. Examples: Duolingo for cooking, or a smaller Calendly, Typeform or Intercom. Read reviews to find what users dislike. [9:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=561s)
- **Good market forgives mistakes.** Pat says that you do not need perfect execution in a growing market with good margins. [11:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Nnpz1wsTjBI&t=663s)
