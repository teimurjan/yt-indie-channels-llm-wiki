## Summary

Ken (Kenneth Law, per his LinkedIn handle) spent 12 years in big tech, last as an engineering manager at Meta. After the 2022 layoffs he no longer trusted job security. In early 2024 he used AI tools to build a small card game. Next he built Harpagia, a text-based idle RPG for fans of incremental games like RuneScape. He launched it while still employed and spent $0 on ads. One critical Reddit thread took revenue from about $1K to $10K per month, and he quit Meta that month. Starter Story producer Gus hosts this episode.

## Key facts

- Harpagia is free to play on iOS and Android. It earns from optional in-app purchases that unlock premium features or speed up progress [2:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=151s).
- Prices run from $2 to $10. Players buy at $4.99 to $9.99. Above $9.99, conversion drops [2:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=151s).
- Past-year numbers (2026-09): about $13K/month average, over $150K total, over $3 revenue per active user [2:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=151s).
- 3,500 to 4,000 MAU, 500 to 600 DAU, over 50,000 downloads. About 6% of players pay, against a 2% to 5% norm for games (2026-09) [2:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=151s).
- Costs: about $500/month for assets, freelance art and subscriptions. Ad spend: $0 [2:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=151s).
- His first app, Big Two Wild, a card game, took about six weeks. He had not written code in over 4 years [3:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=221s).
- First sale came a few days after launch, at $10. Month one: about $150. Month two: about $1,500 total [6:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=370s), [6:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=394s).
- His first Reddit post came six months after launch. Revenue went from about $1K to $10K/month almost overnight [6:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=394s).
- More Reddit posts took revenue to a peak of about $18K/month [8:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=537s).
- Stack: Cursor, ChatGPT, React Native, Firebase for auth and analytics, Discord Nitro, Vercel. He buys art from the Unity Asset Store and GameDev Market and hires on Fiverr and Upwork [11:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=706s).

## Tactics

- **Start with a small scope.** The first app was a card game he knew from high school. It taught AI-assisted coding, app store review and launch [3:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=221s).
- **Set a quit number.** His rule: if the game reached $10K/month with no paid marketing, he would leave his job [6:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=394s).
- **Post on Reddit, and welcome criticism.** His first post showed screenshots, explained the game and stressed that it was free to play. The thread was harsh, but the debate pushed it to thousands of players. Many subreddits allow self-promotion once a month, so he posts only major updates every few months [7:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=478s).
- **Serve a niche.** When players love the game, a few hundred daily players can produce over $100K a year [9:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=595s).
- **Launch early, then fix retention first.** He forced a launch at six months. Retention came before monetization [12:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=757s).
- **Buy assets, outsource art.** Prepackaged assets and freelancers let him focus on the build [11:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=axH1e2cZSrw&t=706s).
