## Summary

Tim Gabe gives four changes that move a SaaS landing page from average to high-converting. He says the average SaaS site converts under 4% of visitors and the best reach almost 12%. Change 1 is one clear call to action. Change 2 is messaging about outcomes. Change 3 is copy built for scanning. Change 4 adds a secondary CTA or an "escape valve" for visitors who are not ready. He backs each with a study or story: Unbounce data, a Columbia University candy-shop study, and Steve Jobs's iPod launch. The video promotes free design strategy calls with his agency.

## Key facts

- Conversion: under 4% on the average SaaS site, almost 12% for the best (2025-05). [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=0s)
- Unbounce study of 18,000+ landing pages: one CTA converted at 13.5%, two at 11.9%, three at 10.5%. [0:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=34s)
- Columbia University choice test: With 24 flavours on show, 3% of shoppers bought. With 6 flavours, 30% bought. That is a 10x increase. [1:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=94s)
- iPod launch, 2001: Jobs sold "1,000 songs in your pocket", not 1 GB of storage. [3:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=189s)

## Tactics

- **One call to action.** Each extra button (demo, subscribe, white paper) adds noise and analysis paralysis. The page must say "here's what to do next". [0:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=34s), [2:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=157s)
- **Sell the outcome, not the tool.** Replace "real-time notifications with Slack and Jira integrations" with "close tickets three times faster without switching tabs". [4:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=250s)
- **Goldilocks copy.** He borrows the Goldilocks principle from James Clear. Too little copy leaves objections open, and too much copy overwhelms. Write what the buyer needs and cut the rest. [5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=300s), [6:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=363s)
- **Section structure.** A headline names a pain or outcome. A subheading adds a new angle. A product visual shows context or contrast. Proof points such as logos and numbers build trust. One CTA closes the section. [6:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=363s), [6:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=414s)
- **Scan test.** For each section, ask whether a fast scroller catches what it is, why it helps and what to do next. [6:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=414s)
- **Primary and secondary CTA.** Visitors arrive at different levels of readiness. Pair "try for free" with "watch a demo" for complex SaaS. [7:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=472s), [8:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=533s)
- **Escape valves.** Chat widgets, support links and save-for-later keep unready visitors engaged. Tim signed up for a Spanish bookkeeping service because its chat bubble was one click away. [8:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=533s), [9:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TIfiy1fodDs&t=594s)
