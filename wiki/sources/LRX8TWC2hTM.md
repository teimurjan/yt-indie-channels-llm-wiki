## Summary

Thomas Mahony was an IT admin who worked with Microsoft Intune every day. Packaging one application for Intune sometimes took an hour, and forum posts showed many admins had the same problem. He built Pckgr, a browser-based tool that deploys apps to Intune in one click. It started as a side project and now makes about $60K/month, bootstrapped, with a small team (2026-03). He launched free on Reddit, then charged $25/month and grew through Microsoft MVPs who made demo videos.

## Key facts

- Revenue: about $60K/month (2026-03). He shows a jump from $447K to $910K, which looks like year-over-year revenue. [1:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=96s)
- The description says 1,000+ customers. The host says the company is 100% bootstrapped. [4:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=277s)
- First price: $25/month, set after the free beta. [3:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=193s)
- He built it before Cursor and Claude Code existed, so he wrote most of it by hand. [2:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=136s)
- Stack: Bubble for the app, GitHub Actions to build and test packages, Microsoft Azure serverless functions, monday.com for tickets, and Microsoft 365 for staff. [9:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=571s)
- Buyers ask about SOC 2 and ISO 27001 compliance. [4:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=277s)

## Tactics

- **Find the pain at work.** He hit the problem himself and then saw the same complaints on forums. Existing tools were too technical or too expensive. [2:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=136s)
- **Launch free on Reddit.** The free MVP got fast traction, useful feedback and some harsh comments. After he fixed early bugs, he added the $25/month plan. [3:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=193s)
- **Partner with niche experts.** Microsoft MVPs made demo videos for a highly targeted audience. A YouTube video keeps bringing customers years later, so the return on one payment is large. [3:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=193s), [4:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=254s)
- **Five-step niche SaaS playbook:** [5:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=335s), [6:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=396s)
  1. Build where you already have credibility.
  2. Look for pain points, not ideas.
  3. Avoid the mass market. Competitors chased companies with millions of devices. Pckgr targets smaller businesses.
  4. Charge early, even at a low price. Paying beta users proved the demand.
  5. Optimize for freedom, not scale at all costs. Keep the team and costs small.
- **Plain design is fine.** The app does one useful job and has no fancy design. [9:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=548s)
- **Ship fast.** AI and low-code tools make it easy for others to copy an idea. Launch early and iterate on feedback. [10:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=608s)
- **Low churn in boring B2B.** Pat Walls assumes churn is very low because IT teams use the tool inside companies. This is his guess, not a stated number. [11:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LRX8TWC2hTM&t=664s)
