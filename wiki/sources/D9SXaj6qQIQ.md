## Summary

Tim Gabe argues that ordinary apps keep users with the same psychology as social media. In his view, the best products win as "psychological engines", not through features or looks. He walks through six examples. Perplexity uses feedback loops, Apple Fitness uses Hick's law, and Waze uses Fitts's law. Headspace sets mood, Discord shows live social proof, and Stompers uses the Zeigarnik effect. Each example ends with a short list of rules to apply.

## Key facts

- Thesis: products that align with decision making, reward anticipation and the need for completion do not need to push users. Users pull themselves in. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=0s) [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=62s)
- Perplexity: loader animation and voice dots react in real time while it answers. [2:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=123s)
- Apple Fitness: a new user gets one clear action ("Let's go"), not 30 workouts. [3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=187s)
- Waze: core buttons are large and placed for one-handed use while driving. [4:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=245s)
- Headspace: color, animation and sound set a calm mood before a session starts. Tim says the company invested millions in this. [5:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=302s)
- Discord: lit avatars, reactions and labels like "2,312 people online" make a server feel alive. [6:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=381s)
- Stompers: packs, streaks and encouragement build loops that never fully close. [7:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=445s)

## Tactics

- **Feedback loops (Perplexity).** Make responsiveness feel smart. Motion shows the system is working. Small flickers or pulses confirm input. Design the waiting moments with care. This usually comes later in an app's life. [2:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=123s)
- **Hick's law (Apple Fitness).** More choices mean slower decisions. Give the whole app one clear focus and remove friction from the first moments. [3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=187s) [3:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=232s)
- **Fitts's law (Waze).** Bigger, closer targets are faster to hit. Design for distraction and limited attention. Show priority with size and put key buttons near the thumb. [4:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=245s)
- **Emotional anchoring (Headspace).** Emotion starts at onboarding. The app's tone tells users how to feel. Color, type and motion are psychology, not decoration. [5:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=302s) [6:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=360s)
- **Real-time social proof (Discord).** Show, do not tell. Display live activity, online presence and reaction counts. Treat community as a UX feature and keep it visible. [6:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=381s)
- **Zeigarnik effect (Stompers).** People remember unfinished tasks and want to finish them. Stack visible progress, keep the next threshold close, and treat completion as a reward. Do not show 20 loops on one screen, because Hick's law still applies. [7:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=445s) [8:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D9SXaj6qQIQ&t=484s)
