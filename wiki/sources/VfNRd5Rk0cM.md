## Summary

Ania Wysocka had a panic attack in her fourth year of university. She found no app that helped, so she built Rootd, an iPhone app for panic attack and anxiety relief. She has no technical background. She sketched the app in a notebook, made wireframes, and hired a student developer. Rootd launched in 2019 and has over 4M downloads and over $1M in revenue (2025-08). Growth came from helpful social media comments, press pitches and app store optimization.

## Key facts

- Rootd: over 4M downloads and over $1M revenue since its 2019 launch (2025-08). [0:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=59s)
- Growth timeline: about 10K downloads in year 1, 100K in year 2, 1M in year 3. [5:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=318s)
- She worked 4 days a week at a job and 3 days on Rootd for several years. About 2.5 to 3 years in, revenue covered over a year of costs, and she went full time. [5:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=343s), [6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=366s)
- She paid for the MVP from her savings. An agency was too expensive, so a student developer built it in a few months. [3:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=208s), [4:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=259s)
- App Store rating: 4.8 out of 5 (2025-08). Users feel better from a panic attack in under 2 minutes. [10:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=643s)
- She ships a release once a week. [9:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=571s)
- Press: Cosmopolitan, Women's Health and Time, all from cold emails. [8:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=491s)
- Features: an SOS panic button, a breathing tool, lessons on anxiety, a journal, over 100 visualizations and sleep sounds, and simple games. [11:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=697s)

## Tactics

- **Validate through competitor reviews.** She downloaded the existing apps and read their reviews. Users said no app explained what a panic attack is or how to get through one in the moment. [2:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=127s)
- **Ship a rough MVP.** The first few hundred users asked her to keep going despite bugs. The core panic button from the first prototype is still the aha moment. [2:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=127s), [4:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=259s)
- **Design before code.** Notebook sketches became wireframes in Photoshop and Illustrator. This cut the build time once she had a developer. [3:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=208s)
- **Be helpful in comments.** She spent hours writing useful comments on posts about anxiety and linked back to Rootd. Answer the question with content from the app, and do not lead with "download my app". [7:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=430s), [8:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=533s)
- **Pitch journalists with a personal story.** She found mental health writers and pitched them on LinkedIn or by email. Few replied, but a few features were large. [7:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=430s)
- **Run the ASO loop.** The page promises what the product delivers. Users then describe the app in reviews with the same keywords, which strengthens ranking. [8:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=491s)
- **Four-step playbook:** deliver what the page promises, listen to user reviews, ship updates often, and partner with therapy groups and B2B clients. [9:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=571s)
- **Track reviews over revenue.** She reads every review herself, even though someone else writes the replies. [10:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=643s), [13:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VfNRd5Rk0cM&t=802s)
