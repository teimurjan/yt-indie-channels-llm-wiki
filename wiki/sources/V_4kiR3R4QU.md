## Summary

Ryan is a former product designer who left a $200K job to build apps. The surname Yao comes from his LinkedIn handle in the description. He used AI coding tools, and his girlfriend Stephanie drew the art. Together they built Cat on Chair, a Pomodoro timer. During a focus session, a cat sits on your chair. At the end, the cat leaves a gift. It is his first app and made over $18K in its last 30 days (2026-04). Growth came from RedNote and from Instagram app-roundup posts, mostly in Chinese and Taiwanese markets.

## Key facts

- Over $18K in the 30 days from April 13. Close to $80K in sales over 8 to 9 months (2026-04). [1:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=84s)
- 127K total downloads. About 24K first-time downloads in the last 30 days (2026-04). [1:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=84s)
- Pricing: free app with pro features. Lifetime $29.99, annual $19.99, plus a family share for up to six people. Most sales are lifetime (2026-04). [2:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=129s)
- Peak rank: #3 free productivity app and #11 of all free apps in Taiwan. [2:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=129s)
- Timeline: work started in 2025-05. The first version was ready in August. A month of beta tests with dozens of RedNote users followed. Launch was August 27 (2025) with about 3,000 downloads in week one. A stable version came only in December or January. [4:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=256s) [5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=300s)
- Stack: ChatGPT Pro plan for code. RevenueCat for paywall and subscriptions at $200 to $300/month. An ASO tool at $10/month. A free user feedback board (2026-04). [6:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=382s)

## Tactics

- **Start where you already have an audience.** Stephanie already shared her illustrations on RedNote, so the beta and early users came from there. Now they target English and worldwide users. [5:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=340s)
- **Borrowed virality.** Instagram influencers featured the app in "app collection" posts, and this gave the big jump. One post passed 2M views. A Taiwanese user then shared it on Threads, which doubled downloads and revenue overnight. [5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=300s)
- **Differentiate a crowded category with theme.** Pomodoro features are hard to improve. So they asked how to copy the physical feel of a cat in a digital app. The cat leaves a gift after a good session and trash after a failed one, a nod to the dead-mouse meme. [8:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=505s)
- **Game mechanics for retention.** Customization inspired by Animal Crossing: cats with backstories, furniture, backgrounds and a gift shop. Tap the cat or say its name, and it meows. [7:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=433s) [9:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=553s)
- **Distinct art style.** Stephanie's illustrations give the app a personality competitors lack. [9:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=553s)
- **Ryan's idea method.** Pick an app you already use. Study competitor App Store reviews and social media. Remix mechanics from other industries, games first, since games are built to motivate and retain. Find an illustrator or use AI for a unique visual style. [10:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=629s)
- **Pat's take.** Lean into your own skill. A handcrafted look that does not feel vibe coded is a likely reason it spread. [13:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=815s) [14:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V_4kiR3R4QU&t=853s)
