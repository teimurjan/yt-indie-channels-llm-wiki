## Summary

Tom Holliday and his brother built Avenue, an embeddable booking and inquiry widget for trades such as electricians and mechanics in Australia. It shows a business's services visually, so customers can buy or inquire without a phone call. It also plugs into Google reservations on the Google Business Profile. Tom had no coding background. He funded the team from a 10-year-old marketing agency. Early growth came from door-to-door sales with iPads and printed brochures. Starter Story's Gus found the business through an anonymous revenue post on X.

## Key facts

- Revenue: A$85K/month, about US$55K/month (2026-09). Fully bootstrapped, launched about a year earlier. [1:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=69s)
- Stripe growth: A$597 in August 2025 to A$63,000 in August 2026 (2026-09). [2:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=155s)
- Timeline: a developer friend built an MVP in February 2025. Tom then hired a lead designer, a head developer and a front-end developer before any revenue. [5:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=304s), [5:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=314s)
- Funding: his agency still had 70+ clients paying for website hosting. That income paid the first hires. [5:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=350s)
- Monthly tool costs (2026-09): AWS 150, Slack 120, Google Workspace 130, Zapier 500 (AI voice credits), Twilio 4,000 for SMS. He also uses Claude. [9:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=559s)

## Tactics

- **Solve one old friction.** The customer has money and wants to buy. The widget removes the need to call or email a service business, and captures sales outside business hours. [2:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=130s), [6:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=391s)
- **Two entry points, one inbox.** A "book online" button on Google leads to a branded form. The same widget is embedded on the client's site, so all inquiries land in one place. [10:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=600s)
- **Door-to-door demos.** Demos on an iPhone made owners think it was an app. iPads showed it embedded in their own branding, and printed brochures explained it. [6:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=391s)
- **Small clients first.** Visibility with many small shops made bigger clients trust them: "I've seen you guys everywhere." [6:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=391s)
- **Let the niche choose you.** They pitched every open business, including music studios. Trades responded best. They give direct feedback and pay only for tools that pay for themselves. [7:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=457s)
- **Referrals from rejections.** Businesses that had no use for it sometimes sent them to a friend whose business fit. [8:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=517s)
- **Sell before you build.** A prospect who asks the price and is ready to pay shows an idea worth pursuing. [10:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=646s)
- **Host takeaway.** Many local owners do not use AI tools. Walking into their shops can find customers that online channels miss. A simple, unoriginal product can win in an ignored niche. [11:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=679s), [12:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=mr4YV-9MY0Q&t=741s)
