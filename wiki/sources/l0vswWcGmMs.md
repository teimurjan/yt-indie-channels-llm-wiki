# Meet The Marketing Genius Behind Cal AI

## Summary

Joseph Choi interviews Alfie Dickens. Alfie fixed chairs at a Minnesota soccer stadium, then got a two-week trial at Cal AI a month or two after it started. Over about two years he grew its influencer program from 20 to 30 creators to more than 200 posting at once. At its peak, Cal AI made about $60M a year (2026-08). The engine was brute-force email outreach, a minimum view clause in each deal, and videos that show the photo-scan feature without a hard sell. Alfie says influencer prices have risen because of agents, so today he runs UGC next to influencers. [49:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2994s)

## Key facts

- Cal AI peaked at about $60M/year, and was later acquired (2026-08). [4:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=254s), [4:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=257s)
- Creators grew from 20 to 30 at the start to 200+ at once, each posting about four videos a month (2026-08). [3:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=191s)
- Outreach: about 25 marketing assistants, each aiming at 100 cold emails a day, so about 2,000 a day. In total they contacted about 220,000 creators and signed about 2,000 to 3,000 (2026-08). [34:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2075s)
- Funnel: of 100 contacts, about 10 reply, 2 book a call and 1 signs. Alfie says reality is closer to 1 signed per 1,000. [35:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2137s)
- Signing goals were 50+ creators a month, and some months hit 100. [15:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=926s), [16:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=988s)
- One creator was paid $20K for a month and drove $120K in signups. A second creator in the same niche flopped. [16:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=988s)
- Some mom influencers were profitable at a $10 CPM. Influencer deals at $1 to $2 CPM became much harder in the last 6 months (2026-08). [17:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1048s), [48:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2926s)
- Referral bonus: about $100 to $200 to a creator for each friend Cal AI signed. [45:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2733s)

## Tactics

- **Build every deal around the "golden moment".** The photo scan of food shows the app without pushing it. Creators get no script and only need to fit the scan into their normal video. [6:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=373s), [7:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=433s)
- **Minimum view clause.** Ask creators what views they get, then set a paid floor a little below their claim. For example, use 700K to 800K for a creator who claims 1M. This protects the CPM and is easy for creators to accept. [9:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=575s), [10:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=637s)
- **Saturation can raise ROI.** Early on they chased low CPMs because people need about seven exposures. Later, viewers knew Cal AI, so each video converted better and they paid more per deal. [12:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=739s), [13:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=798s)
- **Split the niche.** Fitness had sub-niches: bodybuilders, women's fitness and weight loss. Mom creators converted but cost more. [17:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1048s)
- **Avoid food entertainment.** Recipe, cooking and mukbang videos did not convert. Viewers want to enjoy food, not count calories. Broad comedy creators gave cheap views with low intent. [19:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1171s)
- **Day-in-the-life from big creators converts best.** The creator scans breakfast without naming the app. One Mormon Wives cast member drove hundreds to thousands of promo code uses from a single video. [20:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1247s), [21:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1284s)
- **Show the app in the first 15 seconds.** Alfie calls this one of their biggest rules. [22:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1340s)
- **Time posts to trends.** They timed posts just before and after a new season of a hit show. Traffic to her page was high, so the CPM was low. [28:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1730s)
- **Email beats DMs at scale.** Instagram limits DMs per day. AI handled the first email negotiation, then passed warm leads to a manager for a call. [44:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2659s), [45:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2701s)
- **Pitch before you ask for a call.** Lead with low-key videos and a long-term deal, then ask for a call. A call makes a good deal about 10x more likely. [37:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2259s), [38:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2298s)
- **Go around agents.** Get the creator into the group chat, and use referrals to reach creators directly. [31:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=1919s), [45:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2733s)
- **Reward the outreach team.** Monthly prizes went to the assistants with the most outreach and the most signed creators. [42:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0vswWcGmMs&t=2553s)
