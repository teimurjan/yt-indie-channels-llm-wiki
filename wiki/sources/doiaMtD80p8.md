# What Founders Can Learn From Apple's Design Domination

## Summary

Tim Gabe breaks down four design principles behind Apple's software and turns each into advice for founders. The four are physical feedback, math-based visual polish, simplicity, and consistency across an ecosystem. Examples include the rubber-band scroll, the home indicator line, squircle corners, haptic clicks and the three-tap rule. His main point: small, often unconscious details build trust and habit. The video ends with how Duolingo, Revolut and Phantom use emotional design, covered in another video.

## Key facts

- Drag and drop came from Xerox PARC in 1973. Apple made it mainstream with the Macintosh. [2:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=159s)
- Over about 10 years, Apple moved users from a home button to a swipe gesture with no tutorial. [3:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=203s)
- Apple was the first major tech company to formalize human interface guidelines, starting with the Apple II. [8:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=511s)
- iOS 17 adoption reached 77% within 6 months. Android 14 reached 13% after 1 year. [8:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=511s)
- Squircle corners are now a setting in design tools such as Figma. [5:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=306s)

## Tactics

- **Use physics-based feedback.** The rubber-band bounce makes software feel like a real object with weight. Use bounce, momentum and friction in your product. [0:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=47s), [4:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=244s)
- **Add micro feedback.** Small vibrations, elastic bounces and smooth animations tell users the interface responds. They make routine tasks feel playful and reliable. [1:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=103s), [2:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=148s)
- **Teach with visual cues, not tutorials.** The home indicator line shows users to swipe. Show the action with a subtle hint instead of telling users what to do. [3:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=203s), [4:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=244s)
- **Change UX in steps.** Apple did not remove the home button in iOS 1. It waited until users were ready. [4:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=244s)
- **Use math for polish.** Squircles give smoother corners than standard rounded rectangles. Use consistent spacing, consistent rounding and ratios such as the golden ratio. [5:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=306s), [7:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=440s)
- **Design every touch point.** Sound cues, such as the camera shutter, confirm actions. Haptic Touch and the MacBook trackpad fake a physical click. This care matters more as voice and other non-screen interfaces grow. [6:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=378s), [7:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=466s)
- **Remove everything, then add back.** Cut non-essential elements. Reveal complexity step by step, at the moment of need. Hide secondary actions to avoid decision paralysis. [9:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=569s), [10:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=632s)
- **Keep key actions within about three taps.** Camera: one swipe. Silence a call: one press. Pay: double-click and glance. Optimize for the most common tasks. [9:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=569s), [10:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=654s)
- **Design for an ecosystem.** Each device works alone and better together. Learn a pattern once, and it works everywhere, which lowers cognitive load. [10:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=654s)
- **Use animated transitions for spatial continuity.** Users build a mental map of where things live. Blur, translucency and parallax keep a strict system from feeling dull. [12:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=722s)
- **Invest in a design system and muscle memory.** Keep key actions in the same place. Break a pattern only on purpose. [12:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=722s), [13:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=doiaMtD80p8&t=783s)
