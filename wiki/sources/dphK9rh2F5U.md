## Summary

Pat Walls interviews Colin McIntosh, founder of Sheets & Giggles, a direct-to-consumer bedding brand. Colin was fired twice and laid off once before he started the company. He chose a business model first and a product second. He validated with a discount waitlist of 11,000 emails, then ran a crowdfunding launch that made $284K. Podcast and YouTube sponsorships with a matching brand voice took the brand to its first $1M month in November 2020.

## Key facts

- Incorporated October 2017. First box shipped October 2018. First $1M month in November 2020 at about $1.2M in sales. [1:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=72s)
- Sales split: about 90/10 or 80/20 between Shopify site and Amazon (2024-05). [1:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=72s)
- Catalog grew from 5 colors and 5 sizes to 7 sizes, 17 colors and hundreds of SKUs. A mattress launched in late 2022. They ship about 100 mattresses a day (2024-05). [2:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=132s)
- Pre-launch: a $100 set offered at $69 for early sign-ups. This captured 11,000 emails at a 46% capture rate. [6:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=360s)
- Launch: 450 customers on day one at about $100 AOV, so $45K on day one and $284K over the campaign. [6:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=399s)
- SiteGPT chatbot costs about $50/month and cut support tickets by about 30% (2024-05). [10:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=644s)
- About 40 people touch the business daily, with agencies for Amazon ads, performance marketing and affiliates (2024-05). [12:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=728s)

## Tactics

- **Business model first, product second.** Colin wrote his business model before he picked bedding. He calls the target "founder-market fit": the business that fits your skills best. [3:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=213s)
- **Three validation documents.** Build a brand identity map, a financial model and a business model. Send the financial model to people you respect for review. [5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=300s)
- **Sell before you build.** Offer a sign-up with a locked-in launch price. Then survey the list on colors and price, so people feel they help build the product. [5:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=300s) [6:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=360s)
- **Crowdfunding math.** Plan for 30% of the goal on day one. A $100K goal needs $30K on day one, or 300 buyers at $100. Email lists convert at about 3% (2% is weak, 4% is strong), so you need about 10,000 emails. His list converted far above plan. [6:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=399s)
- **Personal contact at scale.** For years he answered every comment and support email himself and wrote all site copy. [7:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=460s)
- **Sponsor shows you love.** Facebook, Instagram, Google and Amazon ads work. The surprise winner was sponsoring podcasts and YouTube channels he watches. Each sponsorship gets a landing page in that audience's voice, and those pages convert far better than the rest of the site. [8:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=488s)
- **Polarize.** Use friends as models for real chemistry. "Be something for someone." One rule: never iron the sheets. [9:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=549s)
- **Four brand name rules.** Easy to spell, share and remember. It names what you sell. It has clean SEO with no competitor in the results. It has a .com for US buyers. Four of four is ideal, three is fine, two is a retry. [9:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=590s)
- **Stack.** Shopify, Google Analytics, Google Ads, Rebuy (in-cart upsells), AfterSell (post-purchase upsells), Okendo (reviews), TrackingMore, SiteGPT, DALL-E, and ChatGPT for first drafts. The chatbot offers code "AI" for 10% off after a good chat. [10:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=644s) [11:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=705s)
- **Mentors over lunch.** He bought three mentors lunch every week and asked questions. [3:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dphK9rh2F5U&t=193s)
