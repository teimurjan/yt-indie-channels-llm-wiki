# How I Work: $10M/Year Serial Entrepreneur

## Summary

Sam Parr describes a typical day. He is a serial entrepreneur whose companies make over $10M a year (2026-07). He works with a phone blocker, time-boxed tasks, a 5-year journal and a Claude skill that researches podcast guests. His main idea: from $0 to $5M or $10M, a founder wins with urgency and "ceaseless action". After that, the founder must delegate and stop meddling with what works.

## Key facts

- His companies make over $10M a year (2026-07). [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=0s)
- He wakes at 6:00 to 6:30, trains about 6 days a week, and eats breakfast with his kids. [0:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=34s)
- He uses the Brick phone blocker and started over a year before the video. He tried a timed kitchen container and the Opal app first. [1:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=74s)
- His commute is about 13 minutes, by scooter or subway. [2:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=153s)
- He records podcasts every Monday and Wednesday. [5:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=307s)
- He thinks he can finish only 1 or 2 real tasks a day. [3:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=224s)
- Evenings: home by 5:30 to 6:00, dinner with kids, eBay and Claude from 8:30 to 10:00, reading from 10:00 to 10:45. [9:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=565s)

## Tactics

- **Do not compare yourself.** He has seen behind the scenes of many businesses, and everyone has complaints. In 9 of 10 cases, focus on your one thing beats starting something new. [1:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=110s)
- **Build systems, not intentions.** A 5-year journal shows the same complaint on the same date across years. Change needs a system, for example a hired coach. [2:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=153s)
- **Time-box everything.** Write the task down in the morning, then put it on the calendar. He calls himself lazy, so only specific, time-bound tasks get done. [3:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=224s)
- **Do not chase numbers.** Topics picked only for views make him dislike podcasting. Guests who break his frame make him enjoy it. [3:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=224s)
- **AI prep.** A Claude skill reads his calendar, finds the day's guest and writes research. Then he reads books on the topic. [5:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=307s)
- **Walk the office.** After recording, he wanders, asks people what is wrong and fixes it. [5:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=307s)
- **Quantity beats perfection.** He cites a pottery-class study. One group had to make many pots, and the other had to make one perfect pot. The first group made better pots and was happier. [6:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=377s)
- **"Zebra calendar" from $0 to $10M.** He books back-to-back 15-minute customer calls with 5-minute breaks. He writes the website copy and does the design himself. [6:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=377s)
- **Delegate at $5M to $10M.** Hire, then leave the compounding business alone. He uses a side "sandbox" project to keep his hands busy. [7:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=437s)
- **Pick ideas by track record.** A beginner needs to act now and pivot later. A proven operator can spend time to pick an idea in the middle of their ikigai: what the world wants, what it pays for, what you are good at, and what you love. [8:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=490s)
- **Cool stuff seems dumb at first.** In year one of The Hustle, people asked what his real job was. Now everyone understands a newsletter or a podcast. [8:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=jjUf7lrb_xQ&t=532s)
