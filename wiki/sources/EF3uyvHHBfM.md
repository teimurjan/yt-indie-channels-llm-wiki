## Summary

Angus Cheng is a developer in Hong Kong. In 2021 he quit his job and built Bank Statement Converter, a website that turns PDF bank statements into spreadsheets. It does one thing and makes $40K MRR (2025-07). He runs it alone with no employees and no outside money. Paid ads, blogging and cold email all failed. Growth came from organic users once he stopped ads and focused on the product.

## Key facts

- MRR by year: 2022 $6K, 2023 $14K, 2024 $27K, and $40K at the time of the video (2025-07). [3:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=214s)
- 75,000 total users, about 1,000 paying customers, about 40,000 visitors a month (2025-07). [3:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=231s)
- About $39K of the $40K monthly revenue is profit (2025-07). [8:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=500s)
- MVP took about a week with a friend, who left after a few weeks. Angus now does development, support, sales and marketing. [1:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=95s), [6:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=370s)
- Google search ads: about $1,000 spent per $300 in sales. Never profitable. He cut them after about 6 months. [4:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=247s)
- Cold email: 3 months, 1 sale, many angry replies. [4:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=247s)
- It took 2 to 3 years before revenue paid his rent. [9:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=568s)
- Stack: core engine in Kotlin (JVM), Next.js front end on Netlify, back end on AWS EC2, Brevo for email, Stripe for payments. [2:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=154s), [8:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=500s)
- Background: he made indie Xbox Live games from 2010 to 2014. He also built Girlfriend Plus, a virtual girlfriend app. He worked at an investment bank in Hong Kong from 2017 to 2019 and at a crypto exchange in 2020. [7:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=477s)

## Tactics

- **Solve your own pain.** He wanted years of spending data in Excel to plan his runway. His bank gave only PDFs, and parsing them was hard. So it was a real problem. [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=62s)
- **Validate with a live MVP and ads.** He bought a domain, launched in a week, and bought Google ads. People uploaded bank statements at once. [1:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=95s)
- **Build first.** An MVP that takes 1 or 2 weeks is a small risk, so pre-launch validation adds little. [2:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=124s)
- **Lean loop.** Show the product to the target audience, not to friends and family. Build features from support emails and analytics drop-offs. Stop work on unused features. [3:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=188s)
- **Stop ads to see organic demand.** With ads off, he still got 2 to 3 signups a day. He then fixed every customer complaint. [4:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=292s)
- **Skip social media.** His users are not on Twitter or his blog. He advises against brand pages on Facebook, Instagram and Twitter. [4:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=247s), [9:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=568s)
- **Go after small markets.** The market is too small for a VC. For one person it is enough. [6:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=409s)
- **Save years of runway.** A few months of runway is not enough. Early hard work pays off in year 4 and later. [8:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=532s), [9:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=EF3uyvHHBfM&t=568s)
