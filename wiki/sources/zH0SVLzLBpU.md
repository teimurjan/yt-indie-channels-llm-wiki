# The Secret Behind Apps Big Players Can't Copy

## Summary

Tim Gabe, who runs the design agency ZipSnap, explains the "hidden assumption". Each app category runs on an unspoken belief about the user, and the leader built its business on it. A newer app that builds the opposite is hard to copy, because the leader risks the product that pays its bills. He shows three cases: Timeleft against profiles and swiping, Speak against tap-through language games, and Evidation against wearables you must buy. The strongest bet goes against the assumption the leader makes its money from.

## Key facts

- Timeleft seats you at dinner with 5 strangers. You do not choose the people or the restaurant. About 2 years in, it was on track for $16M a year. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=0s)
- Timeleft's homepage line: "No bios, no swiping, no planning." Users take a personality quiz, join a group, and learn the restaurant just before dinner. [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=60s)
- Timeleft brought together about 120,000 people a month (2025-08). [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=60s)
- Bumble launched a separate group-meetup app in June 2026 (captioned as "Plants"). In August 2026 it said it planned to move away from the swipe gradually. [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=60s)
- Speak builds lessons around speaking out loud. It announced over $100M in annualized revenue (2025-11). [2:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=121s)
- Evidation is software-only health tracking on the watch you already own. It said it grew more than 8x in a year and raised a Series A from General Catalyst (2025-10). [2:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=121s), [2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=178s)
- Tim has over a decade of software design work, with clients such as Spotify. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=0s)

## Tactics

- **Find the hidden assumption.** List what every big app in your category assumes about the user. Circle the one their business depends on. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=0s), [2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=178s)
- **Flip what users want to do.** Meeting apps assume you want to look people up first. Timeleft removed choice. Bumble, whose main app depends on swiping, had to build a second app to try the opposite. [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=60s)
- **Flip how much effort users give.** Language apps assume a quick tap game. Speak asks users to talk and picks results over fun. Game-style apps can add speaking, but they cannot put it first without asking more of their users. [2:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=121s)
- **Flip what users must buy.** Tracking apps assume you buy a ring or band plus a membership. Evidation runs on the watch you own. Device makers cannot follow, because that tells customers to skip what they sell. [2:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=121s), [2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=178s)
- **Aim at the revenue source.** The best assumption to bet against is the one the leader makes money from. It cannot afford to drop it. [2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=178s)
- **Client case.** Postdocs.ai, an AI grant-writing tool, assumed users wanted a full draft at once ("Boom, you got 20 pages"). ZipSnap added a plan step that made the surprise after generation much smaller. [3:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=223s)
- Tim offers free monthly design strategy calls through ZipSnap. [3:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zH0SVLzLBpU&t=223s)
