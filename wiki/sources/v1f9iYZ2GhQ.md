## Summary

Tim Gabe says founders try to grow with more ads and traffic, but they pour users into a leaky bucket. He offers a four-step product funnel for growth through design. Step 1 is a 3-second trust test for first impressions. Step 2 is the right amount of friction in onboarding and across the app. Step 3 is "win mapping": ask for upgrades, ratings and shares right after a user wins. Step 4 is emotional design that makes the app hard to replace. He backs each step with examples from his agency's client work.

## Key facts

- People form first impressions in about 50 milliseconds and make trust decisions in under 3 seconds. Visual design drives most of that impression. [1:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=98s)
- A bad first impression can lose 20 to 30% of new users at once, with that share of potential revenue. [1:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=98s)
- Tim claims 99% of apps do not reach real commercial success, partly because users feel weak design fundamentals without knowing why. [2:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=158s)
- Research he cites: designs that evoke positive feelings through motion, feedback and human touches are remembered, recommended and revisited more. [10:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=612s)

## Tactics

- **Fix the leak before you buy traffic.** A leaky product funnel wastes revenue, even with positive ad ROI. [0:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=56s)
- **Step 1, pass the 3-second test.** Bad top-of-funnel screens are cluttered and have poor hierarchy, uneven spacing, cheap imagery and vague copy. Use five fixes on the website and App Store screenshots: clean hierarchy, generous spacing, professional imagery that supports the headline, balanced typography, and clear copy about the outcome. [2:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=158s), [3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=220s)
- **Ask: trust builder or trust killer?** Without trust on the first screen, users leave for a competitor they trust. [3:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=220s)
- **Step 2, find the friction sweet spot.** Shorter onboarding is usually the biggest win, but too little friction leaves users lost. For Spark, the welcome screen became an Instagram-story-style carousel. Card setup, the reason users download the app, appears within the first five screens. [4:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=262s), [5:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=324s)
- **Hide secondary data in expandable tooltips.** A crypto trading client kept detailed financial data one tap away instead of on every screen. [5:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=324s), [6:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=386s)
- **Step 3, win mapping.** The peak-end rule says people judge an experience by its most intense moment and its end. So the timing of an ask matters more than the ask. Map each flow's biggest wins and put paywalls, rating prompts and share prompts there. Do not put them at moments of frustration or confusion. [7:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=443s), [8:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=506s)
- **Amplify the win.** For a lending and borrowing platform, the team found peak moments in early UX work and added emotion to them. Users then return the favor or brag, which makes the app a viral engine. [8:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=506s), [9:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=566s)
- **Step 4, emotional design.** Purely functional apps lose users to a competitor's Black Friday sale. For an AI transcription client, the team designed voice-linked audio waves so the tech feels alive. [10:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=612s), [11:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=673s)
