# he had nothing.. until he built a stupid simple $30k/mo app with AI

## Summary

Joseph Choi interviews Dmitri, who built YourMove AI, an AI dating assistant, as a side project while he was a data science manager at Opendoor. It started in 2022 as a party joke on OpenAI's Davinci model. It peaked at about $30K/month at about 80% margin before he sold it. Press coverage brought few users but gave the site strong domain authority. That powered an SEO machine that drove about half of all traffic. He used his salary to pay 10 to 12 Upwork contractors. He quit at about $18K/month and got a layoff severance at the same time.

## Key facts

- YourMove AI writes replies, writes and reviews profiles, and makes AI photos. It peaked at about $30K/month with about 80% margin (2026-01). At the time he stopped work on it, it made $28K/month. [3:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=189s), [7:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=420s)
- He sold the company about 4 to 5 months before the interview. He did not share the price. [7:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=420s)
- Timeline: 6 months before he charged anything, about another year to $1K/month, a year to $10K/month, and a year more to $28K/month. It took about 2.5 years. [0:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=34s), [7:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=448s)
- It started about a year before the RizzGPT wave. The team tried about 12 products, and the ones that made money stayed. [3:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=239s)
- Monetization: a soft paywall on the core product and a hard paywall on AI photos, because of compute cost. [6:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=392s)
- Press included Fox, CNBC, Wired, the Washington Post, the Wall Street Journal and a Newsweek op-ed. One Fox Business spot got fewer views than one good Reddit post. [8:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=510s), [12:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=765s)
- At one point he had 10 to 12 Upwork contractors. Most worked a few hours a week, and a support person cost $5/hour. [43:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=2594s)
- His quit target was $20K/month revenue, which is about $10K/month profit. He left at $17K to $18K. Opendoor's November layoff gave him severance. [51:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=3065s), [53:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=3183s)
- His first dollar came about 4 years after he started trying. Afterwards he joined a16z Speedrun with a new venture-backed company. [34:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=2071s), [50:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=3003s)

## Tactics

- **Feel the pull.** Friends asked to use a half-baked tool built in a weekend. Dmitri had never felt that pull on earlier ideas. [5:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=332s)
- **Charge early.** He waited 6 months to monetize. That was a mistake that a mentor fixed. [6:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=392s), [42:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=2565s)
- **Turn PR into SEO.** Press links gave high domain authority from the start, with no paid backlinks. He then targeted low-competition, high-volume keywords such as pickup lines and "good morning texts." In-article pop-ups promote the app. SEO drove nearly half of traffic. [13:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=839s), [14:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=858s), [18:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1105s)
- **Expect SEO decline.** AI answers replace many informational blog clicks, so traffic fell. [15:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=908s)
- **A web funnel routes to many products.** One site sends each blog post and Google ad to the best-fit product. Dating coaching worked but needed too much upkeep. [15:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=951s)
- **Capture demand versus create it.** Google and SEO capture existing demand but are capped by search volume. Meta and TikTok create demand with more upside. They are auctions, so you must monetize as well as rivals. [20:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1206s), [22:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1340s)
- **Give each channel a real test.** SEO needs about 3 months of focused work before any signal. Make sure a failure means the channel is not viable. Poor execution is a different failure. [24:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1450s)
- **Sell painkillers.** Dating is a painkiller with a large value gap. People offer $10K to $100K "marriage bounties." Vitamins such as a calendar that is 10% better are a hard sell. [26:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1599s), [29:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1795s)
- **Solve problems that tech created.** Bootstrappers win with tools that help people use a bigger app. A new dating app needs network effects and VC money. [31:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1891s), [32:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1956s)
- **Hire coaches, not only doers.** For each new channel, such as Google Ads, he hired a contractor who did the work and trained him. This made the next hire easier. [43:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=2594s)
- **Fixed-price dev work.** Joseph cites a founder who splits an app into 3 to 5 priced parts. He uses ChatGPT to estimate them and caps each iOS app at about $2K. [46:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=2794s)
- **Use the day job as the funding engine.** Dmitri ran the app at a loss in his final year. Contractors at $50/hour were a better use of money than his own $150/hour time. [51:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=3114s)

## Quotes

- "The PR itself didn't generate any money but allowed us a really good SEO strategy." [1:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=66s)
- "With Google ads and SEO you're capturing latent demand ... whereas with Meta and TikTok you are creating induced demand." [20:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1206s)
- "It's much easier to make money on a product when you don't have to sell a person on why they need that product." [29:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CyaWU1qxQg4&t=1795s)
