## Summary

Jay is a former big tech engineer who built NomadTable, an app where solo travelers create activities and meet people nearby. He runs it alone and bootstrapped. He grew it to over 1M downloads and about $65K/month (2026-03) almost only with organic UGC. He first learned to go viral on his own accounts. Then he hired 60 to 70 creators on $1 to $2 CPM deals and handed them his proven formats. His key signal is 3-second retention: formats that hold 75% to 80% of viewers past 3 seconds go viral in time.

## Key facts

- About 60 creators, including Jay, post daily on TikTok and Instagram. They get 30M to 44M views a month (2026-03). [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=0s), [8:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=508s)

## Tactics

- **Go viral yourself first.** Paying creators for unproven formats is useless. Your own viral videos also prove to creators that a CPM deal is easy money. Without proof, start with retainers. [38:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2328s), [39:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2390s), [52:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=3177s)
- **Founder in the trenches.** Jay manages all creators himself in a WhatsApp group and one-on-one. Posting next to them makes them take his feedback. They mostly use his formats, with some freedom. [9:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=558s), [11:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=672s)
- **Hook then demo.** His first viral video borrowed a hook from another niche: years of solo travel, and only now he finds out about this app. Then he showed the app on a phone. A creator version got 74K likes and 30K bookmarks. [12:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=757s), [14:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=845s), [22:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1340s)
- **Specific details sell.** A line about "the most unsocial hostel in Thailand" makes the video feel real. [15:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=908s)
- **Avoid the view trap.** Clickbait hostel hooks got 300K views but only 174 likes and 8 comments. They did not deliver on the hook, so they did not convert. [17:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1051s), [18:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1107s)
- **Text hook over acting.** A good text hook works for most creators, even weaker actors. Jay does all the testing. Creators only remake hooks that hit. [25:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1509s), [26:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1560s)
- **Volume spreads luck.** With 60 to 70 creators at 5K views a day, the baseline brings steady downloads even with no viral hit. [30:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1822s), [32:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=1943s)
- **No paid ads for social apps.** ARPU is too low to pay back ad costs. Paid ads can help only with a cold start. [36:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2172s)
- **Be fair to creators.** No payout cap. Monthly payouts. Views on old posts count for 3 months. [43:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2612s), [44:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2687s)
- **Cold start.** Travelers move, so city-by-city dating-app tactics do not work. A waitlist gave critical mass on day one. Two people in one city are enough for a meetup. He recommends *The Cold Start Problem* by Andrew Chen. [45:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2748s), [46:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2802s), [47:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2836s)
- **Founder-led content early.** Talking videos and public milestones built an audience that wanted him to win and stayed through a rough launch. [48:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=2930s)
