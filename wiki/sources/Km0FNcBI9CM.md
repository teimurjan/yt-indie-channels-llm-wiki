# Zero to $1.1B from Flipping Websites

## Summary

Starter Story visits Thomas Smale, founder of the M&A firm FE International, in his New York office. He started by flipping websites in college on his credit card, then wrote a book and course about it. Readers asked him to sell their businesses, so he fell into M&A. His firm has now sold over $1B of online businesses. He explains how to find, judge, value and negotiate a deal, and how to start with almost no money.

## Key facts

- First deal: bought a website for about $100 and sold it for about $500 within a month, around 2008 to 2009. [0:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=49s)
- He founded FE International around 2010 as he graduated. A book and course paid the rent. The course made about $30K. [1:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=109s), [4:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=257s)
- First hires: a small office and 2 technical staff. Business partner Ismail joined from a large investment bank. The biggest deal at that time was $30K. Growth came from 2012 on. [4:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=257s), [5:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=317s)
- Over $1B in businesses sold, with eight-figure deals now routine (2023-04). The first $10M+ sale came from a referral by a past client. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=0s), [9:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=589s), [10:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=641s)
- Broker fees start around 15% on small deals and fall as the deal size grows (2023-04). [11:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=685s)
- Small online businesses usually sell at 4x to 10x annual profit, not a revenue multiple (2023-04). [11:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=717s)

## Tactics

- **Apply the Pareto principle.** A partner showed he spent 80% of his time on the parts that made 20% of the money. They cut the rest. [5:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=341s)
- **Hire early to scale.** He was not technical, so he hired for that and kept the selling himself. He did not want to stay a freelancer. [4:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=257s)
- **Find deals by direct outreach.** Set a budget and a business model first. Then email owners where they hang out. Say plainly that you want to acquire; A vague "can we call?" gets a no. [6:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=396s), [7:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=457s)
- **Skip the broker on small deals.** A $10K purchase does not need a firm or professional advice. [7:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=457s)
- **Three signs of a good business.** It is evergreen, it has repeat or recurring customers (subscriptions, newsletters, email), and it is growing. Buyers pay more for growth. [8:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=482s), [9:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=544s)
- **Buy where your skill adds value.** For example, a marketer buys a small software tool from a technical founder who cannot market it. Demand and product are already proven. [12:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=776s), [13:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=837s)
- **Negotiate on priorities.** Decide what matters most: cash, transition time, or who keeps the team. Do not fight all 10 points. Pick battles and give the other side wins. Offers often trade cash against a 2-year versus 3-month transition. [14:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=866s), [15:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=926s)
- **Start with $100, not $0.** Get a little capital, flip anything, and aim only for a profit. Small wins build a track record that investors will back. [16:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=994s), [17:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Km0FNcBI9CM&t=1055s)
