# How I Used Reddit To Build a $25K/Month Business

## Summary

Avneesh left big tech to build Savewise, a tool that helps people stack credit card points, airline miles and cashback on online purchases. Launch posts on Product Hunt, Hacker News and Indie Hackers brought traffic that bounced. Cold outreach to influencers failed. Growth came from Reddit and Facebook groups where his users already gathered. He shares a five-step playbook and two posts that grew the app to about $25K a month in 15 months, without ad spend.

## Key facts

- Revenue: about $25,000 a month after 15 months, with $0 spent on marketing (2025-08). [0:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=4s), [1:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=64s)
- Users: tens of thousands, with about 1,500 paying customers (2025-08). [11:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=678s)
- About 97% of revenue comes from a lifetime plan, not monthly or yearly subscriptions (2025-08). [11:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=678s)
- He worked at Microsoft, Instagram, Foursquare and Dropbox before he started. [1:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=94s)
- Launch-site traffic had a 95% to 96% bounce rate. He sent 300 to 400 emails to influencers and got one reply. [2:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=146s)
- One spreadsheet post in a Facebook group sent over 1,500 visitors to the site. [8:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=531s)
- Stack: Next.js on Vercel, Azure for data, Retool, Clerk for auth, v0 for design, PostHog, ChatGPT, Copilot, Cursor and Google Analytics. [12:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=725s)

## Tactics

- **Go where users use products, not where builders gather.** Skip SaaS and Indie Hackers subreddits. Target travel and credit card point communities. [3:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=222s)
- **Step 1: Brainstorm 5 to 15 keywords, interests and demographics.** Use Map of Reddit to find related subreddits from one seed, such as r/creditcards. [4:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=257s)
- **Step 2: Lurk before you post.** Study what people talk about and how. [5:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=306s)
- **Step 3: Set one goal per community.** Feedback, problem discovery or first trials. Start in comments, which is low risk, then move to a top-level post. [5:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=306s), [6:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=367s)
- **Step 4: Set keyword alerts with F5Bot.** Get an email each time Reddit mentions your keywords, then read the thread for feedback chances. [6:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=367s)
- **Step 5: Be helpful first.** Tips on earning points earned him the right to mention the product. [7:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=430s)
- **Give away a data asset.** In a "Rakuten Stacks" Facebook group, he posted a SQL-built sheet of stores with Rakuten and Amex offers. The sheet linked to his site as the data source. [8:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=531s)
- **Build in the weekly Q&A thread, then ask the mod.** He posted for feedback weekly for about 3.5 months. Then he asked the moderator for a top-level post, and it blew up. [9:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=599s)
- **Offer lifetime pricing.** Users asked for it on launch day and paid two or more years up front. [11:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3X4VneEHvig&t=678s)
