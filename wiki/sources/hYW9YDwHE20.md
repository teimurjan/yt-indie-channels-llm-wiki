# My App Failed, Then I Changed One Thing and Made $80K

## Summary

Pat Walls interviews Praneeth, a builder from India who quit a $250K/year software job to build apps. His app Rabbit Holes AI (now called Canvas Mode) is a canvas for AI chats. Users branch a conversation into new nodes instead of one long linear thread. The first version was a web app with a freemium trial and got almost no traction. He rebuilt it as a desktop app with bring-your-own-key AI and a one-time price, and it made about $80K in 6 months.

## Key facts

- Praneeth left a $250,000/year job (2025-10). [0:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=47s)
- After the pricing change, the app made about $80K in 6 months (2025-10). [0:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=47s), [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=225s)
- About $80K gross and about $75K profit after refunds (2025-10). [8:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=528s)
- About 1,200 paying users. 60% hold a lifetime license. 40% bought a one-year license that needs renewal for updates (2025-10). [8:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=528s)
- About 38,000 unique site visitors in total (2025-10). [8:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=528s)
- Price path: $29 under 100 users, then $49. He plans a higher lifetime price and a move to recurring pricing at about 1,200 users (2025-10). [6:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=416s)
- Costs: about $50/month for Vercel hosting and about $200/month for Cursor (2025-10). [10:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=617s)
- Stack: Cursor, a Next.js landing page, an Electron app with React and TypeScript, Cloudflare hosting and the Vercel AI SDK. [9:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=565s)

## Tactics

- **Build from your own friction.** He used ChatGPT for architecture, Claude for code and Perplexity for live facts, each in a separate tab. One canvas fixed that. [1:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=113s)
- **Know why freemium failed.** He had no big personal brand. His ideal customer, a power AI user, already paid for many AI subscriptions. [2:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=162s)
- **Remove variable costs, then sell once.** Bring-your-own-key moves AI costs to users. A desktop app moves server costs to users' computers. With no running costs, a one-time price works. [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=225s)
- **Paying users give better feedback.** After the change, buyers were more serious and more helpful. [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=225s)
- **Add a lifetime deal to any bootstrapped product.** Praneeth argues for it even next to a subscription, even as a limited offer. It brings cash, morale, proof of demand and engaged users. VC money lets a company delay product-market fit, but a bootstrapper cannot. [4:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=288s), [5:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=351s)
- **Start low and raise the price.** Early buyers get a better deal, and each sale tells you the market wants it. [6:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=416s)
- **Call out the customer in the H1.** The landing page names power AI users in the headline and in other places. [7:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=468s)
- **Hint, do not pitch, on Twitter.** He replied to threads about AI interaction and pointed toward his product without a direct sales pitch. [7:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=468s)
- **Use one SDK for many models.** The Vercel AI SDK let him connect many AI providers and focus on the front end. [9:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hYW9YDwHE20&t=565s)
