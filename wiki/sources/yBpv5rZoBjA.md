## Summary

Tim Gabe runs the design agency ZipSnap. For over a decade he designed products for clients from Spotify to startups. He breaks down three mechanisms behind apps that users cannot stop using. The craving machine uses unpredictable rewards. The infinite game uses loss aversion and gives no finish line. The invisible scoreboard uses social comparison. The third locks in the other two, because quitting becomes a public act. He warns that the mechanisms are morally complicated and can build habits or trap users.

## Key facts

- The average app keeps 7% of users after 30 days. The apps in this video reach up to 90% annual retention (2026-04). [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=60s)
- Finch: over 14M downloads, 675,000 ratings at 4.9 stars on iOS, Apple Editor's Choice (2026-04). Free users pick from 3 destinations, paid users from 9. Each has 15 to 20 discoveries. [2:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=166s)
- League of Legends: over 130M monthly players. Hidden MMR keeps players near a 50% win rate (2026-04). [4:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=275s)
- Peloton: 90% annual subscriber retention. The bike costs $1,700 (2026-04). [8:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=501s)
- Strava: over 180M registered athletes. It deleted 3.9M activities in 2025 and early 2026 because users gamed segment leaderboards, for example with e-bike rides. [9:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=593s)
- People feel a loss about twice as strongly as an equal gain. [6:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=396s)

## Tactics

- **Craving machine.** B.F. Skinner showed that variable-ratio rewards cause the most compulsive behavior. This is craving, not pleasure, much like slot machines. [1:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=114s)
  - Finch's bird brings back random discoveries and grows its own personality across six traits, so users keep checking. [3:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=225s)
  - Founder takeaways: keep most rewards predictable and transparent. Add controlled surprises, such as a bonus or an early milestone. Track one progress measure users obsess over, not 20 scattered badges. [5:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=335s)
- **Infinite game.** Use loss aversion and never let users finish. [6:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=396s)
  - ZipSnap designed a diamond streak system for Freecash. A 7-day streak unlocks the first diamond and 42 days unlocks another. A missed day risks the diamonds. Users must earn freezes. [7:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=451s)
  - League of Legends resets ranks each season but keeps honor levels and cosmetics. Peloton's totals for classes, miles and output never cap. [7:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=451s), [9:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=561s)
  - Founder takeaways: audit the product for "done" states. Make streaks compound into value users keep. Use periodic resets that keep earned status. [8:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=501s)
- **Invisible scoreboard.** Social comparison turns progress into status. [9:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=593s)
  - Peloton adds parasocial bonds with star instructors such as Cody Rigsby and Ally Love. AI can copy features but not human connection. [10:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=655s)
  - Founder takeaways: make achievements visible to others and build community dynamics. Design metrics as a mirror that shows how users compare to others. [12:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=764s)
- **Stack all three.** Craving brings users in. The infinite game keeps them. The scoreboard makes quitting a public admission, so engagement becomes identity. [11:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yBpv5rZoBjA&t=717s)
