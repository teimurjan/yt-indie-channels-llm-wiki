# How He Makes $250K per Month From a Simple App (Letterly)

## Summary

Anton built Letterly, a paid app that turns speech into well-written text. Before it, he spent 15 years on six or seven startups that failed. Letterly competes with free tools such as ChatGPT and wins on simplicity: few taps, no friction. He treats user experience as a separate feature and iterates on it at the prototype, build and post-release stages. The team of 10 spends about $200K/month on ads.

## Key facts

- Letterly makes $250K/month, 2 years after launch (2025-11). [1:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=90s)
- It has 30K monthly active users, 20K paid subscribers and 150,000 downloads in 2 years (2025-11). It is a paid app with a free trial. [1:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=113s)
- The idea came from early prototypes that combined ChatGPT with OpenAI transcription. None of them felt finished enough to use. [2:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=146s)
- Use cases: notes, messages, emails, social posts and meeting notes. The target user finds speaking easier than typing. [8:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=533s)
- Stack: the team started with React Native, then moved to Swift on iOS. The backend is Python. [10:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=655s)
- Monthly costs: about $30K in salaries for a team of 10, about $5K in AI costs and about $200K on advertising (2025-11). [11:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=676s)

## Tactics

- **Make simplicity the moat.** Anyone can build the product. Few make it easy to use. Every screen and interaction aims to remove friction. [3:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=224s)
- **UX multiplies every other metric.** Good UX helps onboarding, retention, conversion and referrals. You still need a good problem to solve. [5:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=320s)
- **Ask users why they pick you over the free option.** Early users said Letterly "feels much easier": one widget tap starts a recording, and one tap stops it and gives the text. [5:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=320s)
- **Simplify an existing feature before you add a new one.** The team asks this question for each request. [6:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=381s)
- **Improve a validated idea.** His past startups tried to be unique and built things people did not need. This time he took a tested model and made it simpler. [6:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=381s)
- **Treat UX as a separate, expensive feature.** Iterate on prototypes, review the build before release, and fix what users miss after release. The team sometimes delays a promised feature by a few weeks to make it simpler. [7:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=440s), [8:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=501s)
- **Put the most used action one tap away.** Users can pin favorite rewrite styles under the note. A one-click copy button became one of the most used features. Actions push to Google Docs or Notion. [8:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=533s), [9:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=594s)
- **Founder advice.** Work with co-founders from day one. Build something you can launch in 1 to 2 months. Pick an idea that another company already validated. Charge from day one. Once revenue shows traction, quit your job and focus on it. [12:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wvYej-2KWDg&t=728s)
