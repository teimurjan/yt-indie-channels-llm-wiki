# The Only Formula Behind Truly Great Apps

## Summary

Tim Gabe builds the same wake-up alarm app with AI at four levels of design input. He scores each level from 1 to 5 on point of view, consistency and execution. A bare prompt gives clean but generic "AI slop". Skill files and a basic design system help a little. Too much reference material makes a "Frankenstein" that scores lower than the bare prompt. Only a human with a clear vision gets full marks, by deciding what to cut and how the product works in real life.

## Key facts

- Level 1, bare prompt: point of view 1, consistency 3, execution 1. Total 5/15. [1:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=111s)
- Level 2, skill files plus a basic design system: 2, 4, 2. Total 8/15. [4:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=259s)
- Level 3, every reference and a full design system at once: 1, 1, 2. Total 4/15, below level 1. [5:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=321s), [6:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=373s)
- Level 4, human art direction: 15/15. [13:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=784s)
- A Contra study found that the models with the best taste won on vague briefs. With a specific creative direction, a model that followed the direction well beat them (2026-09). [2:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=162s)
- Porto Rocha built a strong brand with Nano Banana in early 2026. [10:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=616s)

## Tactics

- **Score apps on three axes.** Point of view: does the app stand for something? Consistency: does it hold together as one system? Execution: is there product craft? [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=0s), [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=62s)
- **Direction is the moat, not the model.** Better models raise the floor for everyone. Specific creative direction raises the ceiling. [3:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=207s)
- **Do not overload the AI with context.** Too many directions break spacing, type sizes and component consistency. [5:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=321s), [6:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=373s)
- **One idea for every screen.** The level 4 app follows "waking up gently": a sunrise gradient on each screen, human copy ("GM, Alex, don't fight it"), a clear number hierarchy and a one-emoji mood check. [9:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=555s), [10:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=616s)
- **Design for the real moment.** To stop the alarm, you shake the phone for 15 seconds while a bar fills. There is no snooze. The body has to move, so the function matches the goal. [12:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=727s)
- **Three ways to get taste.** Learn it yourself from free skill stacks by people such as Emil Kowalski (slow). Hire a senior founding designer (expensive, often with equity). Borrow it from a freelancer or agency. Most founders mix all three. [7:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=435s), [8:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=496s)
