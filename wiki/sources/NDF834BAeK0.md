# Glassmorphism Effect in Figma (3 Min Tutorial)

## Summary

Tim Gabe shows how to add a glassmorphism effect to a card component in Figma in about 3 minutes. The same steps work on icons, buttons or any other object. The effect has three parts: a colorful background, a "glassified" object on top, and an optional noise texture. The glass look comes from four settings: linear gradients, opacity, background blur and a drop shadow.

## Key facts

- Glassmorphism needs two or three things. First, a colorful or patterned background. Second, the object you want to look like glass. Third, as an option, a texture or small details. [0:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=12s)
- The four settings to remember: background blur, drop shadow, linear gradients and opacity. [0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=45s)
- Example values: about 50% opacity on the gradients and a background blur of about 12. Tim says to adjust both by eye. [1:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=105s)
- A noise texture at about 20% opacity adds the final detail. [2:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=166s)

## Tactics

- **Start with a colorful background.** Strong color behind the object makes the glass effect stand out. Tim uses a card with a bright image behind it. [0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=45s)
- **Put a linear gradient on the fill.** Add it to the card background so that the faded end sits at the bottom. [0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=45s), [1:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=105s)
- **Flip the gradient on the stroke.** Add a linear gradient to the stroke too, but with the faded end at the top. This gives the edge a light, glassy rim. [0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=45s), [1:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=105s)
- **Lower the opacity of both gradients.** Try about 50%, then adjust. [1:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=105s)
- **Add a background blur and a drop shadow.** Raise the blur until the background looks frosted, for example to 12. Then add a drop shadow and increase its blur. [1:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=105s)
- **Borrow a texture from the Figma Community.** Search "noise" or "texture" and pick one you like. Copy its fill with Command+C (Ctrl+C on Windows), paste it on top of the card, and set the opacity to about 20%. [1:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=105s), [2:46](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=166s)
- **Build the card with auto layout first.** The description links his auto layout tutorial for the card itself. See [Kf8L3CeiWzQ](Kf8L3CeiWzQ.md).

## Quotes

- "The effect is amplified by having colorful objects in the background." [0:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=45s)
- "Play around with" the opacity values, says Tim. [1:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=NDF834BAeK0&t=105s)
