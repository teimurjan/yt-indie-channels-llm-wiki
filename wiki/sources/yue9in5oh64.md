# How I Built It: $20K/Month AI App as a Non-Technical Founder

## Summary

Pat Walls interviews Colin McIntosh, a former recruiter and founder of Sheets & Giggles. His side project, Sheets Resume, is an AI resume builder trained on his own resume rules. He earned trust over 6 years with a Reddit resume guide that became the top Google result for "resume advice Reddit". He first sold manual resume reviews, then had his former recruiting partner Nate build an AI version. It launched free, turned on pricing a week later, and hit about $20K in its second month.

## Key facts

- Pricing: $99 lifetime access or $29 for one week. No monthly plan (2025-01). [1:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=68s), [9:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=551s)
- About $20K in sales in its second month (2025-01). Pat frames it as about $250K/year. Colin estimates about 90% net margin. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=0s), [6:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=374s), [12:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=767s)
- Launched August 26 (2024). First $1,000 day within one week. [5:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=343s)
- Nate, a software engineer at Eventbrite, took about 4 months to build it. A third partner came from Reddit. Equity is split three ways. [5:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=343s), [8:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=482s)
- Colin spends at most 5 to 10 hours a week on it. [8:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=482s)
- His Reddit guide from February 2018 drew about 20,000 questions over 6 years. [3:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=222s), [4:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=283s)
- Manual reviews started at a couple hundred dollars and went to $300, then $400. [4:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=283s)
- Stack: OpenAI and Gemini, a PHP Laravel REST API, AWS, Gmail, Stripe and Mercury. [11:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=707s)

## Tactics

- **Sell your expertise first.** Ask "what do I do better than anyone else?" Give help for free, prove the problem, then monetize. [6:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=374s)
- **Validate with an unscalable service.** Do the work by hand for people, then automate it. Raise the price until demand stops being the problem. [7:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=436s), [4:43](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=283s)
- **Plan before building.** Write a brand identity map (Techstars has a free template) and a two-page business plan with 5 to 7 points of difference. He says not to use AI for this. [7:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=436s)
- **Encode your rules into the AI.** A review that took him about 3 hours now runs hundreds of times a day. [2:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=124s)
- **Find a technical partner through relationships.** He says you cannot convince a random engineer to build your idea without paying a lot. His partners came from years of work and Reddit. [8:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=482s)
- **Launch free inside an existing audience.** He edited the tool into his popular Reddit post. Thousands of free resumes drove word of mouth before pricing. [9:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=551s)
- **Avoid subscriptions for one-off needs.** He calls monthly plans for job seekers predatory. [9:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=551s)
- **Growth that scales without him.** An affiliate marketing agency, basic Google Ads to train the algorithm, and Reddit posts that rank on Google. Keep the product link low in the post so readers trust the content. [10:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=624s), [11:27](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=687s)
- **Use cheap AI tools now.** He suggests website chatbots, AI image and video, and cutting long videos into short clips, while costs stay low. [11:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yue9in5oh64&t=707s)
