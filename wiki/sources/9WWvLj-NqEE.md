# My 2 apps made $1.5M

## Summary

Kelechi is a 22-year-old founder from Nigeria who moved to the US with $100. In 12 months he built two apps with $1.5M in total revenue. Social Wizard is an AI texting and social-skills app for young men. It grew to about $60K/month with paid collabs with micro streamers who showed the product in use. His second app, Clean Eats, scans food barcodes. It hit $10K in 2 weeks and sold to a UK company 4 months later. His playbook is distribution first: test formats on TikTok in volume, then scale proven formats through many creators.

## Key facts

- Total revenue for both apps: $1.5M in 12 months. Downloads: over 700K across App Store and Play Store (2025-10). [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=0s), [0:21](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=21s)
- Social Wizard launched in early 2024. It peaked at about $60K/month and has over 600K downloads (2025-10). [1:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=95s)
- Social Wizard growth: first sale of about $10 in late January. After 4 weeks of tests he found a viral format. MRR was $3.5K 2 weeks later and over $25K after 1 month. Cash collected was about $250K by month 6 and about $500K by year end (2024). [3:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=184s)
- He says he spent $0 on marketing in the last year and the app still makes money (2025-10). [3:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=184s)
- One streamer collab cost about $120. It got 2M views and brought in tens of thousands of dollars. [5:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=358s)
- Clean Eats hit about $10K revenue in 2 weeks. He sold it to a UK company about 4 months later. [1:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=95s)
- Social Wizard pricing: about $10/week, $20/month or $80/year. Users are mostly young men aged 16 to 24. Clean Eats users are mostly women aged 18 to 28. [11:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=674s)
- Margins are over 90% after Apple's cut. Infrastructure costs $1K to $2K/month. Marketing is about 90% of spend (2025-10). [12:45](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=765s)
- Stack: React Native, a NestJS back end on Render or Heroku, Firebase, Mixpanel. [12:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=737s)
- Origin: his first app, Casp, a group photo app, got a few thousand users and then failed. A dating problem led him to an OpenAI script that wrote texts. Friends at homecoming loved it. [2:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=124s), [2:48](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=168s)

## Tactics

- **Show, do not tell.** The viral format: a streamer screenshots a girl's Instagram story, puts it in Social Wizard and reads the AI reply. The viewer sees the product in use. Nobody pitches the app. [4:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=243s), [5:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=312s)
- **Hook with a pause.** The video opens on an image that makes viewers stop scrolling. [5:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=312s)
- **Find the format yourself first.** Kelechi made content himself until a format worked. Then he gave that exact format to small streamers with 100K to 200K views and the right audience. [4:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=243s)
- **Check the niche.** Is a lot of content made in the niche already? If yes, go to mid-size and large creators. If no, make content yourself. Do not outsource this step. [7:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=472s)
- **Creator outreach is a volume game.** Expect about 100 creators to get one that works. Use the first creator's video to sign the next ones. [7:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=472s)
- **Test on TikTok, not Instagram.** New TikTok accounts go viral more easily. Start with 3 videos a day across 2 accounts. After 1 to 2 weeks, go to 10, then 20 a day. [8:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=533s)
- **Use adjacent creators.** His streamers made content about Fortnite and reaction clips, but they reached his audience. A fitness app can use cooking creators. [9:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=596s)
- **Sell the outcome.** Both apps sell confidence. "This food hurts your skin" sells better than "healthy alternatives". [11:14](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=674s)
- **Idea to try:** education apps, such as boxing, swimming or persuasion courses, packaged well. [10:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=644s)
