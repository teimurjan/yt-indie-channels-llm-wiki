## Summary

Tomer was a marketer for other businesses. He heard Zach Yadegari's Cal AI podcast and copied the idea for Israel. He built Cal Buddy, an AI calorie tracker in Hebrew, with Cursor and no developer background. It grew from $1K in its first month (June 2025) to over $81K/month 12 months later (2026-08). Paid ads with locally adapted viral creatives took it to $20K/month. Revenue-share deals with two big Israeli fitness influencers took it to $80K/month.

## Key facts

- Revenue: about $1K in the first month, June 2025. Over $81K in the month before the interview, with steady growth (2026-08). [1:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=104s)
- Built with Cursor. A working version took 2 weeks. Live on the App Store took about 2 months, mostly because of Apple developer approval and App Store review. [3:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=188s)
- Idea found in March 2025. He picked fitness and nutrition because a small market like Israel needs a huge niche. [4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=249s)
- Paid ads alone got the app to about $20K/month in 4 months (2025). [6:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=411s)
- Influencer rev share took it from $20K to $80K/month (2026-08). [7:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=464s)
- Stack and monthly cost (2026-08): Claude $200, PostHog $20, RevenueCat, Supabase $35, Expo for in-app updates $100. [10:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=603s)

## Tactics

- **Localize a proven winner.** Cal AI was not known in Israel. Being the first local version was the simplest zero-to-one move. [2:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=143s), [4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=249s)
- **Small product changes, big brand changes.** The core is the same: photo in, calories and macros out. The changes are language and how Israelis eat and talk about food. [5:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=342s)
- **Win on acquisition cost.** Users at half the acquisition cost of competitors give more cash flow, and more cash flow lets you outspend them. [5:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=342s)
- **Pick the most efficient channel for the market.** Every channel works. In Israel, CPMs are much lower than in the US, so paid ads were profitable without much testing or capital. [6:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=411s)
- **Remix viral content.** He studied the most viral content of similar US apps and of Israeli fitness creators, then made ad creatives from it. [6:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=411s)
- **Influencers as partners, not paid posts.** Big influencers get a share of all Israeli revenue, so incentives align. [7:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=464s), [8:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=499s)
  - The contract sets deliverables: pieces of content and filming days each month.
  - Get social proof first. Waiting until $20K/month gave him the power to avoid a 50/50 split. [9:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=556s)
  - Partners with skin in the game also mention the app on TV and in interviews. [9:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=574s)
- **Look abroad.** Pat notes that markets in many countries are still open, and other founders test ads in cheaper countries such as Argentina first. [12:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=756s)
