# The Psychology of Premium Apps

## Summary

Tim Gabe argues that AI tools give every product the same "decent" look. This creates a "commodity ceiling" on price. Users do not judge premium from looks. They judge it from evidence that the product is good. He names three places where that evidence lives. The first is the unglamorous states that AI skips. The second is consistency across all screens. The third is good recovery from failures. He uses research studies and his agency's work with Undertone and a crypto card client.

## Key facts

- Tim designed interfaces for about a decade before this video, for Spotify and many other companies. [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=0s)
- Packaging studies from 2010 and 2021: the look gets a product picked, but evidence of quality gets it called premium. [1:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=62s)
- A mid-2026 study gave the same design tasks to 5 AI building tools, including ChatGPT and Claude. About a quarter of the design reasoning they stated never reached the interface. The researchers call this "design theater". [1:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=97s)
- A 2009 University of York study moved a website's navigation bar from left to right during a task. Time on that page more than doubled, and later pages were slower too. Most users said it did not slow them much. [4:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=250s), [5:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=311s)
- Chrome's offline T-Rex game, 4 years after its 2014 launch, was played about 270M times a month. [6:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=407s)
- A 2007 review on the "service recovery paradox": a failure that is handled well can leave people as happy as no failure, or happier. [7:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=452s)
- A crypto card client had a redesign that included a clear declined-payment state. After it, new accounts grew 60% and money loaded onto cards grew 79% (2026-10). [8:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=512s)
- A 2018 survey: about a third of people leave a product after one bad experience. [8:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=512s)

## Tactics

- **Fix the states AI leaves out.** Show what the system is doing, let users undo, and help them recover from errors. Content that is easy to take in gets judged more kindly. [1:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=97s), [2:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=160s)
- **Undertone mic test.** A short mic test used to end with "too short to count" and nothing else. Users wanted "anything instead of nothing". [2:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=160s)
- **Walk the core flow.** At each step, users must know what just happened, how to take it back, and what to do next. [3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=187s)
- **AI gains become the new floor.** Every competitor gets the same tool on the same day. Care about every state is what still sets you apart. [3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=187s)
- **Review the app as one product, not screen by screen.** AI agents make new screens in a minute, so 80 screens can drift apart. Use the product for an hour like a real user and note each place where it stops feeling like one product. [4:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=250s), [6:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=369s)
- **One pattern for one state.** Undertone had two different "used all your sessions" screens with no count or date. The redesign has one pattern: a "2/2 sessions used" badge, a clear line of text and the exact refresh date. [5:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=311s)
- **Design the worst moments.** For a declined payment, show an alert to check the balance next to a contact-support button. [8:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=512s)
- **Break your product on purpose.** Turn off Wi-Fi mid-flow, fail a payment and empty every list. Design those states as carefully as success screens. Many teams skip this, so it is a cheap way to feel premium. [8:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=512s), [9:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=564s)

## Quotes

- "Whatever a tool learns, every competitor gets the same day." [3:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=187s)
