# My AI App Makes $100K/Month

## Summary

Pat Walls interviews Nico, a former physics PhD and musician who founded Neural Frames. Neural Frames is an AI music video generator for hobby, indie and AI-music creators. Nico started it at the end of 2022 as his first full-stack project. He built it alone for a year, and the team now has five people and makes about $100K/month. Growth came from a Hacker News launch, a niche focus on musicians, SEO, and a personal indie brand. GPU and API costs take almost half of revenue.

## Key facts

- About $100K revenue last month, team of 5 (2025-07). [0:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=52s)
- About 1,500 customers, about 100,000 monthly active visitors, and 1.5 million videos made in total (2025-07). [8:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=527s)
- Background: physics master's in 2016, PhD finished in 2020, then a deep tech startup in computer vision. He started Neural Frames in 2022. [1:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=119s)
- Costs per month (2025-07): GPUs and text-to-video APIs cost about $45K, almost half of revenue. Servers and storage cost about $5K. Cursor costs about $1K. Smaller tools: Email Octopus $300, Vercel $200, PostHog $120, Ahrefs $110, Intercom $100. Nico says little profit remains. [9:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=599s)
- Stack: Cursor for code, a Python back end and a Next.js front end. RunPod runs the GPUs, and fal gives text-to-video APIs. Other tools: PostHog, Slack, Linear, Notion, Ahrefs, Intercom and Email Octopus. A custom Telegram bot sends server-down alerts. [9:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=552s)

## Tactics

- **Launch on Hacker News early.** He posted about a week after going live, with a rough product. It reached the top 6 on a Sunday. He saw 350 people on the site in 30 minutes. It brought backlinks, contacts and his first revenue. [7:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=448s)
- **Niche down.** The first tagline was "text to video for everyone". A friend advised one use case. A musician who reads "the platform to create music videos" does not need to guess, and is more likely to tell friends. [6:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=395s)
- **Use SEO from the start.** Dedicated landing pages and free tools still rank well on Google. [7:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=448s)
- **Play the indie card.** The footer said "no VC money, just a tiny company". His photo was everywhere, and he recorded the YouTube tutorials himself. People buy from people. [7:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=448s), [8:30](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=510s)
- **Check ideas with keyword data.** Find a keyword in Ahrefs with more than about 1,000 monthly searches and low difficulty. That shows demand without many solutions. [5:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=304s)
- **"Mess around and find out".** Do many different things. Ideas appear where past skills meet, here computer vision and music. [5:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=304s)
- **Bootstrapped freedom.** A bootstrapped company can target smaller markets than a VC-backed company that needs a billion-dollar outcome. [10:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=629s)
- **Ideas he likes (2025-07):** customizable media, an AI podcast generator, voice interfaces, and an AI executive coach with calendar and Slack access. [5:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=miEUhwSR2-0&t=359s)
