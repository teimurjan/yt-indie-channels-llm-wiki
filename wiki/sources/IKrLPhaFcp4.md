## Summary

Tim Gabe argues that rewards and reminders do not build lasting habits. He proposes a "return loop" with two moves. Move one, the routine cue, ties app use to something the user already does every day. Fabulous is the example. Move two, forgiveness design, decides what happens the day after a user misses. Duolingo, Alma, 10% Happier and Noom are the examples. He backs both moves with research on habit formation.

## Key facts

- A 2015 University of Bristol study tested rewards and reminders. Neither built habits that lasted after the study ended. Reminders worked only while they kept firing, and relying on them got in the way of habit forming. [0:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=53s), [1:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=85s)
- Habits formed in 18 to 254 days, according to a study he attributes to Yale. Less than half had formed by day 84. [2:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=146s)
- Fabulous was built with Duke University's Center for Advanced Hindsight. It bundles small habits into one routine tied to one alarm. It has a loyal but niche audience. [3:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=203s), [4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=249s)
- Duolingo's streak freeze is based on a University of Pennsylvania and UCLA study on "slack" in goals. Doubling the freezes a user can hold from 1 to 2 lifted daily active learners by 0.38%. [4:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=264s), [5:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=326s)
- Alma, a nutrition app, has a "vacation mode" that pauses a streak for up to 14 days. The recap calls it "Calm's vacation mode", which looks like a transcription error. [6:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=382s), [7:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=458s)

## Tactics

- **Anchor to an existing routine.** Tie the app to an automatic daily cue: brushing teeth, sitting at the desk, pouring coffee. Do not push the app in the user's face. [1:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=85s)
- **Do not back off after month one.** The new behavior still needs weeks or months to become automatic. Switching to nagging after month one kills the habit just before it sticks. [2:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=146s)
- **Start small, then stack.** Fabulous onboarding tells users to start with one small habit and build on something they already do. [3:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=203s)
- **Mechanism plus design.** Fabulous proves the psychology but its interface is weak, so it stayed niche. You need both to go mainstream. [4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=249s)
- **Design for the miss.** Plan what the app does the day after a user skips. Make the streak survive their worst day. [4:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=264s), [5:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=326s)
- **Forgiveness variants.** Alma pauses the streak (no growth, no reset). 10% Happier's "imperfect meditation challenge" moves the deadline for a missed session. Noom's "pause and preserve" lets users skip a day on purpose. [6:22](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=382s), [7:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=443s)

## Quotes

- "The cue is the engine, but it's not the whole Mercedes." [4:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=249s)
- "What an app does the day after you miss, not the day you show up." [4:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=264s)
- "If even a habit tracker builds you a way to cheat your own streak, that's the strongest sign of how much this mechanic actually works." [7:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=443s)
