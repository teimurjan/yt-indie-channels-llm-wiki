# The Underdog: He Turned His Last $4,000 Into $48M

## Summary

Aaron Spivak and his co-founder Leor started Hush Blankets in Toronto while they both had other jobs. They noticed rising search demand for weighted blankets. Google Ads on a pre-order site worked at first. Then sales fell to zero in their first summer, and they nearly shut down with $4,000 left. They called their customers, learned that the blankets were too hot, and built a cooling blanket. A Kickstarter for it raised over $1M. From then on, customer calls drove every new product: pillows, sheets and a mattress. A Canadian sleep company bought Hush in 2021 after the business reached $48M in 48 months.

## Key facts

- Demand signal: about 300,000 people searched for "weighted blankets" in the coming months [1:08](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=68s).
- They launched in January. Early revenue grew from $30K to $60K to $90K/month (2018). In July sales fell to zero or one per day [2:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=129s).
- The first roll of the custom "ice fabric" cost $100,000. They had $4,000 left [5:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=357s), [6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=366s).
- The Kickstarter goal was $25K and it raised over $1M in 30 days (2019). It was a top-10 Canadian Kickstarter of all time [6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=366s).
- On Dragon's Den, the dragons debated and raised the company's valuation on live TV. The founders say this was a first in 14 seasons [7:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=426s).
- They sold 3,000 pillows in 72 hours to their email list. Sales were $20M in 2020, the 2021 goal was $40M, and the mattress launch did $1.5M [7:47](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=467s), [8:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=530s).
- Exit: Canada's largest sleep company acquired them in October 2021. Total sales went from $0 to $48M in 48 months [9:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=597s).
- On pop-up store opening day, 1,500 visitors came, against 700 at a competitor that opened the same day [9:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=597s).

## Tactics

- **Validate with search volume, then pre-sell.** They put up a pre-order site with basic images of one sample. Then they bought the most expensive keyword, "weighted blankets", on Google Ads [2:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=129s).
- **Forecast for seasonality.** They did not plan inventory for summer, and it almost killed the business [3:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=191s).
- **Call every customer.** In August 2018 they emailed all customers a Calendly link. Every customer said the blanket was too hot, which led to the cooling blanket [4:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=297s).
- **Crowdfund with pre-validated demand.** The Kickstarter "hack" was 3,000 people who had said on the phone that they intended to buy it [6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=366s).
- **TV for reach.** They used Dragon's Den to put the brand in front of a mass audience [7:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=426s).
- **Product line from customer calls.** Pillows, the Ice 2.0 blanket, sheets and the mattress each came from customer interviews. For the mattress, the main pain point was comfort during sex, which led to zoned springs [8:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=530s).
- **Founder storytelling builds super fans.** They shared the ups and downs in public, and customers asked the founders to sign their blankets [10:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IuoscQiQQLg&t=657s).
