Tinder is a swipe-based dating app. In these sources it is a case study in psychology-driven app design, not a featured founder story.

## What it is

- A dating app built around one gesture, the swipe. Matches show only when both people swipe right ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [3:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=183s), [6:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=401s)).
- [Tim Gabe](../people/tim-gabe.md) says it beat Match.com, eHarmony and Plenty of Fish, which had bigger budgets and a longer track record, "with psychology, not technology" ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=60s), [2:02](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=122s)).

## Business model

- Free core loop, then upgrades offered in context: Boost when the user already gets matches, Gold to see who likes you, and Super Likes for the best profiles ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [10:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=610s), [10:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=654s)).
- Dating is one of the highest-monetizing niches on the App Store. A small group of "whales" makes most of the revenue at Tinder, Hinge and Bumble ([source](../sources/ukGtpnfBpvU.md), [2:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=178s), [20:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ukGtpnfBpvU&t=1211s)). This is why [Sway AI](sway-ai.md) chose dating.

## Metrics

All figures are as Tim Gabe states them, with no source given.

- 75M monthly active users and over $2B annual revenue (2025-11) ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [1:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=60s)).
- 1.6B swipes and 26M matches per day (2025-11) ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [3:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=234s), [7:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=458s)).
- 9.6M paying subscribers (2025-11) ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [10:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=654s)).
- Average session of 11 minutes, against 3 minutes for competitors (2025-11) ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [6:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=366s)).
- 11 app opens a day on average (2026-04) ([source](../sources/uoLpH_20qKw.md), [8:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=535s)).

> Conflict: [4yKtlOWClhA](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md) gives "11" as minutes per session. [UoLpH_20qKw](../sources/uoLpH_20qKw.md) gives "11" as app opens per day. Both come from Tim Gabe without a source.

## Design patterns

- **Variable rewards.** Each swipe builds anticipation, and matches arrive on a variable schedule, "like a slot machine" ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [3:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=234s)).
- **Fast onboarding.** Facebook login pulled photos automatically, so users swiped in under a minute. Most dating apps lost over 70% of users after the first session ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [5:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=306s)).
- **Rejection-proof matching.** Nobody sees who rejected them, so users take more risks and stay active ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [7:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=458s)).
- **Celebration screens.** The full-screen match screen with confetti and hearts got screenshotted and shared, which was free marketing ([source](../sources/4yKtlOWClhA.md), [8:26](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4yKtlOWClhA&t=506s)). Tim lists the swipe beside Netflix's "Are you still watching?" as viral UX ([source](../sources/hIm3d3JAsPw.md), [8:18](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hIm3d3JAsPw&t=498s)).
- **Gift, not receipt.** Tim uses Tinder as an example of rewards that turn into habit and identity ([source](../sources/uoLpH_20qKw.md), [8:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uoLpH_20qKw&t=535s)).

## Growth channels
