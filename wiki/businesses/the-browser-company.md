The Browser Company is the maker of the Arc browser, which it dropped to build Dia, an AI browser that answers instead of making you browse.

## What it is

- It sunset Arc despite user backlash and built [Dia](../businesses/dia.md). Dia aims to deliver browsing outcomes without browsing: you ask and it answers [source](../sources/zRUoPIwCxfw.md) [1:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=98s), [2:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=164s).
- [Tim Gabe](../people/tim-gabe.md) uses it as a case of "outcome-first" design. "They're not just adding AI to interfaces, they're replacing interfaces with outcomes" [source](../sources/zRUoPIwCxfw.md) [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=0s).

## Lessons the source draws

- **Do not pad AI onto a legacy UI.** Ask what the outcome is and how many steps you can skip [source](../sources/zRUoPIwCxfw.md) [6:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=377s).
- **The BlackBerry warning.** BlackBerry once owned almost half the US smartphone market and lost to a new interaction layer, not to bad phones [source](../sources/zRUoPIwCxfw.md) [0:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=36s).
- **A two-axis matrix.** Utility or experience, repetitive or complex. Repetitive utility flows are the ones AI removes [source](../sources/zRUoPIwCxfw.md) [6:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zRUoPIwCxfw&t=377s).
