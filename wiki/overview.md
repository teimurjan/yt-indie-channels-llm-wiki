The corpus is 369 YouTube transcripts from three channels. [Starter Story](businesses/starter-story.md) (`starterstory`, 194 videos, 2023-03 to 2026-10) profiles founders of small, profitable businesses: SaaS, mobile apps, e-commerce brands, agencies, newsletters and info products. Superwall (`superwallhq`, 59 videos, 2025-04 to 2026-10) interviews operators of consumer subscription apps about paywalls, onboarding, creators and paid ads. [Tim Gabe](people/tim-gabe.md) (`timgabe`, 116 videos, 2022-05 to 2026-10) teaches product and app design: Figma and Framer tutorials up to 2023, then app psychology, retention and paywall research from 2025. The wiki holds 369 source pages, 342 business pages, 244 person pages and 95 topic pages. Most numbers are self-reported by founders, so each metric carries the date of its video.

## Recurring patterns

### 1. Distribution comes before the product

Founders who win usually have an audience or a proven content format before they build. AI coding makes software cheap, so the scarce part is attention. See [distribution](topics/distribution.md), [personal brand](topics/personal-brand.md) and [build in public](topics/build-in-public.md).

- [Alex Finn](people/alex-finn.md) posted on X for 3 years before [Creator Buddy](businesses/creator-buddy.md) made $100K ARR on launch day (2025-01) ([4hMg_CZauJs](sources/4hMg_CZauJs.md), [9:16](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4hMg_CZauJs&t=556s)).
- [PushScroll](businesses/pushscroll.md) validated with one video (80K views, about 500 comments) before it wrote code ([ST2ROUFbdvU](sources/ST2ROUFbdvU.md), [0:11](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ST2ROUFbdvU&t=11s)).
- [Pat Walls](people/pat-walls.md) argues that AI coding floods every niche with apps, so content decides who wins ([Q4k8JNYKJT0](sources/Q4k8JNYKJT0.md), [16:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Q4k8JNYKJT0&t=1012s)).

### 2. Copy proven demand, then change the niche or the country

Competitors are proof of a market, not a threat. The usual move is to clone a working app for a new audience, language or niche. See [idea validation](topics/idea-validation.md), [competitor alternatives](topics/competitor-alternatives.md) and [consumer apps](topics/consumer-apps.md).

- [Cal Buddy](businesses/cal-buddy.md) copied [Cal AI](businesses/cal-ai.md) in Hebrew and passed $81K/month (2026-08) ([GQ27QVp3SzQ](sources/GQ27QVp3SzQ.md), [1:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GQ27QVp3SzQ&t=104s)).
- [PrayScreen](businesses/prayscreen.md) took the one sec screen-time idea to Christians, a niche that gets new technology late ([PmyXmpkTIdc](sources/PmyXmpkTIdc.md), [7:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PmyXmpkTIdc&t=427s)).
- No competitors means no market. Creating a market is "nearly impossible" ([rPguT9z6kRQ](sources/rPguT9z6kRQ.md), [7:50](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=rPguT9z6kRQ&t=470s)).

### 3. Payment is the only real validation

Waitlists, likes and traffic are weak signals. A card payment, a deposit or a pre-sale is the test. See [idea validation](topics/idea-validation.md), [MVP](topics/mvp.md) and [lifetime deals](topics/lifetime-deals.md).

- Setter AI took a refundable $500 deposit before the product existed ([3eY71OS4MFw](sources/3eY71OS4MFw.md), [3:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3eY71OS4MFw&t=238s)).
- Subscribr sold 50 lifetime licenses for $20,000 in 2 to 3 days, with the price rising every 10 licenses ([7KqlwjBM_tM](sources/7KqlwjBM_tM.md), [5:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7KqlwjBM_tM&t=312s)).
- [Marc Lou](people/marc-lou.md) sold to an escape-room owner after a 42-minute call, before he built anything ([WGVxAmmJUmY](sources/WGVxAmmJUmY.md), [6:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WGVxAmmJUmY&t=369s)).

### 4. Ship in days, with AI writing most of the code

Build time fell from months to days. Non-technical founders ship with Cursor, Claude Code, Rork, Lovable or Bubble. See [vibe coding](topics/vibe-coding.md), [no-code](topics/no-code.md) and [AI apps](topics/ai-apps.md).

- 3AK says an app can reach the App Store for under $500 ([DiRxTfnHTaY](sources/DiRxTfnHTaY.md), [3:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DiRxTfnHTaY&t=204s)).
- Claude Code on a $15 plan unblocked [Maxed AI](businesses/maxed-ai.md) after an earlier app stalled ([aLidq3_FwW0](sources/aLidq3_FwW0.md), [3:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aLidq3_FwW0&t=238s)).
- [Faceless Video](businesses/faceless-video.md) passed $1M ARR in about 10 months on Bubble (2025-06) ([x9TUDb4sLE0](sources/x9TUDb4sLE0.md), [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=x9TUDb4sLE0&t=0s)).

### 5. The founder finds the short-form format, then creators scale it

The most repeated consumer-app growth loop: post on your own accounts until one format works, then hand that exact format to many small creators. Creators who bring real distribution get equity or a revenue share. See [short-form video](topics/short-form-video.md), [UGC](topics/ugc.md), [organic social](topics/organic-social.md) and [influencer marketing](topics/influencer-marketing.md).

- Jay of [NomadTable](businesses/nomadtable.md) got the first 50K to 100K downloads from his own videos. Creators only remake hooks that already hit ([RVnnqTrSKZY](sources/RVnnqTrSKZY.md), [7:44](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RVnnqTrSKZY&t=464s)).
- [Kelechi](people/kelechi.md) made content until one format worked for [Social Wizard](businesses/social-wizard.md), then gave it to small streamers ([9WWvLj-NqEE](sources/9WWvLj-NqEE.md), [4:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9WWvLj-NqEE&t=243s)).
- One creator gave [Fitted](businesses/fitted.md) about 95% of its distribution ([9655E10fxsk](sources/9655E10fxsk.md), [9:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9655E10fxsk&t=574s)). Casper is the 50/50 distribution co-founder of [Payout](businesses/payout.md) ([I10xtUiXfeY](sources/I10xtUiXfeY.md), [3:29](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=I10xtUiXfeY&t=209s)).

### 6. Views are not sales

Virality and conversion often diverge. Sources judge content by comments ("what is the app?"), clicks and purchases, not views. See [analytics](topics/analytics.md) and [ad creative](topics/ad-creative.md).

- [Yev](people/yev.md) says the most viral videos often convert worse than top-25% videos ([lXGmWdFLsuM](sources/lXGmWdFLsuM.md), [13:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXGmWdFLsuM&t=834s)).
- A short clip of the app in use converts about 20x better than a meme slideshow ([CtqEbgutSb4](sources/CtqEbgutSb4.md), [22:42](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=CtqEbgutSb4&t=1362s)).
- Evo Marketing got 40M views and about $1.2M for a client, but about 45% churn from get-rich viewers ([VGJWRjz6nq8](sources/VGJWRjz6nq8.md), [16:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VGJWRjz6nq8&t=1000s)).

### 7. Organic finds the winner, paid ads scale it, and creative is the lever

Paid ads work best on top of content that already proved itself. Creative choice matters far more than targeting, and most creatives fail. See [Meta ads](topics/meta-ads.md), [TikTok ads](topics/tiktok-ads.md) and [ad creative](topics/ad-creative.md).

- Targeting and small tweaks give about 10%. The right creative gives about 10x ([lXGmWdFLsuM](sources/lXGmWdFLsuM.md), [13:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXGmWdFLsuM&t=785s)).
- [Ryan Thorp](people/ryan-thorp.md)'s team boosted videos that broke out of 0 to 300 views, then ran them as Spark Ads ([DcDOgWGh4bc](sources/DcDOgWGh4bc.md), [11:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DcDOgWGh4bc&t=693s)).
- At [Boost App Social](businesses/boost-app-social.md), 1 video in 85 got the spend and about 97% of creatives failed (2026-04) ([7JtttjbTRAQ](sources/7JtttjbTRAQ.md), [0:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7JtttjbTRAQ&t=38s)).
- Raw beats polished. For [Brawler](businesses/brawler.md), raw screen recordings worked and polished visuals made almost nothing ([7yLgHeYeQAE](sources/7yLgHeYeQAE.md), [9:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7yLgHeYeQAE&t=544s)).

### 8. Capture intent where buyers already search

For SaaS and utility apps, search beats the feed. Comparison pages, free tools, App Store keywords and search ads catch people who already want a fix. See [SEO](topics/seo.md), [app store optimization](topics/app-store-optimization.md), [Google ads](topics/google-ads.md) and [YouTube](topics/youtube.md).

- [Late](businesses/late.md) grew on SEO and search ads only. A search for "social media scheduling API" has about 10x the intent of a social viewer ([E_rX4JJrYkY](sources/E_rX4JJrYkY.md), [4:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E_rX4JJrYkY&t=240s)).
- Comparison pages against every competitor take their traffic and get cited by LLMs ([l4WEqPX52Cg](sources/l4WEqPX52Cg.md), [4:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l4WEqPX52Cg&t=293s)).
- At [App Masters](businesses/app-masters.md), one health app made about $4M of about $6M from organic App Store search (2026-07) ([Y6RstwPUWdU](sources/Y6RstwPUWdU.md), [8:37](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=517s)).

### 9. The paywall is the biggest revenue lever, and most tests lose

Paywall work moves revenue more than feature work. Teams that test often win, even though most single tests are neutral or negative. See [paywalls](topics/paywalls.md) and [A/B testing](topics/ab-testing.md).

- [Momego](businesses/momego.md) ran about 10 paywall tests in 2021: conversion went from 0.5% to 8% and revenue from about $8K to over $30K/month ([T5zMsTw8GWQ](sources/T5zMsTw8GWQ.md), [8:32](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T5zMsTw8GWQ&t=512s)).
- Apps that run 50+ paywall tests a year make 18.7x the revenue of apps that run one ([sYRhXB_ZcLI](sources/sYRhXB_ZcLI.md), [10:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYRhXB_ZcLI&t=638s)).
- Of 20 tests at [Social](businesses/social-app-studio.md): 10 neutral, 5 bad, 3 interesting, 2 wins ([oxr_Dz1vhdU](sources/oxr_Dz1vhdU.md), [14:58](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=oxr_Dz1vhdU&t=898s)).

### 10. Test plan structure before price, and remove trial anxiety

Plan count, plan order, trial setup and localization beat price-point tests. Users fear forgetting to cancel, so the best paywalls show a day-by-day timeline and promise a reminder. See [pricing](topics/pricing.md), [free trials](topics/free-trials.md) and [subscriptions](topics/subscriptions.md).

- In Adapty data, localization gave +62.3% LTV and trial structure +59.6% ([sYRhXB_ZcLI](sources/sYRhXB_ZcLI.md), [5:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYRhXB_ZcLI&t=341s)).
- [Blinkist](businesses/blinkist.md)'s trial timeline gave +23% trial signups. [Moonly](businesses/moonly.md) put the trial on annual only and got +39% conversion ([puldme__Ckk](sources/puldme__Ckk.md), [0:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk&t=40s), [3:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=puldme__Ckk&t=195s)).
- [Sunflower](businesses/sunflower.md)'s trial reminders raised revenue 48% (2026-03) ([zFfBuyKoaDM](sources/zFfBuyKoaDM.md), [46:25](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zFfBuyKoaDM&t=2785s)).

### 11. A long, personal onboarding followed by a hard paywall

Many consumer apps run a quiz that personalizes the result and builds sunk cost, then gate the app behind a trial. Tim Gabe's reconciliation: keep onboarding short by default. If each answer changes what the app shows, or the quiz filters for buyers, a long flow is fine. See [onboarding](topics/onboarding.md) and [paywalls](topics/paywalls.md).

- A hard paywall lifted Glam Up conversion by 50% ([Bf12MNPO-Js](sources/Bf12MNPO-Js.md), [40:01](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bf12MNPO-Js&t=2401s)).
- On one app, a hard paywall raised trial starts from 6% to 13%, trial-to-paid held, and revenue doubled ([Y6RstwPUWdU](sources/Y6RstwPUWdU.md), [19:34](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Y6RstwPUWdU&t=1174s)).
- If a long flow buys personalization or filters for buyers, use it. Otherwise keep it short ([Aa89MC8jX2c](sources/Aa89MC8jX2c.md), [3:59](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Aa89MC8jX2c&t=239s)).

### 12. Fix the biggest funnel leak first, and ask at the win

Install day decides most purchases, so the first session matters most. Teams map each funnel step, fix the worst drop-off, and place paywalls, rating prompts and share prompts right after a success. See [conversion rate optimization](topics/conversion-rate-optimization.md) and [analytics](topics/analytics.md).

- 82% (RevenueCat) to 89.4% (Adapty) of subscription starts happen on install day ([sYRhXB_ZcLI](sources/sYRhXB_ZcLI.md), [3:20](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sYRhXB_ZcLI&t=200s)).
- Fixing a funnel step with over 50% drop-off gave 2x to 3x growth. Feature work gave about 10% ([lXGmWdFLsuM](sources/lXGmWdFLsuM.md), [2:36](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lXGmWdFLsuM&t=156s)).
- The peak-end rule says to ask right after a win ([v1f9iYZ2GhQ](sources/v1f9iYZ2GhQ.md), [7:23](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=443s)). Momego asks for a rating at the first view of a live bus on the map ([T5zMsTw8GWQ](sources/T5zMsTw8GWQ.md), [6:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=T5zMsTw8GWQ&t=414s)).

### 13. Retention comes from identity, small competition and clocks, not points and badges

Points, badges and leaderboards alone fail. Apps that keep users give them four things. They get a label they identify with and a cohort small enough to win in. They also get a reason to return on a clock and a plan for the missed day. See [behavioral design](topics/behavioral-design.md), [gamification](topics/gamification.md) and [retention](topics/retention.md).

- [Strava](businesses/strava.md) segments are thousands of local leaderboards, so winning feels possible ([BxhsCu9hNpY](sources/BxhsCu9hNpY.md), [2:04](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BxhsCu9hNpY&t=124s)).
- [Spotify](businesses/spotify.md) Wrapped tells users who they are, not what they did ([Tpg0pxKHrCA](sources/Tpg0pxKHrCA.md), [9:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Tpg0pxKHrCA&t=546s)).
- Put the hero feature on a clock: [Hinge](businesses/hinge.md) Standouts reset every 24 hours ([VkFDdGGGA-s](sources/VkFDdGGGA-s.md), [1:52](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VkFDdGGGA-s&t=112s)). Design for the day after a user skips ([IKrLPhaFcp4](sources/IKrLPhaFcp4.md), [4:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IKrLPhaFcp4&t=264s)).

### 14. Consistency reads as quality, and quality reads as trust

Users judge trust in a fraction of a second. A small system makes a product feel trustworthy: a spacing grid, a type scale, few colors and one pattern per state. This matters more now, because AI makes screens that drift apart. See [design systems](topics/design-systems.md), [UI design](topics/ui-design.md), [grid systems](topics/grid-systems.md) and [mobile app design](topics/mobile-app-design.md).

- People judge trust in about 50 milliseconds, and a bad first impression can lose 20% to 30% of new users ([v1f9iYZ2GhQ](sources/v1f9iYZ2GhQ.md), [1:38](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v1f9iYZ2GhQ&t=98s)).
- Use an 8-point spacing grid, so a stray 31 px stands out ([ohF93_k3IMk](sources/ohF93_k3IMk.md), [1:28](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ohF93_k3IMk&t=88s)).
- AI agents make 80 screens that drift apart. Review the app as one product ([8EAYZe6y3I8](sources/8EAYZe6y3I8.md), [4:10](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=8EAYZe6y3I8&t=250s)). An AI build scored 5/15 from a bare prompt, 8/15 with a basic design system, and 15/15 with human art direction ([YS7uOXjmJTA](sources/YS7uOXjmJTA.md), [1:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YS7uOXjmJTA&t=111s)).

### 15. Software is easy to copy, so the moat is elsewhere

Copycats arrive within weeks, and platforms such as OpenAI or Microsoft can absorb a feature overnight. Sources name distribution, personal brand, community and the emotional or social layer as the defenses. See [AI wrappers](topics/ai-wrappers.md), [AI agents](topics/ai-agents.md) and [community](topics/community.md).

- About 10 copycat Excel formula sites appeared within weeks of [Formula Bot](businesses/formula-bot.md). Microsoft then announced its own OpenAI tools for Excel ([IZsQqarWXtY](sources/IZsQqarWXtY.md), [11:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IZsQqarWXtY&t=700s), [12:40](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=IZsQqarWXtY&t=760s)).
- The agent paradox: if an AI does the task through one text, why open the app? The emotional and social layer becomes the product ([ZXWUg1e1PAs](sources/ZXWUg1e1PAs.md), [0:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZXWUg1e1PAs&t=0s)).
- A clone with 10 followers loses to a founder with an audience ([4hMg_CZauJs](sources/4hMg_CZauJs.md), [11:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4hMg_CZauJs&t=677s)).

## Where sources disagree

- **Hard paywall or freemium.** [Y6RstwPUWdU](sources/Y6RstwPUWdU.md) and [Bf12MNPO-Js](sources/Bf12MNPO-Js.md) found a hard paywall made more money. [D7eaHcznLZU](sources/D7eaHcznLZU.md) is skeptical of hard paywalls. See [paywalls](topics/paywalls.md).
- **Trial or no trial.** [qJ4qYhTqAz0](sources/qJ4qYhTqAz0.md) found a free trial made 140% more revenue than a discounted intro price. [24QMRDXck70](sources/24QMRDXck70.md) shows Claim dropping its trial because paid-only made more. See [free trials](topics/free-trials.md).
- **Lead with weekly or annual.** [sYRhXB_ZcLI](sources/sYRhXB_ZcLI.md) says weekly converts 1.7x to 7.4x better than annual. [lkwX_kc0NS8](sources/lkwX_kc0NS8.md) preselects annual. See [subscriptions](topics/subscriptions.md).
- **Short or long onboarding.** [55hDj88zKa8](sources/55hDj88zKa8.md) says keep steps few. [nQKWYmrixCU](sources/nQKWYmrixCU.md) says a long quiz is an amplifier, because effort makes the result feel earned. Both are Tim Gabe videos. See [onboarding](topics/onboarding.md).

- **Do reminders build habits.** [IKrLPhaFcp4](sources/IKrLPhaFcp4.md) says rewards and reminders do not. [pb4JIH8FubE](sources/pb4JIH8FubE.md) uses streak-risk notifications as a core retention lever. See [push notifications](topics/push-notifications.md).
- **Copy or invent.** [sAr9FWEQZAk](sources/sAr9FWEQZAk.md) says copycat apps cap out at $10K to $100K a month. [GQ27QVp3SzQ](sources/GQ27QVp3SzQ.md) built a copy past $81K/month. See [idea validation](topics/idea-validation.md).
- **Validate first or build first.** [3eY71OS4MFw](sources/3eY71OS4MFw.md) says only a payment before the build counts. [EF3uyvHHBfM](sources/EF3uyvHHBfM.md) says a one-week MVP is the validation, and [E_sPvPHwpuk](sources/E_sPvPHwpuk.md) launched Chatbase with no validation to 16 followers. See [MVP](topics/mvp.md).
- **Free plan or charge from day one.** [ty9ZcimL6VE](sources/ty9ZcimL6VE.md) stayed free for 9 months before it charged. [npcL7oRZQlI](sources/npcL7oRZQlI.md) says free usage disappears at the first charge. See [SaaS](topics/saas.md).
- **Low or premium prices.** [5u9u8yzPEpA](sources/5u9u8yzPEpA.md) says low prices bring more users and word of mouth. [u4tpE9AvBpc](sources/u4tpE9AvBpc.md) favors premium prices. [Bf12MNPO-Js](sources/Bf12MNPO-Js.md) raised prices and won, and [zFfBuyKoaDM](sources/zFfBuyKoaDM.md) lowered them and won. See [pricing](topics/pricing.md).
- **Which short-form platform.** [CdBuMyrqWCs](sources/CdBuMyrqWCs.md) says TikTok converts best. [-OPKa8DIzKU](sources/-OPKa8DIzKU.md) says TikTok does not convert and YouTube does, and [SuUJgmbQrVc](sources/SuUJgmbQrVc.md) says Instagram now drives more views (2026-09). - **Paid influencers for a new app.** [1nfPvJKrYYQ](sources/1nfPvJKrYYQ.md) and [PdudtXwEsOQ](sources/PdudtXwEsOQ.md) got views but almost no sales. - **Search channels.** [NuYO7DWzlaM](sources/NuYO7DWzlaM.md) says SEO is mostly dead outside narrow niches, and [E_rX4JJrYkY](sources/E_rX4JJrYkY.md) says it works for buyer-intent queries. See [SEO](topics/seo.md) and [Google ads](topics/google-ads.md).

## By channel

**starterstory.** Pat Walls and his team interview founders, with revenue in every title. Output grew from 19 videos in 2023 to 71 in each of 2025 and 2026, and shifted toward AI tools and consumer apps. The businesses range from micro-SaaS and AI wrappers to DTC brands, agencies, newsletters and app portfolios. The steady advice: solve a problem you know, copy a proven market and charge early. Keep the day job until revenue is real. Pick one cheap channel, such as Reddit, SEO, short-form or X. Pat sold Starter Story to HubSpot (2026-02) ([v-uhjlMg9L0](sources/v-uhjlMg9L0.md), [9:33](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v-uhjlMg9L0&t=573s)). Numbers are self-reported and rarely verified.

**superwallhq.** These are operator deep-dives on consumer subscription apps: paywalls, onboarding, trials, UGC programs, Meta and TikTok ads, and buying and selling apps. This channel has the densest conversion benchmarks and test results in the corpus. Superwall sells paywall software, so the channel leans toward hard paywalls and constant paywall testing. Most guests run health, fitness, self-improvement and AI-utility apps aimed at the US.

**timgabe.** In 2022 and 2023 (73 videos) Tim Gabe made Figma and Framer tutorials: auto layout, grids, type, color, motion and responsive sites. After a break in 2024, the 2025 and 2026 videos (40) cover app psychology, retention, gamification, paywall research and design in the AI era. Most case studies are large apps (Duolingo, Strava, Spotify, Apple), so they give principles and cite studies more than first-hand revenue numbers. His later videos often argue against tactics the other two channels use, such as stacked streaks and onboarding that is long for no reason.

## Open questions

- **Durability.** Most metrics are a single snapshot. Few sources come back a year later. The corpus cannot say how many of these apps keep their revenue. It also cannot say how fast formats, ad accounts and App Store rank decay.
- **Survivorship.** Almost every guest is a success story. Nobody knows how often "copy a proven app" or a creator program fails. The only base rate is portfolio data, such as 4 of 28 apps that carry one portfolio ([vw2Yc4Y1PsU](sources/vw2Yc4Y1PsU.md), [1:24](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vw2Yc4Y1PsU&t=84s)).
- **Profit, not revenue.** Titles quote revenue or MRR. Few sources give net margin after ad spend, creator pay, AI costs and store fees, so unit economics across channels are hard to compare. See [unit economics](topics/unit-economics.md).
- **Design ROI.** Tim Gabe argues design and emotion are the moat, but no source runs a controlled test of a design overhaul against revenue. The one paywall data point favors a plain native look.
- **Outside the US and outside iOS.** Most app data is US and iOS. Android, web-only funnels and non-English markets appear only in passing (see [localization](topics/localization.md)).
- **Long-term effects of aggressive tactics.** Hard paywalls, 80% to 90% exit discounts, phone farms and loss-based streaks get short-term wins. No source measures refunds, reviews, platform bans or brand damage over time.
- **When to raise money.** Bootstrapped and funded app founders both win. The corpus gives no clear rule for the point at which outside money helps. See [fundraising](topics/fundraising.md) and [bootstrapping](topics/bootstrapping.md).
